# Discovery coverage and negative-search ledger

**Lifecycle:** CANONICAL  
**Authority:** CANONICAL  
**Concept:** Cross-theme discovery coverage and negative-search outcomes  
**As of:** 2026-09-25  
**Issue:** #330  
**Machine ledger:** [coverage-ledger.json](coverage-ledger.json)

The ledger asks whether Unicorn searched the important scarce-complement lanes before underwriting companies. It preserves the thesis chain:

> **Abundant Intelligence → Scarce Complements → Bottleneck Migration → Economic Capture → Capital Allocation**

It is an audit of existing repository evidence. It does not add company research, rerank companies or authorize capital.

## Result vocabulary

| Search result | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `ACTIVE_CANDIDATES` | A bounded architecture-first search found at least one listed candidate still under research or monitoring. |
| `CANDIDATES_FOUND_BUT_REJECTED` | Listed candidates were examined, but their capture, purity, materiality or setup failed the lane-level promotion test. |
| `NO_CREDIBLE_LISTED_CAPTURE` | The bottleneck may be real, but the search found no clean listed vehicle; private/captive benchmarks remain visible. |
| `COVERAGE_INCOMPLETE` | The architecture question is relevant, but no sufficiently bounded search has established either capture or a reliable negative result. |
| `INTENTIONALLY_EXCLUDED` | The layer is outside scarce-complement discovery unless a more specific constrained sub-layer emerges. |

These states prevent three common errors: treating “no candidate found” as “theme disproved”; treating a rejected public proxy as an unsearched lane; and replacing a private architecture leader with a weaker listed company merely to obtain investability.

The JSON record retains searched families, active and rejected examples, private/uninvestable benchmarks, remaining gaps, source files and explicit rescan triggers for every row. `python3 scripts/discovery_coverage.py` validates the distinctions and source paths.

## Private and uninvestable benchmarks

Private benchmarks remain in the ledger when they explain where economics could accrue or why a public proxy is weak. Examples include EV Group in hybrid bonding; Weidmann, Trench/HSP and Maschinenfabrik Reinhausen in transformer components; Rollvis in roller screws; Foretellix and Applied Intuition in verification; and Automata, Opentrons and HighRes in laboratory execution.

Their presence is architecture evidence, not a licence to promote the closest listed company. Re-open a lane when a private specialist lists, is acquired into a public owner where it becomes material, or creates observable pricing/order effects in public suppliers.

## Familiarity-bias test

The canonical ranking's top 20 divides as follows:

| Primary programme | Top-20 observations |
|---|---:|
| Energy / power / cooling | 7 |
| Physical AI | 7 |
| Memory / data movement | 5 |
| Distributed compute | 1 |
| Scientific AI | 0 |
| Workflow / permissions | 0 |

**Interpretation:** 12 of 20 top-ranked observations sit in memory or energy/power/cooling, the repository's deepest established programmes. Physical AI supplies seven more, while newer scientific-AI and workflow lanes supply none. This does not show that the current ranks are wrong: ranking reflects evidence depth, valuation and portfolio usefulness as well as discovery coverage. It does show that universe construction and evidence maturity favour familiar lanes.

The control is therefore procedural: every thesis-first scan must start from the cross-theme lane map; record the searched value-chain families; preserve private/uninvestable benchmarks; state whether the result was active, rejected, absent, incomplete or excluded; and attach a concrete rescan trigger. New memory or power candidates face a higher marginal-research hurdle when they duplicate existing exposures. Under-covered independent lanes receive priority only when a bounded search could improve the portfolio opportunity set.

## What this audit changes

The repository already had substantial negative-search evidence, but it was distributed across dated scans, theme plans, deep dives and frozen historical cases. This ledger makes the search outcome and revisit condition visible in one current surface.

The audit finds:

- strong coverage in memory, electricity/power, cooling, actuation and safety/runtime;
- emerging but still financially unproven coverage in scientific-AI execution;
- active candidates with weak purity in simulation/verification, edge deployment and sensing;
- no credible listed direct capture for fleet feedback/data rights;
- incomplete bounded coverage in networking, industrial control and workflow/permissions;
- one clear “candidates found but rejected” result in narrow 800 VDC/rack power;
- generic model/accelerator discovery intentionally excluded unless a specific scarcity emerges.

This is a discovery-control result. It does not validate any candidate, change the prospective methodology version or establish investment alpha.

## Required use and refresh

Use [coverage-ledger.json](coverage-ledger.json) before a new cross-theme scan. Update an existing lane rather than creating a duplicate. A scan is complete only when it records sources and one of the five result states, plus a rescan trigger. `NO_CREDIBLE_LISTED_CAPTURE` and rejection are valid outcomes.

Refresh a lane when one of its recorded triggers occurs, when a material architecture migration changes the limiting complement, or when a new thesis-first scan produces a bounded negative result. Do not rescan merely because a theme is popular or because the ranked universe has capacity for another company.
