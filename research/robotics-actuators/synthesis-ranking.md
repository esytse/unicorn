## Executive conclusion

The first complete actuator cycle produces a more complex and more useful answer than "buy harmonic-drive suppliers."

Three functions tie at the current **4.4/5 structural frontier**:

1. **qualified precision rotary-transmission manufacture**;
2. **planetary roller-screw precision manufacture**;
3. **integrated actuator industrialization / calibration**.

They are not equivalent investment opportunities.

### Best public-company hunting ground

**Precision rotary transmission ranks first for Gate-B supplier hunting** because it combines:
- direct system criticality;
- meaningful qualification/switching burden;
- actual humanoid mass-production evidence;
- multiple listed companies with high total-company exposure;
- architecture risk that can be explicitly underwritten.

### Most architecture-resilient function

**Actuator industrialization/calibration** is required regardless of whether the joint uses strain-wave, cycloidal, planetary, QDD, roller screw or ball screw. Schaeffler's multi-year, seven-digit actuator supply evidence validates the function at production scale. But merchant capture is less certain because robot OEMs can vertically integrate.

### Hardest precision-manufacturing candidate

**Planetary roller screws** may have the strongest pure manufacturing-physics moat: internal thread machining, many interacting contacts, micron-level error sensitivity and load-sharing/wear complexity. But linear-joint architecture share is uncertain, premium specialists are often private, and Schaeffler/Ewellix exposure is diluted inside a much larger group.

### Important negative result

**Motors, encoders, bearings and tactile/force sensors are currently content-growth layers rather than structural supplier bottlenecks.** Their volumes can grow dramatically without giving suppliers durable scarcity pricing.

The central research conclusion is:

> **The actuator opportunity is a contest between precision-process moats and industrialization moats, not a contest to maximize component count.**

## 2. Architecture map — what we now know

There is no one humanoid actuator architecture.

### High-ratio rotary
Best suited where compactness / low backlash / high reduction dominate.

Likely candidates:
- strain-wave;
- compact cycloidal/RV;
- high-ratio precision planetary.

Typical zones: wrists/distal arms and potentially shoulders/torso depending robot.

### QDD / lower-ratio rotary
Trades gearbox ratio for motor size/current to improve backdrivability/control bandwidth.

Potentially attractive in dynamic hips/knees/ankles and quadrupeds.

### Linear screw actuation
Uses roller/ball screw to convert motor rotation into axial force.

Potentially attractive in payload-oriented lower limbs / high-force short-stroke joints.

### Hands
Architecture is least settled:
- micro geared actuators;
- tendons;
- linkages;
- underactuation;
- simple grippers remain a powerful economic substitute.

**CONCLUSION:** Any company thesis that assumes one architecture across every humanoid joint should be rejected unless direct production BOM evidence supports it.

## 3. Rotary transmission — why it ranks first for company research

Structural score: **4.4**.

Why attractive:
- qualified reducer change can force mechanical/control/lifetime revalidation;
- direct humanoid mass-production evidence exists;
- process/lifetime consistency remains difficult;
- listed pure plays exist.

Why not a monopoly thesis:
- Chinese strain-wave suppliers now mass-produce;
- Schaeffler is entering with planetary and strain-wave modules;
- Nabtesco is pushing Monocrank/RV + strain-wave;
- lower-limb architecture may shift toward QDD or linear.

### Gate-B candidates

#### Harmonic Drive Systems
Reasons to underwrite:
- very high precision-transmission exposure;
- direct disclosure of humanoid prototypes and mass production for some customers;
- deep endurance/process know-how;
- microactuator collaboration with MinebeaMitsumi.

Core risks:
- Chinese share/cost competition;
- strain-wave architecture share;
- current humanoid revenue materiality;
- valuation.

#### Laifual Drive — 03952.HK
Reasons to underwrite:
- listed June 2026, creating a new public pure-play comparator;
- HK listing document says it was one of two Chinese manufacturers with humanoid harmonic-reducer deliveries/mass production by end-2025;
- #2 Chinese robotic harmonic-reducer shipment supplier in 2025 by CIC data cited in prospectus;
- also sells joint modules, potentially moving up the value chain;
- share price around HKD58 on 8 September versus HKD85.5 IPO price, creating a materially different valuation setup from the initial offering.

Core risks:
- short public track record;
- customer concentration / undisclosed humanoid revenue;
- durability claims need field validation;
- capacity expansion could compress prices/margins;
- China governance / access risk.

#### Nabtesco — benchmark, not yet separate issue
Monocrank is technically compelling and specifically targets humanoid shoulder/hip joints, but named humanoid production/customer evidence is currently weaker than HDS/Laifual/Schaeffler. Keep as the industrial-robot durability benchmark until evidence improves.

## 4. Industrialization — why Schaeffler deserves company underwriting

Structural score: **4.4**, highest architecture durability.

Schaeffler evidence is unusually strong:
- integrated planetary / strain-wave / linear platforms;
- seven-digit Humanoid actuator expectation through 2031;
- separate Hexagon actuator supply partnership;
- Neura actuator partnership;
- mass production of humanoid components planned from 2026;
- 12 manufacturing technology classes and 90+ process families;
- real humanoid deployments in its own factories create a field-data loop.

**INTERPRETATION:** Schaeffler may be the clearest current test of whether **automotive industrialization skill can displace traditional robot-component scarcity**.

The Gate-B question is not technical. It is financial:

> Can humanoid actuators become large enough, profitable enough and fast enough to move Schaeffler group earnings?

Its group scale can make the best operating evidence a mediocre investment exposure.

## 5. Roller screws — strongest technical moat, poor public purity

Structural score: **4.4**.

The manufacturing evidence is strong:
- internal-thread difficulty;
- load-sharing/contact stress;
- multi-source machining errors;
- wear sensitivity;
- preload / matching.

But company capture is harder to access:
- Rollvis is private;
- Ewellix is inside Schaeffler;
- many Asian public companies are only at prototype/pilot stages or lack named customer evidence;
- ball screws remain a viable substitute in some joints.

**DECISION:** Do not open a separate pure PRSM company issue until a listed supplier provides primary-source production/customer/financial evidence strong enough to separate it from theme speculation.

For now, test roller-screw economics inside Schaeffler and Minebea where directly relevant.

## 6. Common components — explicit rejection of the content thesis

### Motors
High torque density and thermal performance matter, but adjacent motor supply is broad.

### Bearings
Potentially 100+ units per humanoid creates huge content, but cross-roller/thin-section/miniature suppliers are numerous enough that scarcity is not established.

### Encoders
Every joint needs position feedback, but optical/magnetic/inductive/resolver architectures create substitution.

### Force/torque sensors
Closest common-component watch at 3.9, but motor-current/model-based torque estimation and alternative sensor structures prevent a current Gate-A pass.

**CONCLUSION:** Do not open component company issues from unit-count arithmetic alone.

MinebeaMitsumi is an exception worth Gate-B analysis because it spans many components and explicitly targets humanoid revenue/orders; the thesis is **portfolio content + integrated mass production**, not bearing scarcity.

---

## 7. Dexterous hands — high engineering difficulty, unstable economics

Hand integration passes at **4.1**, but no individual hand component does.

Critical uncertainty:
- fully actuated human-like hand;
- tendon/linkage hand;
- underactuated hand;
- simpler task-specific gripper.

If industrial humanoids favor simple reliable end effectors, the expected hand BOM can collapse.

**DECISION:** keep the hand as an architecture monitor, not a standalone company research lane yet.

## 9. Gate-B scoring framework

For each company, score:

- direct production / named customer evidence;
- share / qualification / process-of-record evidence;
- architecture exposure and transition resilience;
- revenue / backlog / ASP sensitivity;
- gross / operating margin evidence;
- capex / working capital / FCF;
- service / installed-base recurrence;
- customer concentration;
- vertical-integration risk;
- total-company earnings sensitivity;
- valuation and market-cap asymmetry only after Gate B evidence is built.

The company may score highly despite a lower component Bottleneck Strength if it spans several validated functions.
