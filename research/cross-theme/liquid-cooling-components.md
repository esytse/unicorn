# AI liquid-cooling component discovery — 25 September 2026

**Backlog:** #268, parent #261  
**Method:** rack-density change → leak/flow/reliability constraint → qualified component supplier → company materiality.

## World change

AI racks are moving toward much higher thermal loads and larger CDUs. As liquid cooling scales, system availability depends on apparently mundane components: **quick disconnects, manifolds/cold plates, pumps/valves, seals and monitoring**.

The thesis-first question is not which cooling OEM sells the most systems. It is:

> Which small listed component supplier becomes hard to substitute because leaks, pressure drop, serviceability and qualification become deployment constraints?

## Lead 1 — LOTES (TWSE:3533)

### Architecture evidence

LOTES markets liquid-cooling quick-disconnect products for hyperscale/enterprise data centres and AI/HPC rack cold-plate/manifold systems. It also participates in NVIDIA's MGX ecosystem.

Quick disconnects matter because they must permit serviceability while maintaining low leakage, pressure integrity and high flow over repeated mating cycles. Failure cost at rack level can greatly exceed connector value.

### Company-transforming test

LOTES is already a substantial connector/socket company, so product presence alone is insufficient. This pass did not establish primary disclosed liquid-cooling revenue, backlog, customer concentration, capacity constraint or margin contribution.

**State: EVIDENCE BUILD — not promoted to underwrite.**

Upgrade only if cooling revenue/order contribution becomes disclosed/material or capacity/qualification evidence shows scarcity.

## Lead 2 — Auras Technology (TWSE:3324)

Secondary industry research repeatedly identifies Auras in AI-server cold plates/manifolds and liquid-cooling capacity expansion.

The architecture fit is strong, but the current scan did not establish enough primary company evidence on liquid-cooling mix/orders/margins to support a thesis-first promotion.

**State: EVIDENCE BUILD / COVERAGE GAP.**

## Lead 3 — AVC / Asia Vital Components (TWSE:3017)

AVC is structurally exposed to thermal management and liquid-cooling assemblies, but at current evidence quality the thesis resembles a broad AI-cooling beneficiary rather than a newly qualified scarce component.

**State: COMPARATOR.**

## Lead 4 — large diversified connector/fluid-control suppliers

Large incumbents can benefit from quick disconnects, valves and fluid-control demand, but the AI-cooling layer is unlikely by itself to transform consolidated economics. They remain architecture/competition references.

## Known inventory comparison

- **Modine/Modexus:** system-level cooling exposure; already Gate-E work, not a new discovery.
- **Vertiv:** broad system/power/thermal incumbent; scale reduces company-transforming purity.
- **nVent:** broad electrical/cooling exposure; same issue.
- **LOTES/Auras:** closer to the desired component-level bottleneck, but financial materiality evidence is currently weaker.

## Falsifiers

1. Cooling-loop components can standardize and multi-source rapidly.
2. Large OEMs/CDU vendors can vertically integrate manifolds/cold plates/connectors.
3. Capacity growth across Taiwan/China can prevent durable scarcity.
4. A component can be technically critical but economically low-value if qualification does not translate to pricing power.
5. NVIDIA ecosystem participation is not equivalent to material orders.

## Discovery conclusion

**No new underwrite promoted.**

The liquid-cooling component thesis remains structurally attractive, but the current evidence does not yet show the target pre-inflection pattern strongly enough:
**qualification → first production → disclosed 5–15% company materiality → acceleration.**

LOTES remains the highest-information component lead; Auras remains a coverage gap. Neither should be promoted until financial/qualification evidence improves.
