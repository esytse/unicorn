# FORT Robotics / FROB — Canonical Transaction and Economics Underwrite

**Status:** WAIT / event-gated  
**As of:** 24 September 2026  
**Backlog:** #209; transaction monitor #108; comparison #109  
**Decision horizon:** March 2028  
**Confidence:** structural bottleneck **Medium-High**; company capture **Medium**; financial materiality **Low-Medium**; equity/valuation **Low**

> This file canonicalizes what is knowable before the Form S-4/proxy is filed. It does not promote FORT, authorize a trade, or substitute management's illustrative software model for observed recurring revenue.

## Executive conclusion

FORT has credible evidence that machine-independent safety/control can be sold across a broad Physical-AI customer base, and the 2025 operating record is stronger than the prior repo surface implied: audited standalone revenue was **$11.585m**, +62% y/y, gross margin was **66.2%**, mature enterprise customers contributed about **70%** of revenue, and no single customer exceeded 9% of 2025 revenue.

The decisive weakness is economic repeatability. The transaction presentation describes an attractive future software/subscription model, but its detailed software case is explicitly **hypothetical**, not an actual customer deployment, forecast or contracted arrangement. Current public materials do not disclose actual hardware/software/subscription/services revenue mix, ARR, subscription attach, retention by revenue, cash flow, or a recurring-software gross-margin bridge.

At the announced no-redemption case, the transaction starts from **$500m pre-money equity**, **74.2m issued shares**, **$742.4m pro-forma equity value** and **$556.6m enterprise value** at $10/share. That is about **48.0x 2025 revenue on EV/revenue** before proving recurring software economics. Public warrants and contingent sponsor earnout create additional dilution beyond the 74.2m headline share count. Redemptions can also reduce delivered cash.

**Current Gate-E state: WAIT.** The next decision-quality event is the S-4/equivalent registration/proxy filing. Until then, valuation should be treated as a transaction sensitivity, not a conventional forward multiple underwrite.

## 2. Observed operating economics

### FACT — 2024 to 2025

| Metric | 2024A | 2025A | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue | $7.170m | $11.585m | +62% |
| Gross profit | $5.054m | $7.674m | +52% |
| Gross margin | 70.5% | 66.2% | -4.3 pp |
| Operating expenses | $10.340m | $12.266m | +19% |
| Operating loss | $(5.286)m | $(4.592)m | improved |
| Early customers | 201 | 227 | +13% |
| Mature customers (>~$100k annual spend) | 14 | 21 | +50% |
| Revenue / early customer | $14.6k | $15.5k | +6% |
| Revenue / mature customer | $302.6k | $384.5k | +27% |
| Mature-customer revenue | $4.237m | $8.075m | +91% |

**INTERPRETATION:** FORT has genuine land-and-expand evidence: both the number of mature accounts and spend per mature account rose. Operating leverage also improved because revenue grew much faster than opex. But the company remained loss-making and the absolute revenue base is very small relative to the proposed enterprise value.

**COUNTER-EVIDENCE:** gross margin declined in 2025 despite scale. Without mix disclosure it is not possible to determine whether this reflects hardware mix, pricing, acquisition/integration effects or other factors.

## 3. Recurring-economics audit

### What is actually observed

- Pre-2025 customer cohorts were said to contribute an estimated 68% of 2025 **bookings**.
- Mature enterprise accounts expanded spend materially in 2025.
- FORT reports more than 19,500 deployed units.
- The investor deck describes embedded software, base subscription and premium subscription products.

### What is not yet observed

- actual 2025 revenue split: hardware vs embedded software vs subscriptions vs services;
- ARR or recurring-revenue percentage;
- subscription attach per deployed unit;
- gross retention / net revenue retention;
- churn by revenue rather than customer count;
- software gross margin;
- contracted backlog and conversion duration on a revenue basis;
- cash flow / burn and post-Mapless contribution.

**INTERPRETATION:** the evidence supports **repeat customer purchasing**, not yet **recurring software economics**. This distinction is central. Hardware replacements and additional unit purchases can produce durable cohorts without creating SaaS-like economics.

### Dilution outside the headline share count

The presentation explicitly excludes **8.6m public warrants + 0.3m private warrants**, each with an $11.50 strike, and excludes **0.8m sponsor founder shares subject to contingent earnout**. The PIPE/NRA ownership line already includes 1.4m incentive shares (0.4m newly issued and 1.0m transferred founder shares).

A simple maximum-share illustration using only these disclosed excluded instruments is **83.9m shares** (74.2 + 8.9 warrants + 0.8 earnout), before any other S-4-disclosed equity awards or future issuance. This is **not** a closing fully-diluted count: warrants are price-contingent and their exercise would bring cash; earnout conditions matter; the S-4 may disclose additional instruments.

**OPEN QUESTION:** final redemptions. Every $10m of trust cash redeemed reduces cash delivered by roughly $10m before secondary financing responses, while public shares are also redeemed. The S-4/proxy and closing statement are required for the real bridge.

## 5. Valuation burden

At the announced $556.6m EV and 2025 revenue of $11.585m, EV/revenue is approximately **48x**. That multiple is not a forecast; it is a statement about the growth burden embedded in the transaction.

Revenue required for the announced EV to equal selected EV/revenue multiples, holding EV constant:

| EV / revenue | Revenue required | Multiple of 2025 revenue |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 20x | ~$27.8m | 2.4x |
| 10x | ~$55.7m | 4.8x |
| 5x | ~$111.3m | 9.6x |

**INTERPRETATION:** even if FORT maintains attractive gross margins, the equity case needs unusually rapid scaling, a much more recurring/software-heavy mix, or both. A lower public share price can improve the entry case, but price alone does not repair weak recurring economics.

## 6. Competitive and moat due diligence

### Supporting evidence

- FORT positions the platform as machine-agnostic safety/control infrastructure rather than a robot OEM.
- Certification and third-party audited safety-development processes can create time-to-market and switching-cost advantages.
- NVIDIA included FORT in the Halos for Robotics ecosystem, evidence that FORT can complement rather than merely compete with a major compute/platform vendor.
- Mapless AI expands the stack into human-in-the-loop teleoperation and active safety.

### Strongest counter-case

- Robot OEMs, industrial automation incumbents, QNX/Linux/ROS ecosystems, compute vendors and integrators can absorb more safety functionality.
- Certification is valuable but does not automatically confer pricing power or recurring revenue.
- A horizontal safety layer can be technically important while capturing only a small hardware/content dollar amount per machine.
- NVIDIA Halos is simultaneously a route to market and evidence that the surrounding platform can expand around FORT's layer.

**HYPOTHESIS:** FORT's best long-run moat is not the handset/controller hardware itself; it is certification-aware embedded software + fleet safety/observability + cross-OEM workflow integration. Public evidence has not yet shown that this layer is monetizing at scale.

## 7. Thesis breakers and upgrade triggers

### Thesis breakers

1. S-4 shows software/subscription revenue is immaterial and hardware/project revenue dominates without credible attach.
2. Customer retention/bookings cohorts do not convert into durable revenue retention.
3. Redemptions, warrants, earnouts, SBC or financing materially worsen per-share economics.
4. Cash burn implies another substantial raise before recurring economics inflect.
5. Certification scope is narrower or less differentiated than the investment narrative assumes.
6. Large OEM/platform vendors internalize the relevant safety layer and reduce FORT's content opportunity.

### Evidence that would strengthen the case

1. S-4 discloses a meaningful recurring/software share with growing attach to installed units.
2. Revenue retention / repeat expansion is verified on actual revenue, not bookings.
3. Customer concentration remains low and mature accounts continue expanding.
4. Pro-forma cash survives redemptions with a credible runway to operating leverage.
5. Named production deployments demonstrate paid embedded/software content per machine.
6. Software mix lifts gross margin and cash conversion as scale rises.
