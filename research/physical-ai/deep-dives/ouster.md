# Ouster — sensing / perception Gate-D underwrite

**Issue:** #187  
**Date:** 2026-09-24  
**State:** RESEARCH / Gate-D candidate  
**Decision:** **REASSESS — promote to full Gate-E only after valuation + normalized economics are completed.**

## Why it matters

Ouster is the cleanest listed test surfaced by #173 for whether Physical-AI sensing can become an economically scarce complement. The thesis is not that robots need sensors; it is that a supplier can combine lidar, vision, calibration, AI compute, sensor fusion and perception software into a qualified stack with durable margins and switching costs.

## Current operating evidence

- Q2 2026 revenue: **US$55m, +56% y/y, +12% q/q**.
- Product revenue: **US$53m, +51% y/y**.
- More than **17,000 lidar + camera sensors** shipped; lidar was ~53% of units.
- GAAP gross margin **49%**; non-GAAP gross margin **53%**.
- Adjusted EBITDA loss **US$4m**; GAAP net loss **US$18m**.
- Cash, restricted cash and short-term investments: **US$263m** at 30 Jun 2026.
- Q3 revenue guide: **US$54.5–57.5m**.
- Q2 product growth was led by industrial and smart-infrastructure use cases including warehouse automation, yard logistics and intelligent transportation.

## Stereolabs changes the thesis

Ouster closed Stereolabs on 4 Feb 2026 for ~US$35m cash + 1.8m shares (0.7m released over four years). Stereolabs generated ~US$16m unaudited 2025 revenue, was described as high-growth / EBITDA positive, had shipped >90,000 ZED cameras and served >10,000 customers.

The combined platform spans digital lidar, stereo cameras, AI compute, sensor fusion, perception software and AI models. This creates a possible **integration / developer-workflow moat** beyond discrete lidar hardware.

### What is fact vs hypothesis

**FACT:** the combined product stack and customer/developer footprint exist.

**HYPOTHESIS:** bundling improves retention, content per deployment, software mix and switching costs. No durable economics should be credited until disclosed results show it.

## Normalized margin test

Q2 margin improved sharply. The company attributes year-over-year improvement to volume growth and operating efficiencies. Gate-E must still separate:
- mix / Stereolabs contribution;
- temporary or non-recurring benefits;
- lidar ASP movement;
- manufacturing yield / scale;
- software contribution.

A 49% GAAP gross margin is encouraging but **not by itself proof of durable scarcity**.

## Unit / ASP bridge

Q2 shipped >17k combined units, ~53% lidar. Product revenue was US$53m. A blended revenue-per-unit calculation would be misleading because cameras and lidar have materially different prices and product revenue can contain other elements.

Required disclosure/evidence before using ASP as a thesis pillar:
1. lidar units and revenue separately;
2. camera units and revenue separately;
3. comparable-generation pricing;
4. mix-adjusted ASP trajectory.

## Qualification and repeat deployment

The strongest evidence is shifting from pilots toward repeated operational deployments:
- industrial / warehouse / yard-logistics revenue growth;
- smart-infrastructure deployments;
- recent agriculture, inspection and mapping integrations.

Gate-E should distinguish announcements from **repeat production orders**, named multi-site expansions and revenue concentration.

## Competitive falsification

### Camera-only
Cheaper vision can substitute for lidar in benign environments. Ouster must prove that range/precision/reliability and fused perception create enough system value to offset added sensor cost.

### Radar
Radar can outperform in adverse weather and price-sensitive safety applications. Multimodal architectures may preserve lidar but can also cap content.

### Other lidar
Lidar itself can commoditize. Durable capture requires scale, architecture/IP, software, qualification and customer workflow — not merely better point-cloud specs.

### Platform bundling
NVIDIA, Qualcomm, autonomy-stack vendors and OEMs can absorb perception functions. Ouster's risk is becoming a hardware input while the platform captures software economics.

## Financial path

Current evidence supports a credible path toward breakeven but not yet durable FCF. The key Gate-E bridge is:

**revenue growth → normalized gross margin → opex leverage → EBITDA breakeven → FCF conversion**

The US$263m liquidity position gives runway, reducing near-term financing pressure, but dilution/acquisition integration still matter.

## Gate-D decision

**PROMOTE for bounded Gate-E valuation work, but do not add to Top-10 yet.**

The operating evidence is strong enough that Ouster deserves a current valuation/downside/catalyst model. It has not yet demonstrated enough pricing power, recurring software economics or FCF to displace an existing Top-10 candidate solely on the sensing thesis.
