# Humanoid mass-production sensing / perception discovery — 25 September 2026

**Backlog:** #262, parent #261  
**Method:** world change → scarce complement → qualification/production transition → company materiality.

## World change

Humanoid robotics is moving from prototype demonstrations toward platforms designed for serial production. That changes the perception problem: a lab prototype can tolerate expensive/custom sensing and calibration; a mass-produced robot needs repeatable, compact, power-efficient, manufacturable sensing modules that survive qualification and can be supplied at volume.

The discovery question is therefore not “which camera company benefits from robots?” It is:

> **Which perception supplier has crossed from PoC/design-in into an awarded or mass-production programme, while the resulting business is still small enough to transform the company?**

## Bottleneck map

Humanoid perception stack:
**image/depth sensor → optics/module → stereo/depth calibration → ISP/edge processing → sensor fusion → safety/redundancy → robot platform qualification**

Potential scarcity migrates toward:
- calibrated stereo/depth modules;
- reliable compact camera-module manufacturing;
- low-light/high-dynamic-range sensors;
- edge perception compute;
- safety/redundant perception;
- platform-specific qualification and yield.

Commodity image sensors alone are less attractive unless qualification or packaging creates defensibility.

## Negative scan / falsifiers

1. **Sensor commoditisation:** image sensors and camera modules can be multi-sourced; qualification alone does not guarantee pricing power.
2. **Vertical integration:** major robot OEMs can internalize perception modules/calibration, reducing supplier economics.
3. **Architecture uncertainty:** humanoids may converge on different RGB/stereo/lidar/event-camera combinations; there may be no single standard stack.
4. **Volume uncertainty:** “mass production confirmed” does not reveal whether the underlying humanoid programme reaches meaningful unit volume.
5. **Customer concentration:** a sole-source award can create both convex upside and extreme programme/customer risk.
