# Namuga — humanoid stereo-camera programme underwrite

**Ticker:** KOSDAQ:190510  
**Discovery origin:** #262, parent #261  
**State:** **POTENTIAL / UNDERWRITE — unusually attractive early setup, programme economics not yet disclosed**  
**Boundary:** research conclusion only; no brokerage action.

## Why it matters

The discovery chain is unusually close to the target pattern:

**humanoid industrialisation → perception/calibration bottleneck → PoC → technical verification → sole-source award → initial supply → mass production**

Namuga is still a small incumbent camera-module company rather than a robotics pure-play, which is exactly why a successful new robot-vision engine could transform consolidated economics.

## Primary evidence chain

**FACT — Namuga, 14 May 2026:** following its January selection as a 3D sensing camera-module partner for a global automotive robot platform, Namuga said it was setting up a production line for full operation in June and expanding collaboration from lower-body obstacle sensing into the robot “head”.

**FACT — 7 Sep company-announcement reporting:** Namuga said it would exclusively supply a 3D stereo-camera solution for a next-generation humanoid at a large North American robot company after PoC/technology verification. Initial supply is expected in early 2027 and full-scale production in 2028 as the customer builds a large-scale production system.

**FACT — technical moat claimed:** the module uses Namuga's Relative Active Alignment process to fine-tune the two cameras' relative position/angle for accurate 3D depth, with manufacturing capability suitable for mass production.

**INTERPRETATION:** this is stronger than a prototype/design-in signal. It establishes qualification, production preparation and sole-source status. It does **not** establish unit volume, price/content, contract value or programme profitability.

## Programme materiality bridge

The critical variables are undisclosed, so use scenario math rather than pretend precision.

Let:
- annual robot units = **U**
- Namuga stereo-camera modules per robot = **C**
- realised revenue per module = **P**
- sole-source share = approximately 100% while award remains exclusive.

Annual programme revenue = **U × C × P**.

A recent external datapoint strengthens the volume question rather than answering it: Hyundai/Boston Dynamics is building Atlas production capability targeting tens of thousands of robots annually. That supports industrial-scale humanoid demand as plausible, but does **not** identify Namuga's unnamed customer and must not be used as its unit forecast.

## 3x / 5x feasibility

Use **KRW215bn** controlled equity reference:

- 3x → KRW645bn
- 5x → KRW1.075tn
- 10x → KRW2.15tn

At 20x earnings:
- 3x requires ~KRW32bn annual net income;
- 5x ~KRW54bn;
- 10x ~KRW108bn.

At 25x:
- 3x ~KRW26bn;
- 5x ~KRW43bn;
- 10x ~KRW86bn.

Versus 2025 net profit KRW25.1bn, **3x does not require heroic earnings growth if the market re-rates a demonstrably growing Physical-AI engine**. A 25x multiple on roughly current earnings already approaches the 3x equity hurdle; alternatively a more conservative 20x requires only ~28% net-income growth.

That does not make 3x a forecast. It means the starting valuation does **not** mathematically kill the thesis, unlike several late-discovery winners.

5x requires a much more material robot-vision engine; 10x requires major company transformation.

## Why the setup is attractive

1. **Small starting equity:** ~KRW215bn.
2. **Existing profitable base:** not dependent on financing to survive.
3. **Award quality:** PoC → verification → exclusive supply → mass-production preparation.
4. **Manufacturing moat claim:** stereo alignment/calibration rather than commodity sensor resale.
5. **Timing:** initial supply expected 2027, full production 2028, before financial materiality is visible.
6. **Valuation:** ~11x trailing earnings does not obviously price a successful new Physical-AI growth engine.

## Why it can fail

1. **Unnamed customer:** the largest source of uncertainty.
2. **Programme volume:** “mass production” may still mean modest unit counts.
3. **ASP/content:** no disclosed module price or cameras/robot.
4. **Customer concentration:** sole-source upside is also binary programme risk.
5. **Vertical integration / redesign:** robot OEM can internalize calibration/module assembly or switch architecture.
6. **Commodity pressure:** underlying image sensors are not the moat; manufacturing/calibration must remain hard to substitute.
7. **Legacy business:** TTM revenue/profit have weakened in 2026, so robotics may initially offset rather than add to a growing base.

## Unicorn Potential conclusion

**State: POTENTIAL / UNDERWRITE**

- **3x:** economically plausible and worth underwriting; not validated.
- **5x:** plausible only if humanoid/robot vision becomes a large consolidated earnings engine.
- **10x:** unsupported today.

Namuga is currently a **cleaner original-thesis setup than Vicor or AP Memory** because the qualification/mass-production signal is visible while the starting equity value remains small and undemanding. It is also earlier than TAI-TECH in financial proof, so uncertainty is materially higher.

## What would validate it

Promote confidence if:
- customer identity becomes verifiable;
- first 2027 robot-vision revenue/order value is disclosed;
- dedicated production capacity/utilization becomes visible;
- robot/mobility segment reaches ~5–10% of revenue with superior margin;
- additional robot OEM/platform awards diversify customer risk;
- 2028 production guidance provides a credible unit/content bridge.

Park/downgrade if:
- initial orders are immaterial;
- full-scale production slips materially beyond 2028;
- sole-source status changes;
- module economics resemble low-margin legacy camera assembly;
- legacy earnings deteriorate faster than robotics scales.

## Monitoring trigger

This is now an event-driven underwrite. The highest-information next evidence is **first disclosed robot-vision revenue/order economics**, not more thematic research.

Do not manufacture precision around the unnamed customer before disclosure.
