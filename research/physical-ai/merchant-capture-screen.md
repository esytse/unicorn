## Objective

Test the four Physical AI Gate-B merchant-capture lanes against real companies:

1. simulation / digital-twin / closed-loop evaluation;
2. safety runtime / validation / certification;
3. merchant data / robot-brain / fleet-learning;
4. edge compute / deterministic runtime.

The key question is not whether a function is technically important. It is whether a company can retain economics across **multiple customers and deployments** through recurring software/service revenue, data/learning rights, qualification/certification, switching/revalidation cost or installed-base effects.

## Executive conclusion

**INTERPRETATION:** The initial company screen strengthens the Gate-A conclusion that the most interesting Physical-AI merchant profit pools sit in **evaluation/safety/runtime/data infrastructure**, not generic sensors, model weights or commodity robot components.

The strongest private businesses are currently more convincing than the public market expressions:

1. **Applied Intuition** — strongest broad private benchmark across simulation, validation and increasingly full Physical-AI development/deployment tooling.
2. **BlackBerry QNX** — strongest small/mid-cap **public** candidate because safety-critical deterministic runtime already has production economics, high gross margin, royalty backlog and enough size sensitivity for Physical AI to matter if robotics adoption becomes real.
3. **RTI** — strong private deterministic middleware/data-plane candidate with cross-industry production use and DDS/ROS 2 relevance.
4. **Brain Corp** — strongest private merchant fleet-learning/data-flywheel candidate found so far, with >50,000 deployed robots and >25m operating hours.
5. **Foretellix** — strongest specialist private verification/evaluation pure play, with multi-OEM evidence and standards influence.

**No small listed company passes Gate B yet.** BlackBerry/QNX merits a dedicated company underwriting because it is the cleanest listed candidate surfaced by this screen. Qt Group is smaller but currently too peripheral to the autonomy-control loop; UL Solutions has real certification economics but robotics is unlikely to move total-company earnings soon; Hexagon has excellent Physical-AI assets but is too broad and increasingly participates as an integrated robotics vendor rather than a clean merchant infrastructure pure play.

## #100 — Simulation / digital twin / evaluation

### Applied Intuition — private benchmark

**FACT:** Applied says it serves **18 of the top 20 global automakers** and major U.S. defense programs. Its public customer examples include Porsche, General Motors, Toyota and Isuzu using its software for vehicle architecture, virtual testing, sensor simulation and autonomy development.

**FACT:** In July 2026 it launched **Dana**, positioned as an agentic platform spanning build, test, deploy and operate workflows for Physical AI across autonomy, robotics, mining, construction and other intelligent-machine use cases.

**FACT:** Applied's research programme now includes closed-loop RL/post-training, world models, simulation, robot learning and large-scale real-world/synthetic data. The company states that its business generates profitable revenue; third-party private-company estimates put 2025 ARR around $830m, but this is not audited public evidence and should not be treated as fact.

**FACT:** The company raised/tendered at a **$15bn valuation in 2025**.

**INTERPRETATION:** Applied is the clearest evidence that simulation/evaluation can escape a single OEM and become a multi-customer merchant platform. Its move into VehicleOS, autonomy stacks and Dana also shows the profit pool may broaden from standalone simulation into an integrated development/deployment workflow.

**HYPOTHESIS:** The durable moat is not generic simulator physics. It is accumulated workflow integration, proprietary scenario/evaluation tooling, customer-specific calibration, embedded engineering relationships and the ability to connect real-world logs to closed-loop test/deployment.

**OPEN QUESTIONS:**
- What fraction of revenue is recurring software versus forward-deployed engineering/services?
- Does software stay vendor-neutral as Applied sells more of the autonomy/VehicleOS stack?
- How much customer data can be reused across deployments?
- Is $15bn already capitalizing most of the merchant-platform upside?

### Foretellix — specialist evaluation challenger

**FACT:** Foretellix's Foretify platform combines real-world drive logs, simulation, scenario generation, coverage analysis and safety evidence. It cites customers/partners including Volvo Group, Torc/Daimler Truck, Isuzu and Woven/Toyota.

**FACT:** Foretellix led development of **ASAM OpenSCENARIO 2.0** and provides native support in Foretify.

**FACT:** NVIDIA invested in Foretellix's Series C and its platform integrates with NVIDIA DRIVE, Omniverse/Cosmos and the 2026 Alpamayo ecosystem.

**FACT:** Foretellix had raised **$135m** by the 2023 Series C close; newer valuation/revenue data are not publicly verified.

**INTERPRETATION:** Foretellix is closer to the specific 4.6–4.7 Gate-A evaluation bottleneck than most software companies. Standards influence can be an advantage even if OpenSCENARIO itself remains open because the economic value may sit in orchestration, coverage, analytics and reusable validation methodology.

**OPEN QUESTIONS:**
- Can it expand beyond road autonomy into robotics, drones and industrial systems?
- Does OpenSCENARIO standardization help Foretellix scale or commoditize its core layer?
- What paid multi-year production/renewal evidence exists?

### Hexagon — public benchmark, not yet a pure-play pass

**FACT:** Hexagon describes a manufacturing digital-twin feedback loop that links real measurement/quality data back to digital models in real time.

**FACT:** Hexagon Robotics has announced 2026 Physical-AI collaborations with NVIDIA and Microsoft and is deploying its AEON humanoid into industrial applications.

**INTERPRETATION:** Hexagon has unusually strong physical/digital calibration assets — metrology, spatial intelligence, quality data and industrial deployment — but the investment thesis is diluted by group size and by the fact that Hexagon is also becoming an integrated robot/system vendor.

## #101 — Safety runtime / validation / certification

### BlackBerry QNX — strongest listed Gate-B lead

**FACT:** FY2026 QNX revenue was **$268.0m, +14% y/y**, with **83% adjusted gross margin** and **$71m adjusted EBITDA / 26% margin**.

**FACT:** QNX ended FY2026 with an approximately **$950m royalty backlog** and says its embedded technology is in **more than 275m vehicles**.

**FACT:** BlackBerry explicitly says QNX has growing momentum in **robotics, Physical AI and adjacent markets**. During 2026 QNX showcased its General Embedded Development Platform and deterministic/safety foundations for next-generation robotics and Physical AI.

**FACT:** At the 4 Sep 2026 reference BlackBerry's market capitalization was about **$4.5bn**. QNX represented roughly half of FY2026 group revenue, so success in a new QNX end-market can be material to the whole company.

**INTERPRETATION:** QNX is the first public name in this screen where the desired economics already exist in another Physical-AI-adjacent market: high-margin safety/runtime software, production design-ins, per-unit royalty economics and a large contracted royalty backlog. The Gate-B question is whether robotics/industrial autonomy becomes a second scalable royalty pool rather than remaining conference/demo activity.

**HYPOTHESIS:** If Physical AI adopts certified deterministic runtime similarly to automotive, QNX could be a high-quality picks-and-shovels exposure because model/accelerator architectures may change while safety OS/middleware qualification persists.

**THESIS BREAKERS:**
- no named high-volume robotics production wins over the next 12–24 months;
- robotics buyers standardize around open Linux/ROS stacks without paid QNX layers;
- Qualcomm/NVIDIA/in-house stacks absorb the safety/runtime layer;
- QNX growth remains automotive-only while the stock re-rates on unsupported Physical-AI expectations.

### UL Solutions — certification is real, but financial materiality is weak

**FACT:** UL Solutions issued its first global certification for a public-facing robot (Simbe's Tally) in March 2026 and its first hazardous-location robotics certification under UL 6260 in June 2026.

**FACT:** UL already provides robotics testing/certification across industrial, collaborative, mobile and service robots, including ISO 10218, IEC 61508/62061 and related safety regimes.

**INTERPRETATION:** Certification is becoming a real deployment gate as autonomous systems move into public/hazardous environments. However, UL is a ~US$15bn broad testing/certification company, so Physical-AI growth may strengthen the franchise without being a sufficiently pure earnings driver for 'unicorn-like' asymmetry.

### Qt Group — small public adjacency worth monitoring

**FACT:** Qt Safe Renderer 2.2, released/certified in August 2026, is qualified to **ISO 26262 ASIL D**, **IEC 61508 SIL 3**, EN 50128 SIL 4 and other standards.

**FACT:** Qt's safety/quality portfolio also includes Axivion static analysis/architecture verification and other pre-certified tools.

**FACT:** Qt Group's market capitalization was about **€0.9bn** in early September 2026.

**INTERPRETATION:** Qt is small enough to be interesting, but current safety value is concentrated in UI/rendering and development-quality tooling rather than deterministic autonomy control. It should remain a watch candidate unless robotics design wins demonstrate a broader safety/runtime role.

## #102 — Merchant data / robot-brain / fleet learning

### Brain Corp — strongest evidence of a merchant fleet-learning flywheel

**FACT:** Brain Corp says BrainOS powers **more than 50,000 robots** across six continents and has logged **more than 25m autonomous operating hours**.

**FACT:** In H1 2026 it reported **68% y/y growth in global deployments** and more than 5.3m autonomous hours in the half.

**FACT:** BrainOS is sold as an OEM/enterprise autonomy platform rather than one captive robot fleet. Brain Corp says every mile driven by a BrainOS-powered robot feeds data back into the platform, improving navigation/reliability, and cites >10,000 brands/OEM partners.

**FACT:** Platform use spans floor cleaning, inventory/sensing and material movement; named ecosystem relationships include Tennant and Dane Technologies, and Sam's Club uses BrainOS-powered inventory scanning.

**INTERPRETATION:** This is the clearest evidence found so far that the **deployment-data flywheel can be merchantized across multiple robot OEMs/customers**, which is exactly the hard Gate-B test in #102.

**OPEN QUESTIONS / critical diligence:**
- What contractual rights let Brain Corp aggregate or learn from cross-customer telemetry?
- Which data are customer-owned versus platform-owned?
- How much of revenue is recurring software/cloud versus hardware/services/revenue share?
- Does learning transfer across cleaning, sensing and material-movement embodiments or remain use-case specific?
- Is the platform financially attractive after customer-support/field costs?

**INTERPRETATION:** Brain Corp should not pass Gate B until cross-customer learning rights and economics are verified, but it becomes the **private benchmark** for #102.

## #103 — Edge compute / deterministic runtime

### RTI — private middleware benchmark

**FACT:** RTI describes Connext as the leading DDS-based real-time data-streaming platform for intelligent physical systems, with **more than 2,000 designs** and use across autonomous vehicles, robotics, medical systems, aerospace/defense, energy and industrial applications.

**FACT:** Named production/customer evidence includes Volkswagen, XPeng, medical robotics and autonomous/mission-critical systems; RTI states that deployed systems using Connext exceed $1tn in aggregate value.

**FACT:** RTI has supported ROS 2 since inception and launched a new Connext Robotics Toolkit for ROS 2 in June 2026, with commercial production capabilities layered on top of free prototyping/research use.

**FACT:** Connext materials describe an **ISO 26262 ASIL-D safety certification pathway** and DDS is used by AUTOSAR Adaptive and ROS 2.

**INTERPRETATION:** RTI demonstrates that the most defensible edge layer may be neither the accelerator nor the base OS, but the deterministic, safety-aware real-time data plane connecting perception, control and distributed components.

**COUNTER-EVIDENCE:** DDS is an open standard. That increases ecosystem adoption but can reduce proprietary lock-in. ROS 2/open-source implementations, OEM middleware and competing commercial DDS vendors are explicit falsification.

### QNX overlap

QNX and RTI may be complementary rather than direct substitutes: QNX can own the certified OS/runtime foundation while RTI owns the real-time data plane/middleware. This stack-level split should be tested in dedicated company/customer evidence rather than assuming one captures the whole runtime profit pool.

### NVIDIA / Qualcomm

**INTERPRETATION:** NVIDIA and Qualcomm remain essential Gate-B benchmarks for edge compute, but neither is currently a 'unicorn-like' discovery candidate because Physical-AI robotics exposure is small relative to total-company economics and OEM custom silicon is meaningful falsification. They should be used to test platform pull-through, not promoted merely because local inference is required.

## What changed from the opening hypothesis

**Previous hypothesis:** the highest-upside company might be a small public company sitting directly in simulation/evaluation/safety/fleet learning.

**New conclusion:** the merchant platform thesis is supported, but **the strongest evidence-backed companies are mostly private**. The cleanest public exception currently surfaced is **BlackBerry/QNX** because it combines existing production royalty economics with a plausible Physical-AI expansion path and sufficient total-company sensitivity.

This is **not** a watchlist promotion. It is a Gate-B company-underwriting trigger.

## Recommended next work

### P1 — dedicated BlackBerry/QNX underwriting

Quantify:
- automotive vs robotics/industrial QNX design wins and revenue;
- royalty economics by production unit / design-win cohort where recoverable;
- General Embedded Development Platform monetization;
- named robotics OEMs and production timelines;
- competitive positioning vs Linux/ROS 2, Wind River, Green Hills, RTI, NVIDIA and Qualcomm;
- BlackBerry group valuation, QNX standalone value, Secure Communications value and corporate costs;
- 5–10 year bear/base/bull robotics contribution without assuming humanoid unit forecasts;
- current valuation and reverse return hurdles.

### P2 — Brain Corp data-rights/economics diligence

Do not promote the data flywheel until contracts/terms or customer statements establish whether learning can legally cross deployments and whether recurring software economics are attractive.

### P3 — Applied vs Foretellix merchant-evaluation comparison

Separate broad integrated platform value from pure V&V value and determine whether open standards/NVIDIA infrastructure expand or compress independent supplier economics.

### P4 — RTI economics / ownership

Establish ownership, revenue scale, licensing model, profitability and public-investment accessibility; technically it is one of the strongest Gate-B fits but financial evidence is currently insufficient.
