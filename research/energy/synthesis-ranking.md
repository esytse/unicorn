# AI Energy — Cross-Layer Bottleneck Ranking

**Status:** Common-basis structural / economic-capture ranking complete  
**Confidence:** **Medium-High** on the relative top tier; **Medium** on company-level capture before underwriting  
**Last substantive update:** 2026-09-07  
**Backlog:** issue #52

## Executive conclusion

The E2E map identifies many technically difficult layers, but only a smaller set combines:

- structural scarcity;
- direct enough AI / data-centre sensitivity;
- concentrated or qualified suppliers;
- visible price / reservation / backlog / service capture;
- architecture resilience;
- near-enough timing for the economics to matter.

The current **supplier-underwriting priority** is:

1. **large gas-turbine equipment / slots + service**;
2. **transformers / critical substation equipment + MV/HV switchgear**;
3. **integrated thermal systems / qualified cooling capacity**;
4. **integrated data-centre electrical infrastructure / BTM orchestration**;
5. **Western LEU enrichment / UF6 conversion** as a separate nuclear strategic lane;
6. **physical transmission enabling equipment / EPC / GETs** selectively;
7. **nuclear heavy components / HALEU / specialist EPC** as longer-duration optionality.

This is **not** a company ranking and **not** an investment recommendation. It is the gate deciding where company Investment Capture work should concentrate.

The most important distinction is:

> **The strongest physical bottleneck is not always the best merchant profit pool.**

Physical transmission scores 4.7/5 structurally, but its economics are dispersed across utilities, regulators, EPC, conductors and equipment. Large gas turbines score slightly lower at 4.6/5, yet customer reservation behavior, concentrated OEM supply, pricing and recurring service make the merchant capture much clearer.

---

## 1. Ranking method

The ranking uses the validated Bottleneck Strength from the E2E work and adds seven underwriting dimensions scored **1–5**.

| Dimension | What a high score means |
|---|---|
| **AI sensitivity** | AI / data-centre buildout can materially change demand for the function rather than merely contribute to a broad industrial cycle |
| **Supplier concentration** | few qualified suppliers / difficult dual sourcing |
| **Capture evidence** | reservation, prepayment, pricing, margin, backlog or contract evidence shows scarcity converting to economics |
| **Architecture resilience** | function remains necessary if generation source, site architecture, 800 VDC or cooling topology changes |
| **Recurring / service economics** | installed base, long-term service, fuel / capacity contracts or recurring lifecycle economics supplement equipment sales |
| **Timing** | demand is relevant to 2026–2030 earnings rather than mostly 2030s optionality |
| **Capital / overbuild resilience** | capacity expansion is difficult enough that new capex is less likely to destroy economics quickly; 5 is most favorable |

### Underwriting Priority Score

For triage only:

> **average of Bottleneck Strength + the seven dimensions above**

This is deliberately simple. It is not a statistical model and should not override contradictory evidence.

A layer can still enter a strategic lane with a lower composite if it has exceptional concentration / optionality that is structurally different from the near-term AI buildout.

### Why the composite is not the final answer

The table deliberately exposes tensions:

- **LEU enrichment** scores strongly because concentration and contractual economics are exceptional, but AI is only one marginal driver.
- **physical transmission** is extraordinarily structural and AI-sensitive, but supplier capture is dispersed and capital / project execution can consume returns.
- **integrated thermal** has direct AI sensitivity and customer prepayment evidence, but a broader supplier ecosystem than turbines or enrichment.

Issue #53 must therefore underwrite **companies**, not buy the highest numeric row.

## 3. Top tier #1 — large gas-turbine equipment / slots / service

### Why it ranks first

**Bottleneck Strength: 4.6/5.**

This layer has the clearest combination of physical scarcity and merchant capture:

- GE Vernova's slot-reservation / backlog commitments are multiple years of current production;
- Siemens Energy is sold out through FY2028 with later slots filling;
- hot-section blades / vanes constrain output;
- customers reserve future production;
- new-unit pricing / margins are favorable;
- the qualified advanced-class supplier set is concentrated;
- service economics extend for decades after installation.

### AI sensitivity

AI is a meaningful marginal accelerator through both utility and onsite / electrically proximate generation. It is **not** the only driver: replacement generation, grid reliability, industrial demand and Middle East growth provide non-AI support.

That is positive for earnings durability but means company research must isolate how much of the current cycle is AI-specific.

### Main falsification

- capacity expansion shortens slot scarcity faster than expected;
- data-centre onsite-gas projects fail to reach construction;
- gas pipeline / permitting constraints cap deployment;
- renewables / storage / nuclear alternatives reduce incremental turbine demand;
- current valuation already capitalizes peak turbine margins and long service attachment.

### #53 target archetype

Compare **GE Vernova, Siemens Energy and Mitsubishi Heavy Industries** on:

- installed-base / service economics;
- gas-turbine revenue sensitivity;
- slot pricing / backlog conversion;
- capacity expansion;
- broader-company dilution;
- valuation only later in Gate C.

Also search for smaller high-value hot-section / component / service suppliers **only if** primary evidence shows real qualification / bottleneck ownership.

## 4. Top tier #2 — transformers / substations + MV/HV switchgear

These are grouped because many investable suppliers span both, but the economics should remain separable.

### Transformers

**Bottleneck Strength: 4.7/5.**

Strengths:

- long lead times;
- custom / fragmented specifications;
- qualification and factory-test constraints;
- GOES / copper / transport dependencies;
- highly architecture-resilient across grid, gas, nuclear, BTM and data-centre designs.

The strongest revealed preference is customers paying extraordinary logistics costs to secure delivery schedule.

### Switchgear

**Bottleneck Strength: 4.4/5.**

Supply is broader, but the function is unavoidable and data-centre / grid order evidence is strong.

### Why this lane ranks second

It has less concentrated merchant capture than turbines but superior architecture resilience. Every incremental power source — gas, nuclear, renewables, BTM — can create more transformation / switching demand.

### Main falsification

- Hitachi / GE Vernova / Eaton / other factory expansions normalize lead times rapidly;
- DOE / utility standardisation reduces custom complexity;
- high industry capex produces overcapacity after 2028;
- apparent margin strength is mix / backlog timing rather than durable pricing.

### #53 target archetypes

Underwrite both broad platforms and smaller specialists:

- Eaton;
- Schneider Electric;
- ABB;
- GE Vernova / Prolec exposure;
- Hitachi / Hitachi Energy exposure;
- HD Hyundai Electric;
- other listed transformer / switchgear specialists where AI / grid demand can materially move total earnings.

A deliberate **small-cap / specialist screen** is required because the obvious multinational platforms may have excellent quality but limited asymmetry.

## 5. Top tier #3 — integrated thermal systems / qualified cooling capacity

### Why it ranks highly

**Integrated thermal chain: 4.4/5.**

Cooling has the **highest direct AI sensitivity** of the top structural lanes. High-density compute converts nearly every electrical MW into heat.

Strong evidence includes:

- Modine's >$4bn long-term capacity agreement;
- $165m customer prepayment to fund expansion;
- rapid data-centre revenue growth;
- Eaton's ~$9.5bn Boyd Thermal acquisition;
- Schneider / Motivair and Vertiv expansion across the thermal chain.

### Why it does not rank #1

- OCP is standardising interfaces;
- cold plates / CDUs are increasingly multi-source;
- multiple incumbents and ODMs are adding capacity;
- thermal topology can change with coolant temperature and site climate.

The **function** is scarce; many individual components may not be.

### What to own conceptually

Favor suppliers spanning multiple thermal stages:

```text
chip interface
→ liquid distribution / CDU
→ controls / heat exchange
→ facility loop
→ heat rejection
→ lifecycle service
```

rather than companies tied to one cold-plate or chiller implementation.

### #53 target archetypes

- Modine / Airedale;
- Vertiv;
- Schneider / Motivair;
- Eaton / Boyd Thermal;
- nVent and evidence-backed thermal / fluid-connectivity specialists;
- smaller listed specialists only where qualified capacity and customer evidence are direct.

## 6. Top tier #4 — integrated electrical infrastructure + BTM orchestration

This lane combines two functions that increasingly converge in data-centre architecture.

### Data-centre electrical backbone

**4.3/5**, with direct AI order / backlog / margin evidence.

### BTM integration

**4.1/5**, with value in:

- site architecture;
- controls;
- protection;
- synchronization;
- integration of grid / generation / storage;
- schedule execution.

### Why this is a platform lane rather than component hunting

Supply is broad at component level. The potential moat is the ability to deliver a qualified **grid-to-rack / source-to-load system** with reference architectures, testing and lifecycle service.

Vertiv's UtilityInnovation Group acquisition is direct strategic evidence that BTM / microgrid control is being pulled into the data-centre infrastructure platform.

### Main risk

The broad platform winners are already obvious and may trade at valuations that price in the AI infrastructure cycle. Gate B must establish earnings sensitivity; Gate C must be strict.

## 7. Strategic nuclear lane — Western enrichment / conversion

This lane should be researched separately from the immediate power-equipment top tier.

### Western LEU enrichment — 4.6/5

Exceptional characteristics:

- concentrated supplier set;
- geopolitical substitution pressure;
- multi-year centrifuge expansion;
- long-term contracting;
- strategic government support;
- exposure to existing fleet as well as future nuclear.

### UF6 conversion — 4.3/5

Cameco describes Port Hope as one of only three Western UF6 suppliers.

### Why not rank with turbines despite strong scores?

AI data centres are **not yet the primary demand driver**. Hyperscaler nuclear contracting improves the long-run demand signal, but existing-fleet fuel requirements and geopolitical policy dominate near-term economics.

### #53 implication

Treat this as a **strategic diversification / optionality lane**, not proof that it is the best AI-pure-play investment.

Potential listed exposure:

- Centrus Energy — direct enrichment / HALEU optionality, high policy / execution risk;
- Cameco — uranium + conversion + indirect Westinghouse exposure, broader / more diversified;
- other public fuel-cycle exposure only if primary evidence shows direct qualified capacity.

Urenco and Orano are important industry benchmarks but not simple listed equities.

## 8. Selective lane — physical transmission enabling

**Bottleneck Strength: 4.7/5.**

It is structurally one of the strongest constraints, but the value pool is fragmented across:

- utilities;
- regulatory approvals;
- EPC / engineering;
- conductors;
- towers / structures;
- substations;
- GETs / grid controls;
- transformers / switchgear already ranked separately.

### Why selective rather than top-tier broad underwriting

More volume does not guarantee higher returns if:

- project labor / material inflation absorbs price;
- fixed-price execution risk is high;
- utilities capture the economic rent;
- competition is broad;
- capital intensity / working capital rises with backlog.

### #53 implication

Run a targeted screen rather than a large company wave:

- Quanta Services as an EPC / execution benchmark;
- advanced conductor / reconductoring suppliers;
- grid-enhancing technology suppliers with evidence of qualification and adoption;
- smaller transmission specialists only if margin / backlog / moat evidence is strong.

---

## 9. Longer-duration nuclear optionality — do not confuse scarcity with investability

### HALEU — 4.7/5

Technically one of the strongest bottlenecks, but:

- commercial demand is tied to advanced-reactor timing;
- government procurement materially shapes market economics;
- alternative reactor / fuel designs can change HALEU demand;
- new capacity is being subsidized deliberately.

### Heavy forgings — 4.5/5

Excellent qualification / concentration evidence, but reactor output is mainly a 2030s story and public company exposure can be diluted by broader businesses.

### Nuclear EPC / QA — 4.4/5

Very real structural constraint; weaker clean margin capture because delivery risk and labor intensity can absorb economics.

### Decision

Carry these into #53 only as **selective optionality / comparator work**, not a large immediate underwriting wave.

## 10. Deprioritised broad themes

### Generic BESS

**3.4/5.** Important function; broad cell / system supply. Investigate integration, not battery scarcity.

### Reciprocating gensets

**3.6/5.** Strong data-centre demand and service revenue, but broad suppliers / modular expansion limit structural scarcity.

### Generic 800 VDC exposure

**~3.6/5 supplier scarcity.** Technical transition is real; open standards and 80+ ecosystem participants argue against generic moat claims.

### Generic cold plates / CDUs

**~3.8/5 supplier scarcity.** DTC is increasingly mandatory but component standardisation / multi-sourcing is rising.

### Uranium miners

**3.5/5 bottleneck score.** Nuclear-cycle exposure is real, but the tightest structural layers are downstream.

These themes can still produce attractive stocks. They simply do not get priority because of the bottleneck thesis alone.

## 12. Investment Capture questions #53 must answer

For each company:

1. What percentage of revenue / EBIT is exposed to the validated bottleneck today?
2. Is AI / data-centre demand large enough to change **total-company** earnings?
3. Is scarcity producing price / margin / service economics or only backlog / revenue?
4. Does capacity expansion improve share or create overbuild risk?
5. How concentrated are customers / projects?
6. Is the company architecture-resilient?
7. What recurring installed-base / service economics exist?
8. Is the company small enough for the new profit pool to matter?
9. What contradictory evidence would break the thesis?
10. Which smaller specialist offers better asymmetry than the platform incumbents?

No valuation-based promotion should occur until #54, but obvious valuation excess can be noted as execution risk.

## 13. Falsification carried forward

The ranking should change if evidence shows:

- gas-turbine slot scarcity normalizes faster than backlog converts;
- transformer lead times / pricing fall sharply after current capacity additions;
- thermal capacity becomes commoditized after 2027 expansions;
- data-centre electrical standards materially reduce integration value;
- BTM becomes a temporary bridge abandoned after utility connection;
- enrichment overbuild occurs before nuclear demand materializes;
- physical transmission demand converts mostly to low-margin construction volume;
- hyperscaler AI buildout is materially lower than funded / contracted infrastructure suggests.
