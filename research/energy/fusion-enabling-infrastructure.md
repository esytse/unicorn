# Fusion enabling infrastructure — first-pass picks-and-shovels map

**Date:** 2026-09-17  
**Issue:** #155  
**Status:** first-pass discovery; not a portfolio thesis

## Decision summary

**INTERPRETATION:** Fusion is a credible long-duration industrial demand branch, but current public evidence does **not** justify adding a fusion-specific name to the Gate-E Top 10 solely for March-2028 returns. The best public suppliers found are businesses whose scarce capabilities already monetize in other markets; fusion should be treated as optionality until orders/capacity become financially material.

**First-pass shortlist:**
1. **Furukawa Electric (5801.T)** — strongest direct commercial-fusion HTS evidence and the most interesting follow-up candidate.
2. **VAT Group (VACN.SW)** — unusually direct ITER-qualified ultra-high-vacuum valve evidence with strong semiconductor-market fallback economics.
3. **Sumitomo Electric Industries (5802.T)** — direct ITER tungsten divertor-component production evidence plus broad non-fusion industrial earnings support.

**No Gate-E ACTION / REASSESS is created by this discovery.** A dedicated company underwrite is warranted only if fusion exposure becomes large enough to affect 18-month earnings/capex/valuation, or if a supplier wins a material funded order/capacity commitment.

## Company evidence

### Furukawa Electric / SuperPower

**FACT:** Furukawa Electric and group company SuperPower manufacture REBCO high-temperature-superconducting tape used by Tokamak Energy. In 2023 the parties said production had begun for several hundred kilometres of HTS wire for ST80-HTS and were evaluating scale-up for pilot/commercial plants.

**FACT:** In June 2025 Furukawa and Tokamak Energy agreed to establish a joint Japanese operational base for fusion-magnet technology; Furukawa described HTS tape as a critical input to high-field fusion magnets and highlighted non-fusion applications in medicine, manufacturing, power and transport.

**FACT:** A June 2026 Furukawa/Tokamak Energy collaboration announcement says Tokamak Energy had been selected as the UK STEP Magnet Systems Partner under a £70m contract through 2029 and that STEP is expected to use about **30,000 km of HTS tape**. Furukawa and Tokamak Energy are evaluating UK manufacturing capability.

**FACT:** Furukawa also invested in Japan's Starlight Engine in July 2026 to support the FAST fusion project.

**INTERPRETATION:** This is the strongest evidence in the screen that a listed industrial company owns a potentially scarce fusion input with real supply history, manufacturing scale-up activity and multiple fusion programmes. The non-fusion HTS applications improve the downside case if fusion slips.

**OPEN QUESTION:** What share of Furukawa/SuperPower revenue and incremental capex could STEP/FAST/Tokamak orders represent through March 2028? Is HTS tape supply structurally concentrated enough to generate pricing power, or will capacity expansion commoditize it?

### VAT Group

**FACT:** VAT describes itself as an ITER development partner and developed a dedicated ITER vacuum-valve catalog. The products are designed for ultra-high vacuum, extreme temperature, radiation/tritium, magnetic-field and seismic conditions. VAT says the catalog contains roughly 140 gate/angle valves and includes a first-of-kind 1.6 m all-metal pendulum valve.

**FACT:** VAT's core business is high-vacuum solutions for semiconductor, display and digital-technology manufacturing.

**INTERPRETATION:** VAT offers a particularly clean picks-and-shovels pattern: difficult fusion qualification is real, while the core economic engine does not depend on fusion commercialization. The drawback for Gate E is likely total-company sensitivity: fusion orders must become much larger before they can move earnings relative to semiconductor vacuum demand.

**OPEN QUESTION:** Has VAT won material orders from private fusion developers or STEP beyond ITER, and what is the revenue/margin scale?

### Sumitomo Electric / Allied Material

**FACT:** Sumitomo Electric group company Allied Material completed delivery in November 2025 of about **32,000 tungsten monoblocks** for 13 ITER inner vertical-target divertor units. It also has orders for all 58 Japanese-procurement outer vertical-target units, with 30 delivered at the time of the announcement and 28 remaining.

**FACT:** The company states that ITER divertor surfaces can reach roughly 2,300°C and that its tungsten monoblocks were selected for thermal-shock resistance.

**INTERPRETATION:** This is direct manufacturing/qualification evidence for an extreme-materials bottleneck, not thematic adjacency. However, Sumitomo Electric is a large diversified group, so fusion exposure may remain financially immaterial within the Gate-E window.

**OPEN QUESTION:** Can Allied Material's ITER qualification translate into private fusion/reactor orders at a scale that matters to group earnings, and are alternative plasma-facing materials likely to displace tungsten?

## Falsification / risks

- Commercial fusion timelines slip well beyond the 2030s, leaving supplier revenue limited to research/prototype programmes.
- Reactor architectures diverge: stellarator, inertial, magnetized-target or alternative tokamak designs may require different supplier stacks.
- HTS capacity scales faster than demand and removes scarcity/pricing power.
- Plasma-facing materials change as neutron/heat-flux evidence accumulates.
- ITER qualification does not automatically transfer to private commercial-reactor procurement.
- Fusion orders remain too small inside diversified public suppliers to affect per-share economics.

## Gate-E implication

**CONCLUSION:** Park fusion as a monitored energy substream rather than promote it into the active Top 10 today. **Furukawa Electric deserves the first company-level follow-up** if a bounded underwrite can establish (a) HTS supply concentration, (b) funded 2027–28 capacity/order visibility, and (c) enough total-company sensitivity to create March-2028 asymmetry without capitalizing long-dated fusion plants. VAT is the strongest benchmark/second follow-up; Sumitomo Electric is a materials benchmark.
