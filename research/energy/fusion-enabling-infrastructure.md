# Fusion Enabling Infrastructure — Bottleneck Map

**Status:** first-pass discovery complete; monitor / targeted follow-up  
**Backlog:** #155  
**Created:** 2026-09-17  
**Purpose:** test whether fusion creates investable picks-and-shovels opportunities without requiring a bet on which fusion developer wins.

## Executive conclusion

**INTERPRETATION — Fusion is a real and growing industrial supply-chain market, but for most public companies it is not yet large enough to drive an 18-month total-company earnings thesis.** The strongest near-term research signal is not a fusion developer. It is the overlap between fusion-specific qualification and technologies already monetising in semiconductors, grid/power, medical/scientific equipment, nuclear and industrial automation.

The most credible bottleneck clusters from the first pass are:

1. **HTS / superconducting conductors, integrated magnets and cryogenics** — technically central to several magnetic-confinement architectures and already generating explicit contracts/partnerships.
2. **Extreme-condition materials, heat management and fuel systems** — identified by the 2026 Fusion Industry Association supplier survey as the dominant long-term supply-chain concerns, but public-company capture is less cleanly evidenced in this pass.
3. **High-vacuum valves and vacuum architecture** — required at reactor scale and already subject to bespoke radiation/temperature engineering at ITER.
4. **Remote maintenance / robotics** — unavoidable once activated reactor regions become inaccessible to people, but current supplier capture is fragmented and often private/project-engineering based.
5. **High-voltage / pulsed power, diagnostics and controls** — necessary, technically demanding and potentially attractive, but the fusion revenue contribution to diversified public suppliers remains unclear.

**Portfolio implication:** no public company is promoted into the active top-10 solely because of fusion. Fusion should currently be treated as a *free/low-priced option on top of a business that already works without fusion*. The cleanest public-company follow-up from this pass is **Furukawa Electric**, because there is direct HTS supply evidence, an investment/strategic relationship with Tokamak Energy, and broader infrastructure/electronics businesses. **VAT Group** is a second useful comparator because it has direct ITER-specific vacuum-valve engineering plus a much larger semiconductor-vacuum franchise. Neither is promoted without a fresh Gate-E underwrite.

## Market evidence

### FACT

- The Fusion Industry Association's 2026 supply-chain survey reported **$538m** of 2025 spend among participating private fusion companies, up **24%**, with respondents projecting **$681m** in 2026. Seventy-five percent of surveyed suppliers reported investing to expand fusion capacity. The report identifies materials for extreme conditions, heat management and fuel systems as leading long-term concerns. [FIA, 23 Jun 2026](https://www.fusionindustryassociation.org/fia-launches-2026-fusion-industry-supply-chain-report/)
- The UK government's September 2026 fusion investment prospectus lists HTS magnets, gyrotrons, neutral beams, remote maintenance/robotics, diagnostics/sensors, fuel-cycle systems, fusion-hardened materials and lasers/optics among STEP supply-chain needs. [UK fusion investment prospectus](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/uk-fusion-investment-prospectus/uk-fusion-investment-prospectus-html)
- The 16 Sep 2026 FT review highlights the gap between scientific fusion gain and net-electric/commercial plants and the unresolved engineering/economic issues around radiation damage, maintenance, tritium breeding, recirculating power and cost. Developer commercialization dates and LCOE estimates therefore remain claims rather than settled evidence. [Financial Times, 16 Sep 2026](https://www.ft.com/content/ef511d46-a689-4868-9654-15b96a71586d)

### INTERPRETATION

The supply chain has moved beyond laboratory procurement, but current reported private-sector spend remains small relative to the revenue base of most investable industrial suppliers. The correct Unicorn question is therefore **which scarce capability can compound through non-fusion markets today and gain a material second demand leg if fusion scales?**

## Candidate screen

### Furukawa Electric — public; priority follow-up

**FACT**

- Furukawa subsidiary SuperPower agreed to supply several hundred kilometres of specialist HTS wire for Tokamak Energy's ST80-HTS programme. [Furukawa, 11 Jan 2023](https://www.furukawaelectric.com/en/release/2023/kenkai_20230112.html)
- Furukawa invested in Tokamak Energy in December 2023 and in 2026 expanded collaboration around potential UK HTS-tape capability. Furukawa describes applications spanning fusion, power transmission and medical devices. [Furukawa UK, Jul 2026](https://www.furukawa.co.uk/furukawa-electric-and-tokamak-energy-advance-collaboration-on-uk-hts-tape-capability/)
- Furukawa also invested in Japanese fusion company Starlight Engine in July 2026, providing another direct fusion relationship. [Furukawa, 15 Jul 2026](https://www.furukawaelectric.com/en/release/2026/metal_20260715.html)

**INTERPRETATION**

This is the best first-pass example of the desired structure: a listed diversified supplier with a directly evidenced fusion-enabling component and non-fusion end markets. The key unresolved question is whether HTS can become financially material to Furukawa within the active 18-month mandate rather than merely strategic optionality.

**OPEN QUESTIONS**

- Current SuperPower/Furukawa HTS revenue, capacity and margin profile.
- Share of global qualified REBCO/HTS supply and credible competitors.
- Capacity expansion economics and whether supply scales fast enough to destroy scarcity rents.
- 18-month catalysts: STEP procurement, Tokamak Energy volumes, new fusion/medical/grid customers, manufacturing expansion.

### VAT Group — public; priority comparator

**FACT**

VAT states that it acted as an ITER development partner and created an ITER-specific valve catalogue, including all-metal solutions engineered for extreme temperature/radiation and a 1.6 m pendulum isolation valve. VAT's core business is high-vacuum solutions for semiconductor, display and digital-technology production. [VAT ITER case study](https://www.vatgroup.com/case-studies/launching-fusion-energy-vat-supports-iter-with-all-metal-vacuum-valves)

**INTERPRETATION**

VAT fits the resilience test unusually well: fusion-specific engineering can be treated as optionality on top of an established semiconductor-vacuum franchise. However, the same diversification makes fusion unlikely to move total-company earnings soon. It is therefore a useful *quality comparator*, not automatically a fusion portfolio candidate.

**OPEN QUESTIONS**

- Current fusion order/revenue disclosure beyond ITER.
- Whether private fusion developers standardise on VAT or develop alternative architectures/suppliers.
- Incremental margin/qualification moat of fusion valves versus standard semiconductor vacuum products.

### Oxford Instruments — historical evidence; do not promote yet

**FACT**

Oxford Instruments' then-subsidiary Oxford Superconducting Technology won a >£30m ITER superconducting-wire contract in 2009, and ITER records OST supplying Nb3Sn strand. This establishes historical fusion qualification, not necessarily current investable exposure. [ITER supplier record](https://www.iter.org/node/20687/f4e-and-us-iter-share-nb3sn-strand-supplier)

**INTERPRETATION**

Do not infer a current Oxford Instruments fusion thesis from legacy ITER contracts. Corporate ownership/product mix must be re-validated before any follow-up.

## Private-company signal — useful for locating bottlenecks, not directly investable

- **Tokamak Energy / TE Magnetics:** £70m STEP Magnet Systems Partner contract to March 2029; September 2026 Demo4 campaign reports integrated operation of magnets, cryogenics, energisation, instrumentation, controls and quench protection at 13.7 T. This is strong evidence that the bottleneck is the integrated magnet system, not HTS tape alone. [Tokamak Energy, 9 Sep 2026](https://tokamakenergy.com/2026/09/09/tokamak-energy-completes-world-leading-fusion-magnet-campaign-validating-technology-for-commercial-hts-systems/)
- **Commonwealth Fusion Systems:** CFS has begun supplying/licensing HTS magnet technology beyond its own SPARC/ARC programme, including Realta Fusion and Type One Energy. This supports the hypothesis that fusion magnet IP/manufacturing can become a standalone supplier market, but CFS is private. [CFS commercial partners](https://www.cfs.energy/company/commercial-partners/)

## Falsification / anti-hype rules

1. Do not treat a fusion developer's target date as a portfolio catalyst unless a supplier has a funded order tied to it.
2. Do not count strategic partnerships, MOUs or investments as revenue without purchase/contract evidence.
3. Penalise a supplier if fusion remains too small to affect total-company earnings inside the remaining portfolio window.
4. Prefer capabilities with current semiconductor/grid/medical/nuclear/industrial demand so the thesis survives a 10–20 year fusion delay.
5. Re-test scarcity as manufacturing capacity expands; today's specialist capability may commoditise before fusion reaches volume.
6. Do not double-count conventional balance-of-plant exposure already captured in the AI-energy thesis.

## Decision from first pass

**No immediate portfolio promotion.**

The first pass justifies **one targeted Gate-E research issue on Furukawa Electric / SuperPower HTS economics and 18-month catalysts**. VAT Group remains a comparator/monitor unless evidence shows fusion orders becoming financially material. Extreme materials/heat/fuel-cycle suppliers remain an open discovery lane, but should not displace higher-priority P0/P1 portfolio work.

**Confidence:** Medium on the bottleneck map; Low-to-Medium on public-company financial capture because current fusion revenue disclosure is sparse.
