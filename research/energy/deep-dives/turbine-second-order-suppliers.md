# Gas-Turbine Second-Order Suppliers — Hot Section, Forgings & Service

**Status:** Initial second-order screen complete; hot-section blades / vanes / precision castings validated as a bottleneck, but no new Gate-C candidate  
**Backlog:** #72  
**Last substantive update:** 2026-09-08  
**Confidence:** High on hot-section physical scarcity; Medium-High on supplier capture; Medium on public-market asymmetry

## Executive conclusion

**CONCLUSION:** The gas-turbine supply chain contains a genuine **bottleneck within the bottleneck**: qualified hot-section blades, vanes and precision castings. These parts operate at extreme temperature and stress, require advanced superalloys, directional / single-crystal casting, ceramic cores, precision machining, thermal-barrier coatings and long qualification cycles. Evidence from OEMs and suppliers supports a **4.8 / 5 Bottleneck Strength score** for the hot-section blade / vane / precision-casting function.

However, unlike the transformer second-order screen, the research does **not** yet surface an obviously underpriced listed specialist.

- **Howmet Aerospace** is the strongest public evidence of direct merchant capture. It says it is the world's largest gas-turbine blade manufacturer; gas-turbine revenue was about **$1bn in 2025** and management expects **$2bn+ over the next 3–5 years**. Q2 2026 gas-turbine revenue grew **38% y/y**. Engine Products margins and group free cash flow are excellent. But Howmet is already roughly a **$95bn** company and, even after an ~8% 8-September selloff, trades around **44x forward earnings**. The first asymmetry has largely rerated.
- **Anhui Yingliu Electromechanical (603308)** is the strongest smaller listed manufacturing lead. H1 2026 "two-engine" revenue — aerospace engines plus industrial gas turbines — reached **RMB887m, 49.6% of core revenue**, while related backlog reached about **RMB2.2bn / 70% of total backlog**. It is directly developing / supplying Siemens Energy, Baker Hughes and Ansaldo turbine hot-section products, including F/H/HL-class blade programmes. But group H1 operating cash flow was **negative RMB145m**, gross margin fell to 34.0%, and the shares trade around **80x trailing earnings**. It is strategically interesting but not currently a clean Gate-C setup.
- **Precision Castparts (PCC)** and **Consolidated Precision Products (CPP)** validate the scarcity but are not independent listed opportunities: PCC sits inside Berkshire Hathaway and CPP is being acquired by GE Aerospace.
- **Coatings, superalloys and rotor forgings** are critical enabling layers, but public exposures are either diversified, aerospace-dominated, private or lack enough direct industrial-gas-turbine earnings disclosure to beat the OEM / Howmet opportunity on a common basis.
- **Lifecycle service / hot-gas-path repair** is an excellent recurring profit pool, but the strongest economics remain captured by the turbine OEMs through long-term service agreements rather than by a clearly superior independent listed specialist.

The key implication is:

> **The physical bottleneck has migrated upstream into hot-section manufacturing, but the public-market profit pool has not escaped the obvious turbine / aerospace franchises enough to create a new valuation leader.**

## 2. Why hot-section blades and vanes are unusually difficult

### 2.1 Extreme operating environment

**FACT:** Oerlikon Metco describes first-stage industrial-gas-turbine blades as among the most demanding turbine applications, exposed to gas temperatures up to roughly **1,600–1,700°C**, mechanical stress, creep, corrosion and repeated thermal cycling.

**FACT:** Modern parts therefore combine advanced nickel superalloys with engineered cooling passages, bond coats and thermal-barrier coatings. Manufacturing defects or coating failure can reduce life or cause catastrophic hot-section damage.

**INTERPRETATION:** This is not generic metal fabrication. The manufacturing stack combines:

```text
qualified alloy / master melt
→ ceramic core / internal cooling geometry
→ directional or single-crystal investment casting
→ heat treatment / HIP where required
→ precision machining
→ coating / TBC
→ inspection / NDT / dimensional validation
→ OEM production approval
→ field-history / life validation
```

Each stage can become a throughput or yield constraint.

### 2.2 OEM evidence confirms blades / vanes and castings are limiting capacity

**FACT:** The existing energy work records Siemens Energy management identifying **blades and vanes** as a principal production bottleneck during the current gas-turbine ramp.

**FACT:** By Q3 FY2026 Siemens Energy's gas-turbine backlog was about **69 GW**, and manufacturing lead times were three years or longer. The company is expanding capacity while still describing forgings / castings as an active supply-chain challenge.

**FACT:** Industry specialists interviewed by POWER in 2026 identify **rotor forgings and hot-section blades** as the primary supply-chain bottlenecks because of limited qualified suppliers and highly technical manufacturing.

**INTERPRETATION:** The hot-section constraint is therefore directly coupled to the already validated 4.6/5 turbine-slot bottleneck. Faster OEM assembly does not create more deliverable turbines if qualified blades / vanes cannot ramp at the same rate.

## 3. Howmet Aerospace — strongest merchant capture, weak new asymmetry

### 3.1 Direct exposure is now explicit

**FACT:** Howmet's March 2026 Technology & Markets presentation says the company is the **largest manufacturer of gas-turbine blades in the world**.

**FACT:** The same presentation shows gas-turbine revenue of roughly **$1bn in 2025** and expects the business to exceed **$2bn over the next 3–5 years**, driven by heavy-duty, mid-size and aeroderivative turbine demand.

**FACT:** Howmet's 2025 10-K reported **$944m** of gas-turbine revenue, all within Engine Products, versus $755m in 2024.

**FACT:** Q1 2026 gas-turbine revenue was **$284m**, +39% y/y. Engine Products revenue was $1.253bn with **36.6% adjusted EBITDA margin**.

**FACT:** Q2 2026 consolidated revenue rose 24%, with **gas-turbine revenue +38%**. Group adjusted EBITDA margin reached 32.1%; FY2026 guidance was raised to roughly $10.05bn revenue, $3.23bn adjusted EBITDA and $1.9bn free cash flow at the midpoint.

**INTERPRETATION:** Howmet is the cleanest evidence in this work that turbine scarcity is translating into high-margin supplier economics rather than merely more unit volume.

### 3.2 But valuation already recognizes the moat

**FACT:** On 8 September 2026 Howmet traded around **$239**, with market capitalization about **$95.5bn**, roughly **44x forward earnings** and roughly 58x trailing free cash flow on third-party market data.

**FACT:** Shares fell roughly **8% on 8 September** after GE Aerospace announced an $11.75bn acquisition of Consolidated Precision Products, another major precision-castings supplier.

**INTERPRETATION:** The decline is useful falsification evidence. Customers can respond to casting scarcity through vertical integration / acquisition, and Howmet's premium valuation leaves little room for assuming permanent duopoly-like economics.

**COUNTER-EVIDENCE:** The CPP acquisition is predominantly an aerospace-engine transaction, not direct proof that industrial-gas-turbine OEMs will vertically integrate the same way. Existing qualified Howmet relationships and gas-turbine growth remain intact.

### Howmet conclusion

**Investment Capture:** very strong operationally.  
**Asymmetry:** currently limited by size and valuation.  
**Action:** benchmark in #74 rather than open a full Gate-C issue at ~44x forward earnings unless price / earnings change materially.

## 4. Yingliu — a smaller qualified supplier, but valuation and cash conversion are weak

### 4.1 Direct turbine qualification is unusually strong for a smaller listed company

**FACT:** Yingliu's H1 2026 report says global gas-turbine and aero-engine demand remains supply-constrained and that the company is expanding from castings into **integrated casting + machining + coating finished-part delivery**.

**FACT:** The company says it works with **Siemens Energy, Baker Hughes, Ansaldo, China United Gas Turbine and Hangzhou Steam Turbine** on industrial-gas-turbine programmes.

**FACT:** Yingliu has launched development of Siemens Energy **4000F stages 1–4 blades / vanes** and is working on 8000H and 9000HL flagship models. Five major product variants had entered production-part approval / first-unit production stages by H1 2026.

**FACT:** Yingliu renewed a **five-year strategic cooperation agreement with Baker Hughes**, and says 5002E, LT16 and LT12 blade families are in volume production. It is also expanding casting, precision machining and TBC coating capability.

**FACT:** H1 2026 "two-engine" revenue reached **RMB887m**, 49.56% of core revenue; related backlog was about **RMB2.2bn**, roughly 70% of total company backlog.

**INTERPRETATION:** This is direct evidence of entry into the qualified global hot-section supply chain — much stronger than a generic Chinese 'turbine component' story.

### 4.2 The economics are not yet Howmet-like

**FACT:** H1 2026 group revenue was **RMB1.83bn (+32.2%)**, attributable net profit **RMB221m (+17.5%)**, and gross margin about **34.0%**, down from 36.3% a year earlier.

**FACT:** Operating cash flow remained **negative RMB145m**; investing cash outflow was RMB885m as the company continued expanding capacity.

**FACT:** On 8 September 2026 shares closed around **RMB42.09**, market capitalization roughly **RMB31bn**, with trailing P/E around **80x**.

**INTERPRETATION:** Yingliu provides more total-company sensitivity than Howmet but currently combines rapid capex, negative cash conversion and an aggressive valuation. Its smaller size does not automatically create better expected return.

### Yingliu conclusion

**Status:** research comparator; no Watch promotion from #72.  
**Action:** preserve for #74 second-order synthesis. Re-open company underwriting only if valuation compresses materially or FCF / margin evidence improves enough to change normalized returns.

## 5. Precision Castparts and CPP — strong scarcity validation, unavailable / changing ownership

### Precision Castparts

**FACT:** Precision Castparts is one of the world's major investment-casting / forged-component suppliers but is owned by Berkshire Hathaway.

**INTERPRETATION:** Berkshire provides technically public exposure, but PCC economics are far too diluted inside the conglomerate to function as a focused gas-turbine second-order investment.

### Consolidated Precision Products

**FACT:** On 8 September 2026 GE Aerospace announced an agreement to acquire CPP for **$11.75bn**. CPP is a major precision-castings supplier and is expected by GE to generate about $2bn of 2027 revenue.

**FACT:** GE says airfoil demand could rise more than 30% by 2030; the transaction is explicitly intended to secure critical casting capacity and accelerate engine production.

**INTERPRETATION:** The acquisition is strong revealed-preference evidence that qualified casting capacity has strategic value. It is also evidence **against** assuming independent merchant capture lasts forever: a large OEM can buy the bottleneck rather than continue paying an external scarcity premium.

## 6. Rotor forgings — a real bottleneck, less clean listed capture

Large turbine rotors and shafts require extremely large, high-integrity forgings, heat treatment, machining, ultrasonic inspection and long production cycles.

**FACT:** Current industry evidence identifies rotor forgings alongside hot-section blades as a primary gas-turbine bottleneck.

Reference suppliers include major heavy-forging specialists such as Japan Steel Works and other qualified Asian / European forging houses.

**INTERPRETATION:** The physical scarcity is high, but this work does not yet find a pure listed gas-turbine forging supplier with enough segment transparency and valuation asymmetry to outrank Howmet / the turbine OEMs. Many forging suppliers also serve nuclear, steel, defense or other heavy-industry markets.

**Bottleneck Strength: 4.6 / 5.**  
**Action:** keep as a physical bottleneck in #74; do not open a company issue without clearer gas-turbine earnings sensitivity.

## 7. Thermal-barrier coatings — technically critical, broader supplier ecosystem

**FACT:** Oerlikon Metco supplies thermal-spray materials, equipment and process solutions for industrial-gas-turbine hot sections, including MCrAlY bond coats, YSZ thermal-barrier coatings, clearance-control systems and wear / corrosion protection.

**FACT:** Oerlikon describes proprietary coating design and close OEM engineering collaboration as necessary to meet rising IGT temperature and durability demands.

**INTERPRETATION:** Coating recipes / process qualification can create switching costs, and coating content recurs through component refurbishment. But the profit pool spans OEM captive coating lines, component suppliers such as Yingliu that increasingly integrate TBC, and independent surface-technology companies. Supplier concentration is weaker than for the underlying blade casting.

**Bottleneck Strength: 4.2 / 5.**

---

## 8. Superalloys — essential but not the cleanest bottleneck

Nickel-based superalloys and specialty metals enable hot-section creep / corrosion resistance.

**FACT:** Carpenter Technology markets qualified superalloys and specialty materials for turbine discs, blades / buckets, rotors and hot-gas-path components in power generation.

**INTERPRETATION:** Materials qualification matters, but this layer is more diversified across aerospace, defense, medical and other markets, and raw-material / mill capacity can expand more flexibly than qualified single-crystal casting. The premium economics appear weaker and less turbine-specific than Howmet's finished hot-section component position.

**Bottleneck Strength: 4.1 / 5.**

---

## 9. Service / repair — excellent economics, but OEMs still own the best annuity

**FACT:** Siemens Energy says long-term gas-turbine service agreements now average roughly **17 years**, and estimates lifetime service opportunity around **€400m per GW** of equipment backlog.

**INTERPRETATION:** Hot-gas-path components create a valuable replacement / refurbishment annuity. But the same qualification, software, parts and performance guarantees that make the service attractive also strengthen the OEM's position.

Independent repair and aftermarket suppliers exist, but this screen does not find a listed specialist with enough scale / qualification / direct industrial-gas-turbine exposure to displace GE Vernova / Siemens Energy's service economics in the ranking.

**Strategic-function score: 4.5 / 5.**  
**Merchant-capture conclusion:** strong, but principally OEM captured.

## 10. What #72 changes in the energy thesis

The original energy work ranked **large gas turbines / manufacturing slots + long-duration service** as the strongest merchant-capture lane. This second-order work does not overturn that conclusion.

It refines the chain:

```text
gas-turbine order / reservation
        ↓
OEM manufacturing slot
        ↓
large forgings / rotor
        ↓
hot-section blade & vane casting  ← strongest physical second-order bottleneck
        ↓
machining / coating / inspection
        ↓
turbine delivery
        ↓
17+ year service / hot-gas-path replacement annuity
```

**CONCLUSION:** Hot-section blades / vanes are more structurally scarce than the OEM-level picture alone suggests, but **Howmet's valuation and Yingliu's valuation / cash-conversion risk mean the upstream layer does not currently improve the capital-allocation frontier**.

This contrasts with #71, where OLTCs surfaced Huaming as a plausible new smaller-company lead. For turbines, the current best action is to carry Howmet and Yingliu into **#74 common-basis second-order synthesis**, while continuing to treat GE Vernova / Siemens Energy as the cleaner service / installed-base captures.

No new company is promoted to `Watch` or `High-conviction research candidate`.
