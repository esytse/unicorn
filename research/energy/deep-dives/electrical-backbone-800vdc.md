# Data-Centre Electrical Backbone + 800 VDC — Bottleneck Deep Dive

**Status:** Gate A validated for the integrated data-centre electrical backbone; 800 VDC is a validated architecture transition but not yet a concentrated supplier bottleneck  
**Bottleneck Strength:** **4.3 / 5 integrated electrical backbone**; **4.3 / 5 technical need for 800 VDC, 3.6 / 5 supplier scarcity today**  
**Confidence:** **Medium-High** on electrical-backbone structural demand; **High** that rack-density pressure is driving an architecture transition; **Medium-Low** on which 800 VDC sub-layer captures durable economics  
**Last substantive update:** 2026-09-07  
**Backlog:** issue #45

## Executive conclusion

**CONCLUSION:** The data-centre electrical backbone passes Gate A at **4.3/5**, but the scarcity is different from the transformer or gas-turbine bottlenecks.

The strongest evidence is not that one component is globally unavailable. It is that very high-density AI facilities require a tightly integrated, qualification-heavy chain of:

- medium- and low-voltage switchgear;
- transformers;
- UPS / energy storage;
- busway and distribution;
- protection;
- modular power systems;
- controls and monitoring;
- increasingly, power and cooling co-design.

Orders, backlog and margins at Eaton, Schneider Electric and Vertiv show that this system-level demand is already translating into substantial economics. The function is therefore structural and investable enough to pass Gate A.

However, **800 VDC must be treated separately**.

The physical need is compelling: rack power is moving beyond the practical limits of low-voltage distribution, and Google, Microsoft and NVIDIA are jointly standardising 800 VDC through the Open Compute Project. But that same open-standard effort deliberately creates interoperability and a broad supply chain. More than 80 partners are already developing compatible equipment.

> **The architecture transition is real; supplier scarcity is not yet proven.**

This means the best future 800 VDC opportunity is unlikely to be “own every company exposed to 800 VDC.” It is more likely to sit in a narrower sub-layer where safety, fault clearing, conversion density, efficiency, qualification or thermal performance remain difficult to commoditise.

## 1. Where the electrical backbone sits in the chain

A simplified current path is:

```text
utility / campus MV AC
        ↓
MV switchgear + protection
        ↓
transformers
        ↓
LV switchgear
        ↓
UPS / battery
        ↓
PDU / RPP / busway
        ↓
rack PSU / AC-DC conversion
        ↓
54 VDC / lower-voltage distribution
        ↓
point-of-load conversion
        ↓
GPU / CPU / networking
```

The emerging architecture is increasingly:

```text
utility / campus MV AC
        ↓
MV switchgear + protection
        ↓
[existing facility]
480 VAC → side power rack → 800 VDC row/rack distribution

or

[new facility]
MVAC → MW-scale transformer-rectifier / solid-state power block
        ↓
800 VDC data-hall backbone
        ↓
DC protection + busway/connectors
        ↓
DC/DC conversion close to compute
        ↓
GPU / CPU / networking
```

**INTERPRETATION:** 800 VDC does not remove the need for electrical infrastructure. It **changes where conversion, protection, storage and distribution occur**.

That distinction is critical for supplier underwriting.

## 2. Demand is not just more MW — it is much higher power density

**FACT:** NVIDIA states that the legacy 54 VDC rack-level architecture is becoming a bottleneck as AI rack power moves beyond roughly 200 kW and toward megawatt-class systems.

**FACT:** OCP's August 2026 800 VDC work says higher-voltage DC can move the same power at lower current, reducing conductor / copper burden and helping high-density systems scale.

**FACT:** Eaton's 800 VDC technical material describes AI rack densities that historically averaged roughly 10–50 kW as now exceeding 120 kW, with future deployments approaching 1 MW per rack.

**INTERPRETATION:** The important demand variable is no longer only data-centre MW. It is also:

- MW per building;
- kW per rack;
- current per conductor;
- conversion losses per MW;
- electrical-room footprint;
- protection speed;
- heat generated by power conversion.

This is why the electrical backbone can become a system bottleneck even when upstream electricity is available.

## 7. Why the integrated backbone is difficult to substitute

### Reliability and qualification

A hyperscale AI facility cannot treat switchgear, UPS, protection and busway like commodity consumer electronics. Failure can strand tens or hundreds of millions of dollars of compute.

Key requirements include:

- high fault-interruption capability;
- selective coordination;
- arc-flash / safety design;
- redundant architecture;
- battery / UPS ride-through;
- controls integration;
- maintainability without major downtime;
- regulatory and certification compliance;
- validated thermal performance;
- factory and site acceptance testing.

### Sequencing and system integration

The facility is only energisable when all of the required electrical blocks arrive, pass test and integrate.

**INTERPRETATION:** A shortage or qualification delay in a relatively small component can hold up a much larger AI campus. That gives schedule reliability economic value beyond the bill-of-materials share.

### Installed-base / service

Power equipment creates recurring service, monitoring, replacement, retrofit and upgrade demand.

**INTERPRETATION:** Integrated vendors can capture lifecycle value rather than only initial equipment revenue.

## 8. Why the electrical-backbone bottleneck is weaker than transformers or large turbines

The global supplier base is broader.

Major credible providers include:

- Eaton;
- Schneider Electric;
- ABB;
- Siemens;
- Vertiv;
- GE Vernova in selected grid / electrical layers;
- Delta Electronics;
- Legrand / nVent and others in distribution layers.

Factory capacity for many LV / MV products can also be expanded faster than large power-transformer or advanced gas-turbine manufacturing.

**INTERPRETATION:** The moat is therefore less about a tiny number of qualified machines and more about **portfolio breadth + engineering + qualification + delivery capacity + installed service**.

This supports a lower Gate A score than transformers / transmission / turbines.

## 9. 800 VDC — the architecture transition is now real

**FACT:** On 11 August 2026 Google, Microsoft and NVIDIA announced through OCP that they are standardising **800 VDC** as an open architecture for next-generation AI data centres.

**FACT:** The effort includes common system requirements, safety / regulatory work and an MVAC-to-800 VDC conversion workstream.

**FACT:** OCP says the project has moved from technical feasibility toward industry execution.

**FACT:** More than **80 partners** are developing compatible infrastructure.

**FACT:** NVIDIA says existing facilities can use an 800 VDC side power rack connected to 480 VAC, while future facilities can move toward direct medium-voltage AC to 800 VDC conversion.

**INTERPRETATION:** 800 VDC is no longer merely one vendor's concept. Alignment among three hyperscale/AI infrastructure leaders materially lowers architecture-adoption risk.

---

## 10. Why 800 VDC is needed

Power is `P = V × I`.

For a given MW load, raising voltage reduces current. Lower current can reduce:

- copper cross-section;
- cable / busbar bulk;
- resistive loss;
- heat;
- distribution footprint.

It can also allow fewer conversion stages between the utility connection and compute rack.

**FACT:** NVIDIA describes 800 VDC as reducing conversion/routing volume and distribution loss while simplifying the power path.

**FACT:** OCP explicitly identifies lower conductor / copper burden and higher-density scalability as core reasons for the move.

**INTERPRETATION:** The technical need is strong enough to score about **4.3/5** as an architecture pressure.

But technical necessity is not the same as supplier scarcity.

---

## 11. The open standard is powerful evidence AGAINST a simple supplier-moat thesis

This is the most important falsification evidence in the deep dive.

**FACT:** OCP explicitly says the objective is common interfaces so different vendors' power hardware can work together.

**FACT:** OCP argues that suppliers should be able to "build once" against common requirements rather than maintain multiple incompatible hyperscaler-specific architectures.

**FACT:** NVIDIA lists a broad partner ecosystem spanning ABB, Eaton, GE Vernova, Hitachi Energy, Schneider, Siemens, Vertiv, Delta, Lite-On, Infineon, STMicroelectronics, onsemi, Texas Instruments, Monolithic Power Systems, ROHM, Navitas and others.

**INTERPRETATION:** Standardisation should accelerate adoption, but it also lowers architecture lock-in and makes it dangerous to assume that every 800 VDC vendor earns excess returns.

**CONCLUSION:** 800 VDC supplier scarcity today is only about **3.6/5 — Gate A NOT passed as a standalone supplier layer.**

## 12. Where 800 VDC could still create narrow bottlenecks

The open ecosystem does not mean every component is easy.

### 12.1 MW-scale MVAC → 800 VDC conversion

OCP's long-term architecture uses high-density transformer-rectifier units or solid-state transformer skids.

Potential barriers:

- medium-voltage insulation / safety;
- high efficiency at MW scale;
- fault isolation;
- power quality;
- thermal density;
- modular redundancy;
- certification;
- field service.

**HYPOTHESIS:** If direct MVAC → 800 VDC becomes standard, the power block may become one of the highest-value new equipment categories in the data hall.

### 12.2 DC fault protection / solid-state breakers

DC fault interruption is technically different from AC because current does not naturally cross zero each cycle.

OCP specifically highlights fast solid-state breakers and protection devices.

**HYPOTHESIS:** Very fast, high-voltage DC protection could become a qualification bottleneck if safety / reliability requirements narrow the credible supplier set.

### 12.3 HVDC busway, connectors and distribution interfaces

The connector / busway layer must carry very high power reliably while supporting maintenance and fault isolation.

**HYPOTHESIS:** Standard interfaces may increase volume while reducing proprietary moat. Durable value would require superior current density, safety, thermal or serviceability rather than connector compatibility alone.

### 12.4 High-power DC/DC conversion close to compute

The architecture still needs conversion from 800 VDC toward the voltages used by processors.

Potential differentiation includes:

- power density;
- switching frequency;
- efficiency;
- thermal management;
- magnetics;
- advanced SiC / GaN / silicon devices;
- packaging and control.

**OPEN QUESTION:** Does this become a semiconductor/module bottleneck or remain a competitive multi-vendor market?

### 12.5 DC-native storage / UPS integration

Direct DC coupling can integrate BESS / UPS functions without repeated AC/DC conversion and synchronization.

**HYPOTHESIS:** The opportunity may migrate from standalone traditional UPS toward integrated DC energy buffering, fast transient response and system controls.

## 13. Architecture winners and losers by function

| Function | Architecture resilience | Current interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| MV switchgear / protection | **High** | remains necessary upstream of AC or DC data-hall architectures |
| Transformers / MV power conversion | **High, but implementation changes** | function persists; conventional transformer + rectifier may compete with SST |
| Busway / high-power distribution | **High functionally** | voltage / connector format changes, but high-power distribution remains necessary |
| UPS / energy buffering | **Medium-High functionally** | reliability function persists; traditional AC UPS topology may be redesigned / partly bypassed |
| PDU / RPP layers | **Medium-Low** | some intermediate AC distribution layers can be simplified or removed |
| Rack PSU / repeated AC-DC stages | **Low-Medium** | direct DC architecture explicitly removes / centralises some conversion stages |
| DC protection | **Rising** | new safety / fault-clearing requirements may create a new profit pool |
| DC/DC power modules | **Rising** | increasingly critical near the compute load, but supplier concentration unproven |

**INTERPRETATION:** Underwriting should focus on suppliers that own the **power-delivery function across architectures**, not companies whose moat depends on one legacy conversion stage surviving.

## 16. Investment-capture implications

The electrical backbone now joins the validated energy frontier because it has:

- direct AI sensitivity;
- strong backlog / order evidence;
- high reliability and qualification barriers;
- high system criticality;
- recurring service / installed-base value;
- architecture-resilient functions upstream of the exact rack voltage.

Reference companies for later common-basis underwriting:

- **Eaton** — broad grid-to-chip electrical portfolio, very strong Electrical margins / backlog, active 800 VDC architecture development;
- **Schneider Electric** — integrated Energy Management platform, record backlog and strong data-centre demand, broad diversification;
- **Vertiv** — highest direct critical-data-centre sensitivity and strong operating leverage, but valuation / rerating must be tested;
- **ABB** — strong MV / distribution / UPS / DC and SST capabilities plus NVIDIA/OCP architecture exposure;
- **Siemens / GE Vernova** — relevant in upstream electrical / grid layers, though company economics overlap other already validated bottlenecks;
- **Delta Electronics** and other power-system specialists — relevant comparators for power conversion and rack-level systems.

No company is promoted to `Watch` from this function-level result alone.

## 17. Thesis breakers / monitoring

The electrical-backbone thesis weakens if:

- capacity additions drive order growth and backlog back toward normal without sustained margins;
- open modular standards make integrated systems highly interchangeable;
- hyperscalers internalise more engineering and multi-source components aggressively;
- facility relocation to power-rich regions materially reduces urgency / premium for rapid deployment;
- AI rack-density growth slows materially;
- 800 VDC simplifies the system enough that total electrical equipment content per MW falls faster than MW demand grows;
- Chinese / Asian electrical suppliers expand internationally and compress pricing in currently high-margin markets;
- current backlog contains material double-ordering or projects that fail financing / interconnection screens.

The 800 VDC narrow-bottleneck thesis weakens if:

- transformer-rectifier / SST products standardise rapidly across many vendors;
- DC breakers / protection become broadly commoditised;
- safety / certification proves easier than expected;
- direct-MV-to-DC deployment remains niche while AC sidecar architectures dominate for longer;
- rack-level power semiconductor / DC-DC competition keeps margins structurally low.

---

## 18. Decision

**Integrated data-centre electrical backbone: Gate A PASSED — 4.3 / 5, Medium-High confidence.**

**800 VDC: architecture transition VALIDATED, but broad supplier scarcity NOT validated — ~3.6 / 5 supplier-scarcity score.**

The key synthesis is:

> **800 VDC is more likely to redistribute the profit pool than create one universal 800 VDC winner.**

Architecture-resilient upstream functions — MV equipment, protection, transformation / conversion, integrated power blocks and system engineering — look more durable than legacy stages that the DC transition is explicitly designed to simplify.

The energy programme now has five validated structural functions:

1. transformers / critical substation equipment — 4.7;
2. physical transmission deliverability — 4.7;
3. large gas-turbine equipment / slots — 4.6;
4. MV/HV switchgear — 4.4;
5. integrated data-centre electrical backbone — 4.3.

The next validation should test **behind-the-meter / microgrid + storage integration**, followed by **cooling / heat rejection**. After those, the programme should stop broad bottleneck discovery and run common-basis Investment Capture across the strongest supplier sets.
