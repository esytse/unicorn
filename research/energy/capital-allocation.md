## Executive conclusion

The energy research reaches the same uncomfortable conclusion as the memory work:

> **The bottlenecks are real, the companies are capturing them, but most obvious beneficiaries are already priced for a large portion of the buildout.**

On the current normalized scenario set, **no company passes strict Gate C at the reference prices**.

The closest setups are:

1. **Siemens Energy** — best current valuation / capture balance; base return ~**11.2%** and ~12% hurdle near **€144** versus ~€148 reference.
2. **nVent** — smaller, high-sensitivity platform; base ~**10.5%**, 12% hurdle near **$147** versus ~$156.
3. **Vertiv** — exceptional business / AI sensitivity but more valuation already embedded; base ~**8.5%**, 12% zone near **$245** versus ~$281.
4. **Hainan Jinpan** — asymmetric specialist screen; base ~**8.4%**, 12% zone near **CNY59** versus CNY67.91, with much lower evidence quality.
5. **Centrus** — event-driven nuclear optionality; probability-weighted screen ~**7.3%**, 12% base-value zone near **$147** versus ~$174; not comparable to conventional EPS compounding.
6. **Modine** — high growth / direct thermal scarcity but FCF and customer-concentration risk; base ~**7.7%**, 12% zone near **$165** versus ~$195.
7. **HD Hyundai Electric** — strong transformer economics but current rerating leaves base ~**6.2%**; 12% zone ~**KRW606k** versus KRW763k.
8. **Eaton** — highest-quality diversified electrical platform, but base ~**6.0%**; 12% zone ~**$325** versus ~$411.
9. **GE Vernova** — strongest Gate-B capture but most valuation already embedded under a normalized-EPS approach; base ~**4.6%**, 12% zone near **$702** versus ~$942.

The key capital-allocation distinction is:

> **Best business ≠ best expected return.**

GE Vernova / Vertiv / Eaton are among the strongest operating franchises in the programme. Siemens Energy and nVent currently offer the better balance of capture and valuation. The specialist lane can create higher upside, but only by accepting materially worse downside / evidence quality.

No company is promoted to `High-conviction research candidate`.

## 1. Gate C definition

A company passes energy Gate C only if all of the following are true:

- Gate A structural bottleneck is validated;
- Gate B company capture is validated;
- base-case annualized return is approximately **12% or better** over the modeled horizon;
- bear downside is tolerable relative to upside and thesis confidence;
- the scenario does not require current peak margins / backlog forever;
- balance sheet / dilution / M&A / working-capital risk is manageable;
- the terminal valuation is normalized rather than simply preserving today's multiple;
- thesis breakers and monitoring triggers are explicit.

A high-quality company can remain `Watch` without passing Gate C.

## 3. Modeling method

For conventional operating companies:

1. infer a normalized forward EPS baseline from reference price / current forward P/E;
2. grow that EPS through 2030 using explicit bear / base / bull assumptions;
3. apply a **normalized 2030 terminal P/E**, generally well below current peak-growth multiples;
4. calculate annualized price return from the reference price to end-2030;
5. ignore dividends in the headline return, making the screen slightly conservative for dividend payers;
6. calculate the reference price that would produce 10%, 12% and 15% annualized return under the base scenario;
7. run a reverse test: what 2027–2030 EPS CAGR is required at today's price to achieve those return hurdles using the base terminal multiple?

**Centrus is modeled separately** with probability-weighted terminal equity values because near-term EPS is a poor representation of an enrichment-capacity buildout whose economics depend on government awards, financing, customer prepayments and plant execution.

The scenario set is intentionally more conservative than current sell-side growth forecasts for the highest-growth names. For example, current consensus 3-year EPS growth is roughly 29.6% for GE Vernova, 40.1% for Vertiv, 30.7% for nVent and 45.6% for Modine; the base cases below use materially lower sustainable growth for normalization.

## 8. Company capital-allocation conclusions

### 8.1 Siemens Energy — closest to Gate C

**Decision:** **Watch — preferred current energy capital-allocation monitor.**

The rerating is substantial, but the combination of Gas Services scarcity, Grid Technologies growth, customer advances, improving Gamesa economics and a ~25x forward multiple gives more valuation room than GE Vernova / Vertiv.

The base case gets to ~11.2%, just short of Gate C. A price around **€144** under the current assumptions clears ~12%.

**Breaker:** Gamesa again consumes cash or Gas/Grid margins fail to persist as backlog converts.

### 8.2 nVent — specialist with the best near-term asymmetry / quality balance

**Decision:** **Watch — second capital-allocation monitor.**

nVent's smaller size, 47% organic Q2 growth, electrical + cooling content and improving cash generation create real sensitivity. The risk is that standardized liquid-cooling components and capacity additions reduce future margins faster than our base case.

Base return ~10.5%; ~12% zone **~$147**.

### 8.3 Vertiv — exceptional franchise, demanding starting valuation

**Decision:** **Watch — quality leader, wait for valuation or earnings catch-up.**

The platform is arguably the best direct listed expression of AI power + cooling infrastructure. The problem is not thesis quality; it is a ~$108bn valuation and ~36x forward earnings after a major rerating.

Base return ~8.5%; ~12% zone **~$245**.

### 8.4 Hainan Jinpan — interesting specialist, lower evidence quality

**Decision:** **Investigating — asymmetric monitor, not capital-ready.**

A CNY31bn-scale company with >4x data-centre order growth can move much faster than Eaton or GE Vernova if modular power / overseas qualification works. But competitive durability, global qualification, cash conversion and governance need materially more evidence.

Base return ~8.4%; ~12% zone **~CNY59**.

### 8.5 Centrus — optionality, not conventional compounding

**Decision:** **Investigating — event-driven nuclear option.**

Centrus can generate extraordinary upside if U.S. enrichment capacity scales with customer / government funding, but the bear case is severe. 30%+ share-count growth over the last year reinforces dilution risk.

The base event value of ~$240 gives only ~7.8% annualized from ~$174; a ~12% zone is roughly **$147**. The bull case is large enough to keep the name active, not enough to call it capital-ready.

### 8.6 Modine — direct scarcity evidence, poor current FCF conversion

**Decision:** **Investigating — wait for margin / FCF proof or lower price.**

The $4bn LTA and $165m customer prepayment are exceptional evidence. But current P/FCF above 100x and Q1 margin pressure show that the growth is expensive to deliver.

Base return ~7.7%; ~12% zone **~$165**.

### 8.7 HD Hyundai Electric — great transformer economics, valuation reset still needed

**Decision:** **Watch — specialist benchmark, wait.**

Backlog and profitability are excellent, but the stock has already rerated massively. The base scenario normalizes transformer margins / multiple as global capacity expands.

Base return ~6.2%; ~12% zone **~KRW606k**.

### 8.8 Eaton — highest-quality diversified platform, insufficient current return

**Decision:** **Watch — quality benchmark, not current Gate C.**

Eaton's architecture resilience is exceptional and Boyd broadens the platform. But at ~$411 and ~27x forward earnings, a 25x 2030 multiple plus 12% EPS growth produces only ~6% annualized.

~12% zone **~$325**.

### 8.9 GE Vernova — strongest capture, weakest normalized current-price asymmetry

**Decision:** **Watch — operational leader, valuation-bound.**

Current free cash flow is helped materially by customer advances and backlog timing, so the model deliberately avoids capitalizing the current ~20x P/FCF at face value. A forward-EPS normalization plus 22% base growth and 30x terminal P/E produces only ~4.6% annualized.

~12% zone **~$702**.

The thesis can outperform this model if Gas Power service earnings and Electrification margins compound much faster than expected. That upside should be proven in earnings, not assumed because the bottleneck is strong.

## 10. Monitoring triggers

### Positive triggers

- Siemens Energy trades around / below the mid-€140s without deterioration in Gas/Grid backlog or Gamesa cash generation;
- nVent approaches the high-$140s while organic growth and FCF remain strong;
- Vertiv approaches the mid-$240s, or earnings rise enough to move the 12% zone upward;
- Modine proves Data Centers margin / FCF recovery as new capacity ramps;
- HD Hyundai Electric maintains backlog / margins while valuation compresses;
- Jinpan provides stronger overseas hyperscaler qualification / cash-conversion evidence;
- Centrus converts contingent enrichment commitments into funded capacity with limited shareholder dilution;
- GE Vernova proves service + Electrification normalized EPS significantly above the current forward baseline.

### Negative triggers

- large-load project cancellations / financing failures materially reduce credible AI power demand;
- transformer / turbine / cooling capacity expansion collapses lead times and pricing faster than modeled;
- customer advances reverse and expose weaker normalized cash generation;
- open standards commoditize thermal / electrical content faster than integrated platforms can capture service economics;
- acquisitions dilute ROIC at Eaton / Vertiv;
- specialist growth requires persistent working-capital / equity funding;
- nuclear policy / advanced-reactor schedules reduce the probability of Centrus commercial expansion;
- China / overseas qualification or governance issues weaken Jinpan's accessible economics.
