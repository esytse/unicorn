# Centrus Energy — AI Energy Investment Capture

**Status:** Investigating  
**Investment Capture Score:** **4.0 / 5**  
**Confidence:** Medium-Low  
**Primary lanes:** Western LEU enrichment; HALEU enrichment / deconversion optionality

## Current conclusion

**INTERPRETATION:** Centrus is the cleanest listed small-company exposure in this programme to the validated Western enrichment / HALEU bottleneck. It therefore offers unusually high theoretical asymmetry, but the economics remain much more dependent on government policy, financing, construction milestones and advanced-reactor execution than the near-term turbine / electrical candidates.

## Evidence

**FACT:** Q2 2026 revenue was **$176.1m**.

**FACT:** total backlog was **$4.5bn** at June 30, 2026 and extends to 2040.

**FACT:** Centrus reported about **$3.0bn of contingent LEU / HALEU sales commitments**, with $2.4bn under definitive agreements supporting potential new production capacity.

**FACT:** the company signed a **$900m DOE HALEU enrichment award**.

**FACT:** Centrus and X-energy signed a commercial LEU / HALEU supply agreement that can include customer prepayments.

## Why capture can persist

- Western enrichment supplier concentration is exceptionally high;
- domestic U.S. qualified centrifuge capacity is strategically valuable;
- multi-year fuel contracts can create recurring / contracted economics;
- company size gives much greater earnings sensitivity than diversified nuclear suppliers;
- policy support for domestic enrichment can accelerate capacity buildout.

## Counter-evidence / risks

- customer / government concentration is severe;
- contingent backlog depends on operational milestones and project financing;
- DOE stated it did not currently intend to exercise further options under one HALEU operating contract and the proposed FY2027 budget did not include further operation funding under that contract;
- large-scale expansion requires substantial capital and execution;
- HALEU demand depends heavily on advanced-reactor projects reaching commercial deployment.

## Thesis breakers

- commercial LEU/HALEU commitments fail to become funded plant capacity;
- DOE / federal policy support weakens materially;
- advanced-reactor schedules slip into the late 2030s;
- enrichment expansion costs overwhelm future margins / cash generation;
- foreign or allied supply expands faster than expected and reduces strategic scarcity.

## Gate-C question

What probability-weighted value should be assigned to commercial enrichment expansion without treating contingent backlog or HALEU deployment as certain?
