# Hainan Jinpan Smart Technology — AI Energy Investment Capture

**Status:** Investigating  
**Investment Capture Score:** **4.0 / 5**  
**Confidence:** Medium-Low  
**Primary lanes:** transformers; prefabricated data-centre power modules; emerging SST optionality  
**Last evidence refresh:** 2026-09-08  
**Backlog:** issue #64

## Current conclusion

**INTERPRETATION:** Jinpan remains one of the more interesting smaller listed challengers surfaced by the E2E work because AI/data-centre orders can materially change company economics and its product set spans transformers, MV/LV distribution and prefabricated power modules rather than a single component.

The September evidence refresh strengthens the **demand / deployment** side of the thesis but does **not** resolve the two key Gate-C gaps:

1. primary public disclosures still do not cleanly identify the current North-American hyperscaler customers behind the order surge; and
2. H1 2026 cash conversion was weak as inventory, receivables and capacity needs absorbed the accounting profit.

The evidence remains strong enough to investigate, not strong enough to promote to `Watch`.

## Demand and backlog evidence

**FACT:** H1 2026 total new sales orders were **RMB7.493bn, +69.31% y/y**. Data-centre new orders were **RMB3.869bn, +336.25%**, data-centre revenue was **RMB1.265bn, +122.57%**, and period-end data-centre backlog was **RMB5.092bn, +179.07%**.

**FACT:** H1 overseas new orders were **RMB4.190bn, +270.69%**, overseas revenue was **RMB1.481bn, +68.30%**, and overseas backlog was **RMB6.239bn, +122.65%**, equal to **58.32%** of total backlog. Management says the overseas order structure is concentrated mainly in AIDC data centres, but the filing does not disclose the exact overlap between overseas and data-centre backlog.

**FACT:** management has described the installed data-centre base inconsistently. An April 2026 investor record said the company had completed **about 400 data-centre projects**; a September 2026 investor response described **more than 100 projects**. The existence of a substantial installed base is credible, but the exact project count should therefore be treated as low-confidence until reconciled.

**FACT:** older company investor materials provide named domestic data-centre references including **Baidu, Huawei, Alibaba, China Mobile, China Telecom and China Unicom**. This supports real data-centre qualification history, but it does not verify the identity or scale of current North-American hyperscaler orders.

## Overseas / hyperscaler qualification

**FACT:** the company states that it serves leading global data-centre and energy/power customers and has long-term relationships with international enterprises. It also states that AIDC is the main category within the current overseas order mix.

**OPEN QUESTION:** public primary disclosures reviewed in this refresh do **not** name Meta, Microsoft, Amazon or Google as the customers behind the current H1 2026 overseas data-centre order surge. Secondary market commentary makes more specific claims, but those claims are not strong enough to enter the canonical thesis as fact without recoverable primary/customer evidence.

**INTERPRETATION:** qualification risk has fallen relative to a company with no overseas operating history, but named hyperscaler / repeated multi-site qualification remains unverified. This is still the largest evidence-quality gap in the company thesis.

## Cash conversion and balance-sheet evidence

**FACT:** H1 2026 attributable net profit was about **RMB303m**, but operating cash flow was **-RMB23.8m**, versus **+RMB234.8m** a year earlier.

**FACT:** cash paid for property, plant, equipment and intangibles was about **RMB129.1m**. A simple operating-cash-flow-minus-capex proxy was therefore roughly **-RMB153m** for H1 2026.

**FACT:** the cash-flow reconciliation shows an approximately **RMB651.6m inventory build**, a **RMB45.1m increase in operating receivables**, and a **RMB125.9m increase in operating payables**.

**FACT:** consolidated inventory rose from **RMB2.590bn at December 2025 to RMB3.239bn at June 2026**; accounts receivable rose from **RMB2.368bn to RMB2.564bn**; contract liabilities rose from **RMB522m to RMB712m**. Short-term borrowings also increased from about **RMB492m to RMB938m**.

**INTERPRETATION:** the cash-flow deterioration is consistent with a business scaling inventory and delivery capacity against a rapidly larger backlog, not proof that the economics are poor. But it directly falsifies any shortcut from order growth to immediate free-cash-flow capture. The next important evidence is whether the working-capital build reverses as H2 2026 / 2027 deliveries convert.

## Capacity and architecture evidence

**FACT:** Jinpan opened an additional Virginia production base in H1 2026 and has described a wider overseas production footprint including the United States, Mexico and Malaysia. The company says it evaluates capacity before accepting orders.

**FACT:** the Tongxiang data-centre power-module / VPI-transformer digital-factory infrastructure was completed in H1 and production-line installation / commissioning was under way.

**FACT:** Jinpan's first **13.8kV SST prototype adapted for the North-American grid** completed internal testing and was sent overseas for testing in H1 2026. Management also reports cooperation with a domestic cloud provider around future high-voltage-DC compute-centre demand.

**INTERPRETATION:** SST remains **optional upside**, not base-case earnings. Overseas testing is meaningful technical progress but is not evidence of commercial volume qualification.

## Why capture could persist

- smaller company size creates high earnings sensitivity to AIDC order conversion;
- prefabricated integrated power modules benefit from speed-to-deployment;
- transformer / switchgear content is architecture-resilient;
- overseas revenue and backlog growth demonstrate that the opportunity is not purely domestic;
- contract liabilities and backlog provide some forward visibility;
- SST development provides optionality if 800-VDC / higher-efficiency architectures mature.

## Counter-evidence / risks

- named North-American hyperscaler qualification is not publicly verified in the primary evidence reviewed;
- H1 operating cash flow was negative despite positive earnings, with a large inventory build;
- receivables and short-term borrowing increased as the company scaled;
- Chinese electrical-equipment competition can be intense and price-led;
- current order growth can attract rapid capacity expansion and weaken scarcity economics;
- the exact data-centre project count is inconsistent across company communications;
- SST commercial adoption remains early and should not be capitalized as base-case earnings;
- investor access, governance, disclosure quality, tariffs and geopolitical constraints require a discount in Gate C.

## Thesis breakers

- overseas backlog fails to convert to cash / acceptable margin;
- H2 2026 / 2027 working-capital absorption remains structurally high after deliveries scale;
- data-centre share proves project-driven rather than repeatable;
- transformer/module margins erode as competitors add capacity;
- named global qualification remains unverified while the thesis increasingly depends on North-American AIDC;
- SST remains a prototype without commercial wins.

## Gate-C state

At the **8 September 2026 close of about CNY65.74**, the stock remained above the existing normalized **~CNY59 12% return zone** from issue #54. The lower price improves the valuation setup versus the 7 September reference but does not by itself clear Gate C.

**OPEN QUESTION:** can backlog conversion produce materially better normalized earnings and free cash flow while independently verifiable global qualification improves enough to justify a higher-confidence base case?
