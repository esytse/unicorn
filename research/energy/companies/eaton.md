# Eaton — AI Energy Investment Capture

**Status:** Watch  
**Investment Capture Score:** **4.3 / 5**  
**Confidence:** Medium-High  
**Primary lanes:** transformers / switchgear / protection; data-centre electrical backbone; thermal via Boyd

## Current conclusion

**INTERPRETATION:** Eaton is the strongest architecture-resilient electrical-platform candidate in the current screen. It captures multiple validated functions and remains relevant whether incremental power comes from grid expansion, gas, nuclear or behind-the-meter generation.

## Evidence

**FACT:** Q2 2026 sales were **$8.5bn**, with 14% organic growth.

**FACT:** rolling orders increased **41%** in Electrical Americas and **33%** in Electrical Global.

**FACT:** Electrical-sector backlog was substantially higher year over year; Electrical Americas margin reached **27.5%**.

**FACT:** Boyd Thermal contributed to Electrical Global in its first full quarter after acquisition, expanding Eaton from grid-to-rack electrical infrastructure into chip-side / liquid thermal management.

## Why capture can persist

- unavoidable transformation, protection and distribution functions;
- qualified installed base and engineering depth;
- data-centre content can rise with rack density;
- thermal adjacency improves source-to-chip architecture breadth;
- customer/end-market diversification is stronger than at specialist suppliers.

## Counter-evidence / risks

- broad group scale reduces asymmetry;
- $9.5bn Boyd acquisition increases integration / ROIC risk;
- transformer/switchgear capacity additions can normalize lead times;
- electrical margins may include favorable backlog mix and price/cost timing.

## Thesis breakers

- order/backlog growth slows before capacity expansion is absorbed;
- Boyd fails to earn adequate returns on acquisition capital;
- normalized Electrical margins fall materially below current levels;
- integrated platform breadth does not translate to cross-selling / service economics.

## Gate-C question

Does the normalized post-Boyd earnings profile justify current valuation after assuming transformer/switchgear scarcity gradually eases through 2028–2030?
