# Vicor — VPD scarce-complement underwrite

**Ticker:** NASDAQ: VICR  
**Discovery origin:** thesis-first scan #249  
**State:** **POTENTIAL / WATCH — structural capture validated; current equity setup not yet a Unicorn underwrite**  
**Boundary:** research conclusion only; no brokerage action.

## Thesis chain

**World change → scarce complement → bottleneck migration → company capture**

AI accelerators require increasingly dense low-voltage/high-current power delivery. Vicor's VPD architecture addresses the “last inch” power-delivery problem and is protected by foundational patents. The important 2026 state change is not merely product relevance: Vicor is monetising the architecture through both modules and licensing while expanding manufacturing capacity.

## Evidence chain

### Architecture / IP

**FACT — primary:** on 17 Sep 2026 Vicor granted another non-exclusive VPD licence to a leading AI OEM. Licensees may source covered modules from other suppliers, while Vicor modules can reduce the royalty burden. Vicor positions licensing as a way for OEMs/hyperscalers to obtain freedom to operate, multi-source flexibility and scalability.

**FACT — primary historical context:** Vicor said in Oct 2025 that licensing/settlements following ITC action were expected to contribute nearly **$300m of revenue through 2026**.

**INTERPRETATION:** VPD can generate economics even when Vicor is not the sole module manufacturer. That creates a royalty layer on top of product economics, but non-exclusive licensing also limits assumptions of manufacturing monopoly.

### Capacity

**FACT — primary:** on 11 Sep Vicor said its 320k sq ft Fab-1 utilization was approaching capacity. It is acquiring a 334k sq ft Merrimack building plus 54 acres in Hooksett for Fab-2/Fab-3; combined future footprint is nearly 1m sq ft. Initial Fab-2 deployment has about a one-year lead time.

**INTERPRETATION:** capacity expansion corroborates demand/architecture adoption, but future fabs imply capital requirements and execution risk. Do not treat square footage as earnings capacity without module-output/capex evidence.

### Financial base

**FACT — primary:** Vicor reported 2025 net revenue of **$407.7m**, versus $359.1m in 2024.

**FACT — current secondary market/financial reference:** Q2 2026 revenue was about **$143.35m** and net income about **$49.8m**; market references on 24 Sep show roughly **46.1m shares** and approximately **$12.8–13.1bn equity value** around $276/share.

**FACT — primary company disclosure referenced in the discovery record:** following the latest VPD licence, Vicor raised Q3 sequential revenue-growth guidance from nearly 10% to **>20%**, explicitly due to licence royalties.

## Shareholder-capture test

### What passes

1. **Bottleneck migration:** passes. VPD addresses a physical constraint created by accelerator power density.
2. **Economic capture:** passes structurally. Vicor can monetize both proprietary modules and patent licences.
3. **Demand/state change:** passes. New OEM licences, raised royalty-linked guidance and near-capacity Fab-1 are mutually reinforcing signals.
4. **Operating leverage:** potentially strong because licensing revenue can carry much higher incremental economics than manufactured modules.

### What does not yet pass

1. **Starting valuation:** at ~US$12.8–13.1bn equity value, the discovery has already been heavily repriced.
2. **Royalty durability:** licence economics, duration, per-system/processor rates and future customer mix are not sufficiently disclosed to build a defensible recurring royalty bridge.
3. **Capacity economics:** Fab-2/3 capex, module output, utilization and return on invested capital are not yet quantified.
4. **Customer concentration / architecture substitution:** four major licences validate adoption but also concentrate the thesis in a small set of AI platforms; alternative architectures and commoditized licensed modules can reduce product capture.

## Unicorn Potential sanity check

Using ~US$13bn current equity as the controlled reference:

- **3x:** ~US$39bn equity.
- **5x:** ~US$65bn.
- **10x:** ~US$130bn.

At a mature 30x earnings multiple, those values require roughly:
- 3x → **US$1.3bn** annual net income;
- 5x → **US$2.2bn**;
- 10x → **US$4.3bn**.

Even at a premium 40x multiple:
- 3x → ~US$975m;
- 5x → ~US$1.63bn;
- 10x → ~US$3.25bn.

Q2 2026 net income of ~US$49.8m annualizes mechanically to only ~US$199m, and that quarter may contain unusually high licensing economics. The current market value therefore already capitalizes a large portion of the VPD opportunity.

**Conclusion:** Vicor is an excellent validation of the *original thesis* but currently a weaker validation of the *small-company multibagger setup*. The company was discovered correctly; the equity may have been discovered late.

**Unicorn state:** **POTENTIAL / WATCH — 3x requires an order-of-magnitude earnings step from the current quarterly base; 5x/10x are unsupported today. Do not promote to UNDERWRITE until royalty/module economics can bridge the starting valuation.**

## Comparison with known power/cooling inventory

Vicor differs from Siemens Energy, nVent, Vertiv, Eaton, GE Vernova and Modine because the scarce complement sits much closer to the accelerator package and includes an IP/royalty layer. That makes the business model potentially more convex.

However, the current ~US$13bn starting equity value removes much of the “small overlooked supplier” asymmetry that the original Unicorn thesis seeks. Vicor should therefore be used as:
1. a **proof that point-of-load power architecture is a real bottleneck migration**;
2. a **map for finding smaller suppliers/IP holders upstream/downstream of VPD**;
3. a monitored company whose valuation may later reopen the equity setup.

## What would change the conclusion

**Upgrade evidence**
- disclosed royalty economics that support a durable high-margin recurring revenue base;
- additional major VPD licences with identifiable processor/platform volume;
- Fab-2/3 economics showing unusually high incremental returns;
- material price compression without thesis deterioration.

**Downgrade evidence**
- alternative power architectures bypass VPD patents;
- licensing rates compress materially as adoption broadens;
- major licensees shift away from Vicor modules without sufficient royalty replacement;
- Fab expansion requires disproportionate capital or suffers utilization delays.

## Next thesis-first implication

Do **not** spend the next discovery cycle trying to prove Vicor deserves today's valuation. Follow the bottleneck outward:

**high-current point-of-load delivery → VPD/vertical architecture → packaging/passives/magnetic components/thermal interfaces → smaller listed suppliers whose economics may inflect before valuation recognizes it.**

That preserves the original thesis and uses Vicor as a discovery signpost rather than forcing it into the portfolio.
