## Why this company surfaced

Furukawa Electric is a listed diversified industrial company with direct, recoverable evidence that its SuperPower business supplies high-temperature superconducting (HTS) wire into fusion development. Unlike a pure fusion developer, the company also serves infrastructure, automotive and electronics markets, making it a candidate for the Unicorn test: **does a scarce fusion-enabling capability have attractive economics even if fusion commercialization is delayed?**

## FACT

- In January 2023 Tokamak Energy announced that Furukawa Electric and its US subsidiary SuperPower were expected to supply more than several hundred kilometres of specialist HTS wire for ST80-HTS. Source: https://www.furukawaelectric.com/en/release/2023/kenkai_20230112.html
- Furukawa entered an investment agreement with Tokamak Energy in December 2023. In 2026 the companies expanded collaboration to explore UK HTS-tape R&D, prototyping, manufacturing processes and future production capability. Source: https://www.furukawa.co.uk/furukawa-electric-and-tokamak-energy-advance-collaboration-on-uk-hts-tape-capability/
- Furukawa states that HTS applications extend beyond fusion into power transmission and medical devices. Same source.
- Furukawa invested in Japanese fusion company Starlight Engine in July 2026, adding a second direct fusion relationship. Source: https://www.furukawaelectric.com/en/release/2026/metal_20260715.html

## INTERPRETATION

The evidence is sufficient to establish **real fusion supply-chain exposure**, but not sufficient to establish financial materiality, pricing power or a Gate-E portfolio case. The most important next question is not whether fusion needs HTS; it is whether Furukawa/SuperPower owns enough qualified capacity/IP/customer position for HTS growth to materially change Furukawa's earnings and valuation inside the remaining portfolio window.

## HYPOTHESIS

If qualified REBCO/HTS tape remains capacity-constrained while STEP, Tokamak Energy and other magnetic-confinement programmes move from prototypes toward larger magnet systems, SuperPower could become a scarce picks-and-shovels supplier. Non-fusion applications could provide downside support and manufacturing learning.

## OPEN QUESTIONS / Gate-E work

1. Quantify SuperPower/HTS revenue, capacity, capex and margins inside Furukawa.
2. Map qualified competitors and realistic market shares: SuperPower versus other REBCO suppliers.
3. Determine whether STEP/Tokamak demand represents funded procurement, expected demand or only strategic planning.
4. Identify 18-month catalysts that can change total-company earnings expectations.
5. Test whether capacity additions eliminate scarcity before fusion volumes scale.
6. Build valuation/upside/downside cases at current market price and compare marginal portfolio contribution against the active top-10.

## Current decision

**Research candidate only — no portfolio promotion.** Direct evidence is strong enough for a bounded Gate-E underwrite, but current financial capture evidence is insufficient.

**Confidence:** Medium on fusion relevance; Low on near-term financial materiality pending follow-up.
