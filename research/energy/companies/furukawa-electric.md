# Furukawa Electric / SuperPower — Gate-E HTS Underwrite

**Status:** Research candidate only — no portfolio promotion  
**Theme:** Energy / fusion enabling infrastructure  
**Parent research:** `research/energy/fusion-enabling-infrastructure.md`  
**Gate-E issue:** #158  
**Research date:** 2026-09-19

## Decision summary

Furukawa Electric has unusually direct listed-company exposure to second-generation REBCO high-temperature superconducting (HTS) tape through SuperPower. The exposure is real: Furukawa/SuperPower is a supplier to Tokamak Energy, the group invested in Tokamak Energy and Starlight Engine, and the companies are studying future UK tape capability. The problem for Gate E is financial capture, not technical relevance.

At the 18 September 2026 close of **¥3,901**, Furukawa's equity value was about **¥2.74tn** and the shares had rerated roughly fourfold from year-end 2025. Group FY2027 guidance had already been raised to approximately **¥1.53tn revenue / ¥123bn operating profit / ¥105bn attributable profit**, driven primarily by strong data-centre-related demand rather than disclosed HTS economics. SuperPower/HTS revenue, margin, production capacity and backlog are not separately disclosed in the investor evidence reviewed.

**Gate-E conclusion: WATCH / RESEARCH CANDIDATE — not ACTION and not a Top-10 displacement.** HTS is strategically scarce enough to monitor, but there is not yet evidence that SuperPower can move Furukawa's total-company earnings sufficiently by March 2028 to justify paying the current group valuation for fusion optionality.

**Confidence:** High on direct HTS/fusion relevance; Medium on competitive/scarcity evidence; Low-Medium on near-window financial materiality because segment economics are undisclosed.

## FACT — operating and financial baseline

- Furukawa Electric owns U.S.-based **SuperPower**, which commercialises REBCO second-generation HTS tape.
- SuperPower's published product specification supports 200–500 m piece lengths, with some specifications above 900 m, across multiple tape widths and stabilisation options. This is direct manufacturing/product evidence, not a capacity disclosure.
- In January 2023 Tokamak Energy announced that Furukawa Electric and SuperPower were expected to supply **more than several hundred kilometres** of HTS tape for ST80-HTS.
- Furukawa entered an investment agreement with Tokamak Energy in December 2023. In June/July 2026 the companies expanded collaboration to research, prototyping, manufacturing-process evaluation and feasibility work for potential future UK HTS-tape capability.
- The 2026 Furukawa/Tokamak release states that STEP is expected ultimately to use about **30,000 km** of HTS tape, but the UK programme targets a prototype plant around 2040. This is long-duration demand context, not a Furukawa order or March-2028 revenue forecast.
- Furukawa invested in Starlight Engine in July 2026. Starlight's financing round raised more than **¥6bn** externally; Furukawa did not disclose the size of its own investment.
- Furukawa's FY2027 Q1 (quarter ended June 2026) produced record first-quarter results: approximately **¥365.2bn sales, ¥25.5bn operating profit and ¥22.2bn attributable profit**. Management raised FY2027 guidance to roughly **¥1.53tn sales, ¥123bn operating profit and ¥105bn attributable profit**.
- The latest retrieved market reference is **¥3,901 on 18 September 2026**, implying about **¥2.74tn market capitalisation** and roughly **23x forward earnings** on the market-data reference.

## FACT — competition and supply response

HTS tape is not a single-supplier bottleneck.

- **Fujikura** announced ¥6.0bn of HTS capacity investment in FY2024 targeting roughly 3–4x prior capacity by FY2027, followed in February 2026 by another **¥5.6bn** investment intended to approximately double capacity again. Fujikura says this would place it among the world's largest HTS production capacities.
- Other REBCO suppliers exist globally, and the fusion screening work identifies Faraday Factory Japan and Shanghai Superconductor among scale competitors. Exact like-for-like qualified capacity and customer share are not sufficiently disclosed to assign a robust market-share number.
- Qualification matters: fusion magnets require demanding in-field performance, long lengths, mechanical consistency and application-specific conductor design. SuperPower's long history and Tokamak relationship are evidence of capability, but do not establish monopoly economics.

## INTERPRETATION — bottleneck and scarcity

**Bottleneck direction: structurally important, but supply is expanding.**

REBCO tape is a credible enabling bottleneck for compact high-field fusion magnets. Hundreds-of-kilometres procurement for ST80-HTS and STEP's eventual tens-of-thousands-of-kilometres requirement show that successful fusion programmes can create large physical demand. However, competitors are investing aggressively before commercial fusion volumes arrive. The investable question is therefore not whether HTS matters; it is whether qualified demand grows faster than qualified capacity and whether SuperPower retains enough pricing/margin advantage for the economics to become material to a ¥2.74tn parent.

Fujikura's announced capacity expansion is important counter-evidence against assuming persistent scarcity rents. Capacity additions can be rational because future demand may be very large, but they also reduce the probability that tape scarcity alone produces extraordinary margins.

## FACT vs OPEN QUESTION — funded demand

### Direct / credible evidence

- Tokamak Energy's 2023 supply announcement is concrete evidence of a several-hundred-kilometre supply relationship with Furukawa/SuperPower.
- Furukawa/Tokamak's 2026 UK collaboration is a real programme for R&D, prototyping, process evaluation and feasibility assessment.
- Furukawa's Starlight investment creates a second direct fusion relationship.

### Not yet established

- No reviewed source discloses current SuperPower revenue, operating profit, backlog, annual kilometre capacity, utilisation, realised tape pricing or Furukawa's share of Tokamak procurement.
- The potential UK manufacturing capability remains conditional on feasibility work; it is not yet a disclosed production-capacity commitment.
- STEP's estimated 30,000 km requirement is programme demand expected over a much longer horizon, not a current order for SuperPower.
- A Furukawa UK page appears to attribute an April 2026 £70m STEP magnet-system contract to Furukawa, while Tokamak Energy's own release identifies **Tokamak Energy** as the Magnet Systems Partner. The latter is treated as the more credible attribution; the discrepancy is preserved rather than converted into Furukawa backlog.

## Gate-E financial-materiality test

At ~¥2.74tn market value, even a meaningful HTS business must become large quickly to alter the March-2028 equity outcome.

A simple sensitivity illustrates the hurdle rather than forecasts it:

- **¥10bn incremental HTS operating profit** would equal only about **8%** of current FY2027 group operating-profit guidance.
- **¥25bn incremental HTS operating profit** would equal about **20%** of guidance.
- **¥50bn incremental HTS operating profit** would equal about **41%** of guidance.

There is currently no disclosed revenue/capacity/backlog evidence supporting any of those incremental-profit cases by March 2028. This makes HTS optionality financially diluted by Furukawa's much larger optical/data-centre, infrastructure, automotive and electronics businesses.

## Valuation / downside

**FACT:** the 18 September close was ¥3,901; market cap ~¥2.74tn; the market-data reference shows ~30.6x trailing and ~23.4x forward P/E. The stock has rerated sharply versus 2025 as group earnings accelerated.

**INTERPRETATION:** current valuation can be justified or undermined by the broader data-centre/optical earnings cycle independently of fusion. That makes Furukawa a poor pure-play expression of the HTS thesis at present: an investor can be right on fusion tape but still earn a poor return if optical earnings/multiples normalise.

**Downside / permanent-loss vectors:**
- data-centre optical earnings normalise faster than current valuation assumes;
- REBCO capacity expands ahead of qualified demand and compresses tape economics;
- fusion timelines slip beyond the active portfolio window;
- SuperPower loses qualification/share to Fujikura or other suppliers;
- UK manufacturing requires substantial capital before contracted demand is visible;
- strategic investments remain technically useful but immaterial to group earnings.

## Catalysts inside the March-2028 window

A reassessment becomes worthwhile if one of the following occurs:

1. Furukawa discloses **SuperPower/HTS revenue, backlog, capacity, utilisation, capex or margins** at a level that can be bridged to group earnings.
2. A named fusion customer places a **large funded tape order** with disclosed volume/value/timing that is incremental to the already-known ST80 relationship.
3. UK feasibility work converts into a **funded manufacturing commitment** with customer/programme support and a credible return profile.
4. A material qualification/share win shows SuperPower retaining scarcity despite competitor capacity expansion.
5. Furukawa's group valuation resets materially while the HTS and core earnings thesis remains intact; no price threshold is invented here because the current evidence does not support one.

## Thesis breakers / falsification

- qualified REBCO capacity grows materially faster than funded demand and tape pricing/margins commoditise;
- SuperPower loses major fusion qualifications or cannot scale long-length production competitively;
- fusion procurement slips far enough that meaningful HTS earnings remain outside March 2028;
- HTS economics remain undisclosed/immaterial while group valuation continues to capitalise aggressive growth elsewhere;
- a substitute magnet/conductor architecture materially reduces REBCO intensity.

## Portfolio comparison

Furukawa adds a differentiated fusion/HTS return engine, but **not yet a superior deployable one**. Its current equity value is already large, the stock has rerated sharply, and the fusion exposure is financially diluted. Existing Gate-E candidates with explicit near-term company-level catalysts and clearer earnings sensitivity remain higher priority.

**Current portfolio role:** Watch / research candidate.  
**Position size:** 0%; no entry rule is supported.  
**Top-10 implication:** no displacement.  
**Next action:** event-driven monitoring only; do not consume routine heavy-research capacity until financial-materiality evidence improves.
