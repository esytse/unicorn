# AI Energy — Supplier Investment Capture

**Status:** Gate B common-basis underwriting complete  
**Confidence:** Medium-High on relative quality/capture; Medium on smaller-specialist durability; valuation is explicitly deferred to Gate C  
**Last substantive update:** 2026-09-08  
**Backlog:** issue #53

## Executive conclusion

The energy programme has now moved from **where scarcity exists** to **which listed suppliers are actually converting that scarcity into earnings, margins, backlog, service economics or strategically valuable capacity**.

The strongest current Gate-B group is:

1. **GE Vernova — 4.4/5 — Watch**
2. **Vertiv — 4.4/5 — Watch**
3. **Eaton — 4.3/5 — Watch**
4. **Siemens Energy — 4.3/5 — Watch**
5. **HD Hyundai Electric — 4.2/5 — Watch**
6. **nVent — 4.2/5 — Watch / smaller specialist-platform challenger**
7. **Mitsubishi Heavy Industries — 4.1/5 — Watch / benchmark**
8. **Quanta Services — 4.1/5 — Watch / transmission-execution benchmark**
9. **Cameco — 4.1/5 — Watch / strategic nuclear comparator**
10. **Centrus Energy — 4.0/5 — Investigating / asymmetric nuclear optionality**
11. **Hainan Jinpan Smart Technology — 4.0/5 — Investigating / smaller transformer-power-module challenger**
12. **Modine — 3.9/5 — Investigating / high-AI-sensitivity thermal specialist**

This ranking is **not a valuation ranking**. It deliberately does not answer whether any of these shares are attractive at today's prices.

The central Gate-B conclusion is:

> **GE Vernova / Siemens Energy own the clearest scarcity-to-service model; Vertiv / Eaton own the broadest architecture-resilient AI-infrastructure platforms; HD Hyundai Electric / nVent / Modine / Jinpan offer greater earnings sensitivity and potential asymmetry but with more concentration, capacity-expansion or competitive risk.**

Nuclear is a separate strategic lane. **Centrus has the cleanest direct exposure to the highest-scoring HALEU / Western enrichment bottleneck, but also the highest policy, funding, customer and execution risk. Cameco is much more diversified and durable but correspondingly less asymmetric.**

## 1. Method

Score 1–5 on the ten dimensions required by issue #53. Higher is better.

| Dimension | High score means |
|---|---|
| Revenue sensitivity | validated bottleneck can materially change total-company revenue / earnings |
| Pricing / margin capture | scarcity is visible in price, margin, prepayments, backlog quality or cash flow |
| Competitive durability | qualification, installed base, technology or execution makes substitution difficult |
| Share / content runway | credible room to gain share or increase content per MW / project |
| Service / recurring | aftermarket, service, controls, fuel contracts or lifecycle revenue add durability |
| Capital intensity | company can capture growth without disproportionate capital burden |
| Customer concentration | lower dependence on one buyer / project / government counterparty |
| Architecture resilience | benefits survive grid/BTM/generation/cooling/800-VDC migration |
| Asymmetry / size | bottleneck can materially change total-company economics rather than be diluted |
| Execution resilience | evidence supports ability to deliver through supply-chain and scaling constraints |

The score is an arithmetic average used for triage only. Contradictory evidence can override the number.

## 3. GE Vernova — clearest scarcity + service capture

**FACT:** Q2 2026 orders were $24.2bn, backlog increased $13bn sequentially, total backlog reached $176bn, and Gas Power equipment backlog / slot reservations rose to **116 GW**. GE Vernova expects at least 125 GW by year-end 2026.

**FACT:** Q2 adjusted EBITDA margin reached **11.3%**, up 340 bps organically, while free cash flow in the quarter / first half was unusually strong.

**INTERPRETATION:** This is the cleanest evidence in the programme that a validated physical bottleneck is translating into customer reservations, backlog, margin expansion and cash generation.

**INTERPRETATION:** GE Vernova is also more architecture-resilient than a pure gas-turbine thesis because Electrification participates in transformers / grid equipment while Gas Power creates decades of service attachment.

**COUNTERPOINT:** AI is only one driver of the cycle. Grid investment, replacement generation, industrial demand and Middle East power projects are important. Wind remains a lower-quality segment and group size reduces asymmetry.

**Decision:** **Watch, ICS 4.4/5.** Gate C must normalize turbine-cycle margins and test whether current valuation already capitalizes scarcity + service economics.

## 4. Vertiv — strongest direct AI-infrastructure sensitivity

**FACT:** Q2 2026 sales were **$3.274bn, +24% y/y**, adjusted operating profit rose 51%, and adjusted operating margin reached **22.6%, +410 bps**.

**FACT:** Vertiv raised full-year 2026 guidance to about **$14bn sales** at the midpoint and ~31% organic growth.

**FACT:** Free cash flow was $925m in Q2 and the company ended the quarter in a net-cash position despite continued capacity investment.

**INTERPRETATION:** Vertiv is the most direct listed expression of the integrated **electrical + thermal + controls + service** AI infrastructure thesis in the current shortlist.

**INTERPRETATION:** The UtilityInnovation Group, Strategic Thermal Labs and ThermoKey acquisitions extend the platform across BTM power orchestration, chip-side cooling and heat rejection, increasing architecture resilience.

**COUNTERPOINT:** Vertiv is already an obvious AI beneficiary; rapid capacity expansion and a strong rerating can move risk from business quality to valuation. Component standardisation also limits moat in some sub-layers.

**Decision:** **Watch, ICS 4.4/5.** One of the leading Gate-C candidates, but valuation discipline will be decisive.

## 5. Eaton — best diversified architecture-resilient electrical platform

**FACT:** Q2 2026 sales reached **$8.5bn**, with organic growth of 14%.

**FACT:** rolling orders rose **41%** in Electrical Americas and **33%** in Electrical Global; Electrical-sector backlog increased strongly year over year.

**FACT:** Electrical Americas operating margin was **27.5%**; Electrical Global benefited from Boyd Thermal in its first full quarter after acquisition.

**INTERPRETATION:** Eaton captures several validated functions at once: transformers / switchgear / protection, electrical distribution, power quality, and now chip-side / liquid thermal management through Boyd.

**INTERPRETATION:** Its architecture resilience is exceptionally high: grid power, onsite gas, nuclear, BTM and 800 VDC all still require high-integrity transformation, protection and distribution somewhere in the chain.

**COUNTERPOINT:** Eaton is large, diversified and already widely recognized as an AI-power beneficiary. The $9.5bn Boyd acquisition increases thermal exposure but also raises integration / return-on-capital risk.

**Decision:** **Watch, ICS 4.3/5.** High-quality benchmark and Gate-C candidate; asymmetry may be weaker than specialists.

## 6. Siemens Energy — turbine + grid dual exposure with improving group quality

**FACT:** Q2 FY2026 orders reached **€17.7bn**, backlog **€154bn**, driven by record Gas Services orders and sharply higher Grid Technologies orders.

**FACT:** Q2 Gas Services orders were **€8.869bn**, up 32.4% comparable; Grid Technologies orders were €6.996bn, up 41.5% comparable.

**FACT:** Q3 2026 reporting and contemporaneous company / Reuters commentary show U.S. data-centre and Middle East demand driving another record quarter; data-centre operators and Middle East customers represented about half of quarterly gas-turbine orders.

**INTERPRETATION:** Siemens Energy offers nearly the same structural combination as GE Vernova: scarce advanced turbines + service and a very strong grid-equipment business.

**COUNTERPOINT:** Siemens Gamesa has historically diluted group quality and execution confidence, although 2026 performance improved materially. The group is large and rerated.

**Decision:** **Watch, ICS 4.3/5.** Strong Gate-C comparator to GE Vernova; company-level returns will depend on how much Gamesa risk remains embedded and how much gas/grid upside is already priced.

## 7. HD Hyundai Electric — smaller transformer specialist with direct scarcity evidence

**FACT:** Q2 2026 revenue rose **26%** to KRW1.14tn and operating profit rose **37.3%** to KRW287bn.

**FACT:** Reuters reports backlog of approximately **$8.5bn** at end-June 2026, up 23% in six months, with more than three years of major-equipment production capacity substantially committed and discussions extending to 2030 deliveries.

**FACT:** the company explicitly links strong North American / European demand to hyperscaler and data-centre investment.

**INTERPRETATION:** HD Hyundai Electric has materially greater earnings sensitivity to the transformer / power-equipment bottleneck than diversified Western electrical groups, while retaining exposure to architecture-resilient grid equipment.

**COUNTERPOINT:** rapid expansion, regional/customer concentration, foreign-listing access and a prior share-price rerating matter. Transformer scarcity may normalize as global capacity additions arrive.

**Decision:** **Watch, ICS 4.2/5.** One of the highest-priority specialist candidates for Gate C.

## 8. nVent — high-growth electrical + cooling challenger

**FACT:** Q2 2026 reported sales were **$1.5bn, +53%**, with organic growth of **47%**; adjusted EPS increased 69% and free cash flow more than doubled.

**FACT:** management explicitly cited significant data-centre growth and announced another liquid-cooling manufacturing expansion.

**INTERPRETATION:** nVent is interesting because it straddles **electrical connection / protection** and **liquid cooling**, two functions that remain relevant as AI rack density rises.

**COUNTERPOINT:** the liquid-cooling ecosystem is increasingly standardized and multi-vendor. Rapid sales growth includes portfolio transformation / new products and must be normalized before attributing the full step-up to durable AI scarcity.

**Decision:** **Watch, ICS 4.2/5.** Smaller and potentially more asymmetric than Eaton / Vertiv, but requires stricter source-level decomposition in Gate C.

## 9. Mitsubishi Heavy Industries — high-quality turbine benchmark, diluted by conglomerate breadth

**FACT:** MHI Q1 FY2026 order intake was **¥2.022tn**, revenue ¥1.194tn and business profit ¥159.6bn; group guidance was raised materially.

**FACT:** external reporting based on company guidance describes gas-turbine demand running far above historical levels and MHI working to shorten lead times / expand output.

**INTERPRETATION:** MHI is a credible third advanced-class gas-turbine supplier, preventing an overly simplistic GE/Siemens duopoly thesis and supporting the structural scarcity conclusion.

**COUNTERPOINT:** defense, aerospace and other industrial businesses dilute AI-power earnings sensitivity; the company is not the cleanest expression of turbine scarcity.

**Decision:** **Watch / benchmark, ICS 4.1/5.** Include in Gate C mainly to benchmark turbine economics and valuation against GE Vernova / Siemens Energy.

## 10. Quanta Services — excellent execution capture, weaker pure scarcity economics

**FACT:** Q2 2026 revenue was **$9.56bn**, adjusted EBITDA $1.1bn and free cash flow $0.9bn.

**FACT:** total backlog reached **$53.4bn**, including Electric backlog of roughly $43.8bn.

**INTERPRETATION:** Quanta captures physical transmission / substation / grid buildout through scarce engineering, construction and execution capacity rather than proprietary equipment.

**COUNTERPOINT:** EPC economics are structurally less concentrated and can be more labor-, project- and working-capital-sensitive than turbine or transformer manufacturing. AI is one contributor to a much broader utility infrastructure cycle.

**Decision:** **Watch / benchmark, ICS 4.1/5.** Important Gate-C comparator but not yet the preferred merchant-scarcity expression.

## 11. Nuclear lane — Cameco versus Centrus

### Cameco

**FACT:** Cameco spans uranium and fuel services and has Westinghouse exposure. Q2 2026 results show substantial group EBITDA but also quarter-to-quarter volatility from Westinghouse project timing.

**INTERPRETATION:** Cameco is the more diversified and durable way to participate in the Western nuclear fuel / services renaissance, including conversion exposure and downstream reactor-service optionality.

**COUNTERPOINT:** uranium mining remains a weaker bottleneck than enrichment / HALEU; AI data centres are not the main near-term demand driver; company size dilutes the pure bottleneck sensitivity.

**Decision:** **Watch / nuclear comparator, ICS 4.1/5.**

### Centrus Energy

**FACT:** Q2 2026 revenue was **$176.1m**; the company reported a $4.5bn total backlog and roughly $3.0bn of contingent LEU/HALEU enrichment sales commitments.

**FACT:** Centrus signed a **$900m DOE HALEU enrichment award** and a commercial LEU/HALEU agreement with X-energy that can include prepayments.

**INTERPRETATION:** Centrus is the cleanest listed exposure in this screen to the validated HALEU / Western enrichment bottleneck and therefore has the greatest small-company asymmetry.

**COUNTERPOINT:** the same concentration creates severe risk: DOE funding / option decisions, construction milestones, customer prepayments, advanced-reactor timelines and large capacity requirements can dominate economics. The current DOE FY2027 proposal did not include further operation funding under one HALEU contract, illustrating policy risk directly.

**Decision:** **Investigating, ICS 4.0/5.** High optionality, not yet equal in evidence quality to the near-term power-equipment Watch names.

## 12. Emerging specialist lane — Jinpan and Modine

### Hainan Jinpan Smart Technology

**FACT:** Reuters reports H1 2026 new data-centre orders more than quadrupled year over year and related backlog nearly tripled.

**FACT:** Jinpan's H1 filing describes prefabricated data-centre power cabins / modules spanning MV switchgear, transformers, LV distribution, bus systems and intelligent monitoring, plus active solid-state-transformer development.

**INTERPRETATION:** Jinpan is one of the more interesting smaller listed candidates because it combines direct AI electrical sensitivity, modular source-to-load integration and emerging SST optionality.

**COUNTERPOINT:** supplier concentration / qualification outside China is less established than for HD Hyundai / Eaton; overseas expansion, competitive pricing and access/governance require deeper underwriting.

**Decision:** **Investigating, ICS 4.0/5.** Keep as a specialist challenger rather than promoting on growth alone.

### Modine

**FACT:** Modine Q1 FY2027 Data Centers sales were **$348.6m, +90% y/y**.

**FACT:** a strategic customer committed to more than **$4bn** of 2027–2029 Airedale cooling capacity and paid **$165m upfront** to support expansion.

**FACT:** Data Centers gross margin fell sharply in Q1 FY2027 because of expansion costs, production inefficiencies, material / supply-chain pressure and warranty effects.

**INTERPRETATION:** Modine has the strongest direct customer-revealed evidence of cooling-capacity scarcity in the programme and unusually high AI sensitivity for a smaller company.

**COUNTERPOINT:** customer concentration and capacity-execution risk are material, and the same expansion needed to capture demand can suppress margins / FCF. Cooling interfaces are less concentrated than turbines or transformers.

**Decision:** **Investigating, ICS 3.9/5.** High-priority Gate-C asymmetric screen despite a lower quality-adjusted ICS.

## 13. What changed versus the bottleneck ranking

The company work changes the practical priority in three ways.

### 13.1 Platform companies move up

At the function level, turbines ranked first. At company level, **Vertiv and Eaton move alongside GE Vernova** because they span multiple bottlenecks and remain relevant when architecture migrates.

### 13.2 Smaller specialists become explicit

**HD Hyundai Electric, nVent, Modine and Jinpan** deserve separate Gate-C treatment because AI / data-centre infrastructure can move total-company earnings more than at broad conglomerates.

### 13.3 Nuclear bifurcates sharply

**Cameco** offers diversification / durability; **Centrus** offers much greater bottleneck purity / asymmetry with much greater policy and execution risk. They should not be scored as interchangeable nuclear exposure.

## 15. Thesis breakers to carry into Gate C

- AI campus announcements collapse after stronger project screening / financing requirements;
- turbine / transformer / cooling capacity expansion normalizes lead times faster than expected;
- customer prepayments prove temporary and do not translate to supplier FCF;
- margins are driven by peak backlog mix rather than durable pricing / service economics;
- open standards commoditize cooling or 800 VDC content faster than integrated platforms can differentiate;
- hyperscalers vertically integrate enough design / procurement to pressure supplier economics;
- nuclear fuel expansions arrive before advanced-reactor demand, creating overcapacity;
- geopolitical / subsidy support changes materially;
- large acquisitions destroy ROIC despite strategic logic;
- working capital / capex absorbs the apparent earnings growth at specialist suppliers.
