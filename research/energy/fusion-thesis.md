# Fusion Enabling Infrastructure — Working Thesis

> **If fusion becomes commercially important, value is likely to accrue first to scarce enabling capabilities that are already useful elsewhere; therefore investability should be judged on current non-fusion economics plus evidenced fusion qualification, not on confidence in a developer's power-plant date.**

## What would strengthen the thesis

- repeat orders across multiple fusion architectures;
- qualification bottlenecks that survive capacity expansion;
- disclosed pricing/margin/cash evidence;
- non-fusion markets that absorb capacity and protect downside;
- catalysts inside the active 18-month portfolio window.

## What would weaken it

- rapid conductor/component commoditisation;
- vertically integrated fusion developers internalising key supply;
- one-off prototype engineering that never becomes repeat product revenue;
- fusion exposure remaining immaterial to public suppliers;
- timelines slipping without adjacent-market economics to support investment.

**Current confidence:** Medium as a research thesis; insufficient evidence for a portfolio thesis.
