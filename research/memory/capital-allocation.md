# AI Memory — Capital Allocation Scenarios

**Status:** Initial capital-allocation comparison  
**Confidence:** Medium  
**Last substantive update:** 2026-09-07  
**Backlog:** issue #27  

## Purpose

Move beyond Bottleneck Strength and Investment Capture scores and ask the question that matters for capital allocation:

> **At the current price, which validated AI-memory beneficiary offers enough risk-adjusted upside after allowing for a normal semiconductor-capex cycle?**

This is a research framework, not an investment recommendation. Scenario outputs are deliberately approximate; they are intended to expose what must go right, not manufacture false precision.

---

## 1. Current conclusion

**CONCLUSION:** **No candidate passes Gate C at the 4 September 2026 reference prices.** The underlying businesses remain attractive, but the market has already capitalised a meaningful portion of the AI-memory opportunity.

The capital-allocation order is now:

1. **Onto Innovation — preferred valuation/quality watch.** Best current balance of bottleneck quality, earnings visibility, balance sheet and valuation. It is closest to Gate C, but the base case still produces only about a 10% annualised return under the assumptions below.
2. **SUSS — asymmetric watch.** Smaller market value and strong 2027 backlog create substantial upside if execution and HBM qualification hold, but the bear case is much worse than Onto because of customer/process concentration and margin volatility.
3. **Camtek — quality company, insufficient current margin of safety.** Order visibility is excellent, but the price already assumes substantial post-2027 earnings compounding.
4. **FormFactor — strongest direct HBM test evidence, weakest valuation asymmetry at the current price.** The company may execute well and still generate only modest returns if the terminal multiple normalises.

**ASMPT was screened as an optional comparator but is not promoted into the primary allocation set.** At HK$167.70 on 7 September 2026 and about 28x published forward earnings, its broader-company dilution means it does not currently offer a clearer valuation case than the four names above.

## 3. Normalisation discipline

The strongest current results are occurring during an unusually strong AI/HBM investment cycle. The model therefore does **not** simply extrapolate current quarterly growth.

The common framework is:

- use 2027 consensus EPS as a visible-cycle anchor;
- allow the **bear case** to miss that anchor by 20% for Onto/FormFactor/Camtek and 25% for SUSS, then assume low or negative 2027–2030 EPS growth;
- use consensus as the **base anchor**, followed by materially slower growth than the current AI ramp;
- allow a modest upside to the consensus anchor in the **bull case**, followed by sustained share/content gains;
- use terminal P/E multiples below or around current forward valuations rather than assuming today's enthusiasm persists;
- calculate returns from the September 2026 price to end-2030, approximately **4.25 years**;
- ignore dividends in the return table, making the comparison slightly conservative where distributions occur.

These are **HYPOTHESES**, not forecasts.

## 6. What the current price already requires

A useful reverse test is to ask what **2027–2030 EPS CAGR** is required to earn a **12% annual return** if the company finishes 2030 at the base-case terminal multiple.

| Company | Base terminal P/E | Required 2027–30 EPS CAGR for 12% return |
|---|---:|---:|
| **Onto Innovation** | 23x | **~17%** |
| **SUSS** | 20x | **~20%** |
| **Camtek** | 23x | **~28%** |
| **FormFactor** | 22x | **~30%** |

This is the clearest valuation result from #27.

- Onto does **not** require an implausible operating outcome, but it still needs strong multi-year compounding after an already-large 2026/2027 step-up.
- SUSS's required growth is achievable in a successful AI/AP cycle, but execution risk is much higher and the bear case is severe.
- Camtek and FormFactor require unusually strong earnings compounding for several years **after** the current AI-driven earnings acceleration. That is a poor margin-of-safety setup even if the businesses remain excellent.

## 8. Company-level capital view

### Onto Innovation — closest to Gate C

**FACT:** Q2 revenue was $343.1m, +35.3% y/y. Non-GAAP gross margin was 57.0%, Q3 non-GAAP operating-margin guidance was 31.5–32.5%, and backlog exceeded $1bn.

**FACT:** Onto ended Q2 with $1.88bn of cash/investments and $1.47bn of 2031 notes. That leaves positive net liquidity before other liabilities, although the convertible structure introduces potential dilution/settlement considerations.

**INTERPRETATION:** Onto has the best combination of process-control bottleneck quality, visible HBM/AP demand, strong margins and a starting valuation below FormFactor/Camtek on the 2027 EPS anchor.

**Why Gate C still fails:** the base case is only ~10% annualised from the current price. A 12% hurdle needs roughly 17% EPS CAGR from 2027–2030, so there is not yet a large margin of safety.

**Monitoring:** HBM VPA conversion; backlog extending into 2028; Dragonfly/3Di share; non-GAAP gross margin; advanced-packaging revenue; price around **$250 or below** absent thesis deterioration.

### SUSS — best small-cap asymmetry

**FACT:** H1 order intake reached €410m and backlog €473.7m, including roughly €220m scheduled for 2027. H1 net cash was €63.6m, equity ratio 59%, and free cash flow +€16.4m.

**FACT:** Company-published analyst consensus for 2027 is €591.7m revenue, 15.5% EBIT margin and €3.41 EPS; 2028 consensus is €4.62 EPS. These estimates sit well above the 2026 trough and therefore already assume a substantial recovery.

**INTERPRETATION:** SUSS offers much more percentage upside than the large US names if HBM/AP orders translate into durable margin expansion. It also has the widest credible downside because temporary-bonding share, customer concentration and product mix can change quickly.

**Why Gate C still fails:** the base-case return is ~9% and the bear case can destroy more than half the equity value. A better margin of safety appears around **€64** for a 12% base-case return and nearer **€57** for a 15% hurdle, unless evidence improves enough to raise normalized earnings.

**Monitoring:** temporary-bonding orders at the contested Korean customer; 2027 backlog conversion; EBIT margin >13–15%; hybrid-bond production qualification; price around **€64 or below**.

### Camtek — strong orders, expensive duration

**FACT:** Q2 revenue was $133.2m; non-GAAP gross margin 51.4% and operating margin 27%. Management guided Q3 revenue to $158–160m and expects further growth into 2027.

**FACT:** Cash, deposits and marketable securities were $815.8m at Q2. The balance sheet is not the main investment risk.

**INTERPRETATION:** Camtek remains a high-quality way to capture inspection/metrology complexity, but roughly 30x 2027 consensus EPS means investors need sustained post-2027 growth and premium terminal valuation.

**Why Gate C fails:** the base scenario produces only ~4% annualised return. A 12% hurdle at a 23x terminal multiple requires roughly 28% EPS CAGR from 2027–2030.

**Monitoring:** 2027 conversion of the 2026 order surge; Hawk share at HBM/OSAT customers; gross margin around 50%+; price nearer **$105** unless estimates move substantially higher.

### FormFactor — superb HBM evidence, most demanding earnings hurdle

**FACT:** Q2 revenue reached $258.2m, +31.9% y/y, with HBM among the key growth drivers. Q3 guidance is $270m ± $10m revenue, 54% ± 1.5% non-GAAP gross margin and $0.86 ± $0.09 non-GAAP EPS.

**FACT:** Q2 cash and marketable securities were about $346m versus roughly $12m total debt, giving the strongest simple net-cash position in the primary set.

**INTERPRETATION:** FormFactor remains one of the highest-quality technical franchises in the research, but the share-price rerating has moved faster than the conservative normalized earnings case.

**Why Gate C fails:** the base scenario produces only ~1–2% annualised return. To earn 12% at a 22x terminal P/E, 2027–2030 EPS would need to grow roughly 30% annually.

**Monitoring:** HBM probe-card revenue/share at all three memory makers; gross-margin durability; HBM profit disclosure; price nearer **$68** unless normalized EPS estimates rise materially.

## 10. Evidence against the capital-allocation conclusion

The current ranking can be wrong in both directions.

### Evidence that could make the analysis too conservative

- HBM/AP equipment content could compound for longer than a normal semiconductor-capex cycle.
- hybrid bonding, larger packages and higher test/inspection insertion could raise supplier content faster than unit volumes.
- 2027 consensus estimates may understate incremental margins if capacity scales efficiently.
- Onto, Camtek or FormFactor could take material share from larger competitors.
- SUSS's record backlog could mark a structural step-up rather than a cyclical peak.

### Evidence that could make it too optimistic

- 2026–2027 orders may include multi-year capex pull-forward.
- HBM makers and OSATs can dual-source and use qualification leverage to reduce supplier economics.
- terminal multiples can fall below the bear assumptions in a semiconductor recession.
- current consensus EPS already assumes unusually strong AI infrastructure spending.
- technology transitions can redistribute value among suppliers faster than the model assumes.

### Cross-theme AI-infrastructure financing evidence — Oracle, 12 September 2026

**FACT:** Oracle's disclosures show both exceptionally strong AI-cloud demand and a capital-intensive build-out. FY2026 free cash flow was **negative $23.7bn**; Oracle raised **$43bn of debt and $5bn of equity** in FY2026 and expected about **$40bn** more debt/equity financing in FY2027. It also said **$75bn** of hardware in large AI contracts was customer-prepaid or customer-supplied. Q1 FY2027 then reported **$664bn RPO**, more than **$30bn of additional AI-cloud contracts**, **850MW** of additional datacentre capacity delivered and completion of a **$20bn** at-the-market common-equity sale. [SRC-AIINF-001][SRC-AIINF-002]

**FACT (secondary):** The Financial Times reported permit/regulatory hurdles in Oracle's OpenAI build-out and investor concern about margin compression associated with the AI datacentre investment. [SRC-AIINF-003]

**INTERPRETATION:** Strong compute demand does not guarantee that every planned AI deployment is financed and built on the expected timetable. This strengthens the existing downside case that 2026–2027 HBM / advanced-packaging equipment orders may include pull-forward and that a later infrastructure-capex digestion could slow HBM, packaging, probe-card, bonding, inspection and test demand.

**WATCH:** Treat hyperscaler / neocloud financing stress, project or permitting deferrals, GPU deployment slippage and reduced HBM procurement as cross-theme signals that could raise the probability of the memory-cycle bear case.

**CONCLUSION:** Supporting downside evidence only. It does **not** change the current Memory ranking, valuation zones or Gate C decisions.
