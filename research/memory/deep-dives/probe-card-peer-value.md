# Probe-card peer value screen — JEM, Micronics, FormFactor and Technoprobe

**Research stream:** AI memory — HBM test / probe cards  
**Status:** Active peer-value screen  
**Confidence:** Medium  
**Last substantive update:** 2026-09-07

## Purpose

Compare the main listed probe-card beneficiaries of HBM/AI demand and separate **business quality** from **valuation attractiveness**.

## Current conclusion

**CONCLUSION:** FormFactor remains the best diversified technology benchmark, but the peer comparison surfaces a potentially more asymmetric valuation candidate: **Japan Electronic Materials (JEM, TSE: 6855)**. **Micronics Japan (MJC, TSE: 6871)** is the strongest quality/value comparator, while **Technoprobe (BIT: TPRO)** currently shows the most extreme operating leverage and should be treated as the operating benchmark rather than a value pick.

**INTERPRETATION:** The relevant opportunity is not simply “HBM grows.” The investment question is which supplier captures more test content, share and margin per unit of HBM growth **without already discounting that outcome in the equity valuation**.

## Japan Electronic Materials — why it matters

**FACT:** JEM says FY2027 Q1 revenue and profit rose sharply because sales of memory probe cards expanded materially and domestic factories maintained high utilisation.

**FACT:** On 7 August 2026 JEM raised its first-half outlook and incorporated that increase into full-year guidance. FY2027 guidance is now **¥36.4bn revenue, ¥9.45bn operating profit and ¥6.7bn net income**. Management expects probe-card demand to remain firm in the second half, while explicitly preserving caution around demand and FX.

**FACT:** JEM says prior capacity investments are now translating into higher production as memory-probe-card demand expands.

**INTERPRETATION:** JEM offers unusually high operating sensitivity to the memory probe-card cycle. The small absolute earnings base means incremental HBM demand can produce disproportionate earnings growth if utilisation and mix remain strong.

**HYPOTHESIS:** If JEM’s current market valuation remains materially below the premium multiples attached to FormFactor and Technoprobe while its earnings base continues to scale, JEM may offer the best **valuation-adjusted** exposure to the same HBM-test bottleneck.

**Open questions:**

1. What share of JEM revenue/gross profit is specifically HBM rather than broader DRAM?
2. Which HBM manufacturers and insertions use JEM probe cards?
3. How much of FY2027 margin is sustainable after utilisation normalises?
4. Has recent equity issuance materially diluted per-share upside?
5. What normalized-cycle multiple is appropriate after allowing for memory cyclicality?

## Micronics Japan — why it matters

**FACT:** MJC describes itself as having the world’s leading share in memory probe cards and says HBM demand drove strong DRAM probe-card sales during 2025.

**FACT:** The new Aomori factory building increased production capacity, contributing to record probe-card sales.

**FACT:** The company’s FV26 plan targets **¥80bn revenue, ¥20bn operating profit, 25% operating margin and 23% ROE** in FY2026, based on an assumed 20% probe-card market CAGR from 2024–2026. Planned 2023–2026 investment totals ¥48bn capex plus ¥22bn R&D.

**INTERPRETATION:** MJC appears to combine stronger incumbent memory positioning than FormFactor with a cleaner memory-probe-card exposure than Technoprobe. That makes it a particularly useful benchmark for assessing whether JEM’s lower valuation reflects true competitive weakness or simply lower investor attention.

**Open questions:**

1. Is MJC’s claimed memory leadership specifically maintained at HBM4/high-speed insertions?
2. How concentrated is revenue by SK hynix / Samsung / Micron?
3. How much incremental capacity remains after the Aomori expansion?
4. Does the current valuation already discount FV26 margin targets?

## Technoprobe — operating benchmark

**FACT:** Technoprobe H1 2026 revenue was **€464.1m**, +42.4% y/y; gross margin was **53.8%**; EBITDA was **€206.2m**, +93.8%; EBITDA margin reached **44.4%**; and net profit was **€143.1m**.

**FACT:** Management raised 2026 guidance to **€1.05–1.10bn revenue** and **46–48% EBITDA margin**, and increased planned capex through Q1 2027 to **€350m**.

**FACT:** Technoprobe says the revenue acceleration was driven primarily by AI-related volumes and that it plans to expand capacity and technology development further.

**FACT:** The group historically specialised in non-memory probe cards and expanded into memory probe cards during 2025.

**INTERPRETATION:** Technoprobe demonstrates just how attractive advanced probe-card economics can become when volume, mix and utilisation align. It is therefore the best peer for testing whether FORM/JEM/MJC margin assumptions are conservative or aggressive.

**Risk:** Its extraordinary 2026 profitability can also mark a cyclical/mix peak. Valuation work should normalize rather than capitalize 46–48% EBITDA margins indefinitely.

## FormFactor — role in the peer set

FormFactor remains the **technology-diversification benchmark** because it combines HBM/DRAM probe cards with foundry/logic, systems, silicon photonics and co-packaged-optics exposure. The existing company file records direct HBM shipments to all three major manufacturers and strong operating leverage.

**INTERPRETATION:** The peer screen weakens the case for paying a large valuation premium for FormFactor solely because HBM test is attractive. JEM and MJC provide direct memory-probe-card alternatives, while Technoprobe demonstrates equally strong or stronger current economics.

## Valuation conclusion

**CONCLUSION:** Current research priority should be:

1. **JEM — first valuation deep dive.** Most likely source of mispricing if HBM exposure and customer quality are verified.
2. **Micronics — second.** Stronger incumbent quality; likely deserves a premium to JEM, but may still offer better risk/reward than FORM.
3. **FormFactor — quality benchmark.** Excellent business evidence, but existing capital-allocation work shows demanding normalized return requirements at the September 2026 reference price.
4. **Technoprobe — operating benchmark.** Exceptional economics and growth, but the main task is determining how much is already priced in and how sustainable current margins are.

No peer is promoted to `High-conviction research candidate` yet. JEM’s apparent discount is a **research lead, not a conclusion**, until HBM-specific exposure, customer concentration, dilution, normalized earnings and valuation are verified on a common basis.

## Thesis breakers for the JEM undervaluation hypothesis

- JEM has materially weaker HBM4/HBM4E qualification than MJC/FORM.
- Current earnings are primarily conventional DRAM cycle leverage rather than HBM content/share gains.
- Capacity growth requires disproportionate capital intensity or recurring dilution.
- Customer concentration is extreme and share is not durable.
- Normalized operating margins fall far below current levels.
- The valuation discount disappears after adjusting for cycle-normalized earnings and balance-sheet differences.
