# SUSS

**Research stream:** AI memory — HBM wafer thinning / temporary bonding / hybrid bonding  
**Status:** Watch  
**Confidence:** Medium  
**Last substantive update:** 2026-09-07  
**Backlog:** closes issue #15 when merged

## Snapshot

**FACT:** SUSS MicroTec SE (Xetra: SMHN) supplies semiconductor equipment across advanced backend and photomask processes. For the HBM thesis, the key exposure is temporary bonding/debonding used to mechanically support ultra-thin DRAM wafers during thinning and backside processing. SUSS also has wafer-to-wafer and die-to-wafer hybrid-bonding platforms.

**FACT:** At its 2025 Capital Markets Day, SUSS described temporary bonding as its key Bonding revenue driver, said it was the market leader supplying **two of three HBM IDMs**, and positioned hybrid bonding as a future growth area where it was still a follower.

## Why it may matter

**HYPOTHESIS:** HBM layer-count growth forces thinner dies and more wafer-handling precision, which can increase temporary-bonding/debonding capacity and content even if the final die-stacking technology moves from TCB toward hybrid bonding. Because SUSS is only about €1.4bn in market value, a large HBM order cycle can move the company's earnings materially.

**CONCLUSION:** SUSS has the best pure small-cap asymmetry of the first research wave, but also the weakest transition certainty. **Investment Capture Score: 3.7/5.** The existing HBM temporary-bonding position is validated; hybrid bonding is not yet a proven HBM production moat. Status moves from `Investigating` to `Watch`, with a requirement to monitor customer share and hybrid-bond qualification closely.

## Evidence

### Product / technology

**FACT:** Temporary bonding/debonding supports ultra-thin wafers during grinding, polishing, backside processing and handling. This is structurally relevant to HBM because higher stack counts require thinner DRAM dies.

**FACT:** SUSS's 2025 Capital Markets Day materials explicitly describe temporary bonding/debonding as the key Bonding revenue driver, with HBM wafer thinning as a key application and market leadership at two of the three HBM IDMs.

**FACT:** SUSS's roadmap argues TBDB remains necessary across HBM3E, HBM4, HBM4E and HBM5 and that increasing layer count drives additional TBDB capacity. Its hybrid-bonding systems target advanced logic first and HBM later.

**FACT:** The XBS300 hybrid-bonding platform supports automated wafer-to-wafer and collective die-to-wafer processes and includes sub-100nm overlay capability in company specifications.

**INTERPRETATION:** The strongest part of the SUSS thesis is **not hybrid bonding today**. It is the installed temporary-bonding/debonding position that remains relevant even as downstream stacking architectures change.

### Customers / adoption

**FACT:** SUSS says it supplies two of three HBM IDMs for temporary bonding/debonding.

**FACT:** Q1-26 interim reporting said orders for Bonding Tools linked to customers' ongoing HBM capacity expansion were already significantly higher than in the entire first half of 2025.

**Negative evidence:** On the H1-26 call, management said it was not yet seeing renewed temporary-bonder momentum from one Korean customer and acknowledged significant competition and changing process-of-record requirements there.

**INTERPRETATION:** This is exactly the kind of contradictory evidence the thesis must preserve. The broad HBM market is strong, but SUSS's share is not guaranteed at every customer or generation.

### Commercial / financial

**FACT:** 2025 revenue was a record **€503.2m**, +12.6% y/y, with gross margin **35.7%** and EBIT margin **13.1%**.

**FACT:** Q1-26 order intake reached **€149.3m**, +69.5% y/y; the order book rose to **€330.1m**. Sales were only €86.5m and EBIT margin fell to 4.3%, showing the lag and operating leverage inherent in the business.

**FACT:** H1-26 order intake reached **€410.0m**, more than 2x sales, and the order book reached a record **€473.7m**. About €220m was scheduled for 2027. Advanced Backend Solutions accounted for **€326.2m** of H1 order intake.

**FACT:** Q2 profitability improved to **38.0% gross margin** and **9.0% EBIT margin**.

**Important caveat:** A major €115m Q2 order was for coating tools to an OSAT in the AI chain, not HBM temporary bonding. The record group backlog therefore cannot be treated as pure HBM bonding exposure.

### Competitive position

Temporary bonding competitors include EV Group and other equipment suppliers; SUSS's own materials acknowledge stronger competition at some HBM accounts.

Hybrid bonding is even more competitive, with Besi, EVG, ASMPT and others targeting advanced logic and memory. SUSS calls itself a follower in hybrid bonding today.

**INTERPRETATION:** SUSS has a credible installed/qualified moat in TBDB but not a proven end-to-end bonding moat. The investment thesis works best if TBDB remains mandatory *and* SUSS earns incremental hybrid-bond share; the second condition is optional upside, not base-case evidence.

### Service / installed base

**FACT:** SUSS's 2030 plan aims to increase service from roughly 18% to 25% of group sales. Management describes the installed base as a strategic lever for upgrades, spares, service and warranty contracts.

**INTERPRETATION:** A larger service mix could make the economics of equipment share more durable, but this is still a future target rather than current HBM-specific proof.

## Evidence against the thesis

- One key Korean HBM customer has significant competing suppliers and no clear H2-26 order momentum yet.
- Hybrid bonding could shift value toward suppliers with stronger die-to-wafer alignment / placement positions.
- Group backlog is inflated by large non-bonding AI orders; not all advanced-backend demand is HBM TBDB.
- 2026 revenue and EBIT are guided below 2025, showing order timing can create sharp earnings volatility.
- TBDB may remain technically mandatory without allowing exceptional pricing power.
- Customer process-of-record changes can displace qualified tools faster than a simple "2 of 3 customers" statistic suggests.

---

## Risks and substitutes

- EVG / regional competitor share gains;
- customer-specific process changes;
- hybrid-bond transition timing;
- order lumpiness and working-capital execution;
- high R&D spending to enter new markets;
- multiple compression after a large share-price rerating.

## Catalysts

- new HBM4/HBM4E temporary-bonding orders from the third / contested HBM customer;
- disclosed HBM hybrid-bond production qualification;
- conversion of €220m+ 2027 backlog with better operating leverage;
- service mix increases toward the 25% target;
- evidence that layer-count growth increases TBDB tools per unit of HBM capacity.

## Thesis breakers

1. Loss of a major HBM temporary-bonding process-of-record without replacement business.
2. HBM layer growth fails to increase TBDB equipment intensity.
3. Hybrid bonding reduces or redesigns wafer-thinning flows in a way that materially lowers SUSS content.
4. Advanced Backend order growth fails to translate into sustainable >15% EBIT margin over the cycle.
5. SUSS remains a hybrid-bond follower with no meaningful HBM production adoption through the HBM5 transition.

## Open questions

1. What percentage of Bonding Systems revenue/order intake is HBM TBDB today?
2. Which two HBM IDMs use SUSS in production, and at what share of capacity?
3. What is the expected tool count / revenue intensity change from 12-high to 16-high HBM?
4. What proportion of installed TBDB revenue becomes recurring service/spares?
5. Which customer and production generation will provide the first true HBM hybrid-bond qualification?
