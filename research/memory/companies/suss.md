## Snapshot

**FACT:** SUSS MicroTec SE (Xetra: SMHN) supplies semiconductor equipment across advanced backend and photomask processes. For the HBM thesis, the key exposure is temporary bonding/debonding used to mechanically support ultra-thin DRAM wafers during thinning and backside processing. SUSS also has wafer-to-wafer and die-to-wafer hybrid-bonding platforms.

**FACT:** At its 2025 Capital Markets Day, SUSS described temporary bonding as its key Bonding revenue driver, said it was the market leader supplying **two of three HBM IDMs**, and positioned hybrid bonding as a future growth area where it was still a follower.

**CONCLUSION:** SUSS remains one of the strongest small-cap HBM equipment asymmetry candidates in the universe, but the current Gate-E setup is **Watch / pre-entry catalyst asymmetric**, not an immediate core allocation. The HBM/advanced-packaging demand evidence is strong; the decisive uncertainty is whether the record backlog converts into durable 2027–2028 earnings while SUSS retains temporary-bonding process-of-record share through the architecture transition.

## Evidence

### Product / technology

**FACT:** Temporary bonding/debonding supports ultra-thin wafers during grinding, polishing, backside processing and handling. This is structurally relevant to HBM because higher stack counts require thinner DRAM dies.

**FACT:** SUSS's 2025 Capital Markets Day materials explicitly describe temporary bonding/debonding as the key Bonding revenue driver, with HBM wafer thinning as a key application and market leadership at two of the three HBM IDMs.

**FACT:** SUSS's roadmap argues TBDB remains necessary across HBM3E, HBM4, HBM4E and HBM5 and that increasing layer count drives additional TBDB capacity. Its hybrid-bonding systems target advanced logic first and HBM later.

**FACT:** The XBS300 hybrid-bonding platform supports automated wafer-to-wafer and collective die-to-wafer processes and includes sub-100nm overlay capability in company specifications.

**INTERPRETATION:** The strongest part of the SUSS thesis today is the installed temporary-bonding/debonding position, not an assumed future hybrid-bond moat.

### Customers / adoption

**FACT:** SUSS says it supplies two of three HBM IDMs for temporary bonding/debonding.

**FACT:** Q1 2026 reporting said Bonding Tools orders linked to HBM capacity expansion were already significantly higher than in the entire first half of 2025.

**Negative evidence:** On the H1 2026 call, management said it was not yet seeing renewed temporary-bonder momentum from one Korean customer and acknowledged significant competition and changing process-of-record requirements.

**FACT / positive counter-evidence:** Management also said SUSS had delivered a system to the second Korean HBM customer after a long qualification effort, with another system in the pipeline, and remained deeply engaged in throughput/yield optimization and incremental tool installation at the non-Korean customer.

**INTERPRETATION:** The broad HBM market is strong, but SUSS's share is not guaranteed at every customer or generation. The second Korean customer provides real optionality; the contested Korean account remains the main falsification risk.

### Commercial / financial

**FACT:** 2025 revenue was a record **€503.2m**, with gross margin **35.7%** and EBIT margin **13.1%**.

**FACT:** 2026 guidance is **€425–485m revenue** and **35–37% gross margin**, confirming 2026 as a transition year rather than a straight-line growth year.

**FACT:** H1 2026 order intake reached **€410.0m** and order book **€473.7m**, with approximately **€220m** scheduled for 2027.

**Important caveat:** A major €115m Q2 order was for coating tools to an OSAT in the AI chain, not HBM temporary bonding. The record group backlog therefore cannot be treated as pure HBM bonding exposure.

### Competitive position

Temporary-bonding competitors include EV Group and other equipment suppliers; SUSS's own management acknowledges stronger competition at some HBM accounts.

Hybrid bonding is more competitive, with Besi, EVG, ASMPT and others targeting advanced logic and memory. SUSS has described itself as a follower in hybrid bonding today.

**INTERPRETATION:** SUSS has a credible installed/qualified moat in TBDB but not a proven end-to-end bonding moat. Hybrid-bond capture remains optional upside, not base-case evidence.

## Evidence against the thesis

- One key Korean HBM customer has significant competing suppliers and no clear renewed temporary-bonder momentum yet.
- Hybrid bonding could shift value toward suppliers with stronger die-to-wafer alignment / placement positions.
- Group backlog includes large non-bonding AI orders; it cannot be treated as pure HBM TBDB demand.
- 2026 revenue and EBIT are below 2025 levels, demonstrating order timing and operating leverage risk.
- TBDB may remain technically mandatory without allowing exceptional pricing power.
- Customer process-of-record changes can displace qualified tools faster than a simple "2 of 3 customers" statistic suggests.

## Open questions
