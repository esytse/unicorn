## Snapshot

**FACT:** ASMPT (HKEX: 0522) supplies semiconductor and electronics-manufacturing equipment. Its advanced-packaging portfolio spans thermo-compression bonding (TCB), fine-pitch flip-chip/mass-reflow processes and hybrid bonding, giving it exposure to multiple possible HBM and heterogeneous-integration architectures.

**FACT:** ASMPT reported US$532.1m of advanced-packaging revenue in 2025, up 30.2% year over year and representing about 30% of Group revenue. TCB revenue grew about 146% year over year. The company says its HBM4 12H TCB solutions were first to secure orders from multiple HBM players, while HBM4 16H flux-based tools are in sampling and its AOR fluxless process is under qualification.

**FACT:** Q1-26 Group revenue rose 32.0% year over year to HK$3.97bn and bookings rose 71.6% to HK$5.67bn, the highest in four years. Management continued to cite TCB, hybrid bonding and other AI-related products as structural growth drivers.

## Why it may matter

**HYPOTHESIS:** A supplier spanning TCB, mass-reflow/fine-pitch assembly and hybrid bonding may be less exposed to one HBM process architecture than a pure TCB supplier. The trade-off is lower total-company earnings sensitivity because ASMPT is broader and has substantial non-HBM businesses.

**CONCLUSION:** ASMPT offers one of the more architecture-resilient bonding portfolios in the research set, with credible multi-customer HBM4 evidence. **Investment Capture Score: 3.7/5.** It moves from `Research queue` to `Watch`. Its broad portfolio reduces transition risk but also dilutes the asymmetric earnings sensitivity that makes smaller HBM specialists attractive.

## Evidence

### HBM production and qualification

**FACT:** ASMPT says HBM4 12H TCB tools secured orders from multiple HBM players. Its 2025 annual report says the company captured sizeable HBM share with Q4 shipments and that flux-based HBM4 16H tools were adopted for sampling while AOR fluxless technology was under qualification.

**FACT:** In Q1-26 a key memory player was using ASMPT's flux-based TCB tool for sampling and qualifying its AOR fluxless process for HBM4 16H.

**INTERPRETATION:** Multi-customer evidence is strategically important because it reduces dependence on one HBM IDM or one proprietary process flow.

### Cross-architecture capability

**FACT:** ASMPT markets thermo-compression bonding, high-precision flip-chip/mass-reflow assembly and hybrid-bonding systems. Its hybrid-bonding tools received customer buy-offs and additional shipments in 2025, while second-generation development continued.

**FACT:** The company's AOR approach removes oxides without flux and targets fine-pitch HBM bonding where contamination and cleaning become more difficult.

**INTERPRETATION:** ASMPT is better positioned than a TCB-only supplier if value migrates toward fluxless processes or hybrid bonding, although high-volume HBM hybrid-bond adoption is still not established.

### Financial capture

**FACT:** 2025 advanced-packaging revenue was US$532.1m, +30.2% y/y, and TCB revenue increased approximately 146%.

**FACT:** Q1-26 bookings were HK$5.67bn, +71.6% y/y, with book-to-bill 1.43. Revenue was HK$3.97bn, +32.0% y/y.

**Negative evidence:** Much of the Group's demand also comes from SMT, photonics, mainstream die/wire bonding and non-HBM applications. Strong AI demand therefore does not translate one-for-one into HBM earnings.

## Evidence against the thesis

- Hanmi, Hanwha, Besi, EV Group and others compete across TCB/hybrid-bond steps.
- HBM hybrid bonding may favor specialists with stronger wafer-to-wafer/die-to-wafer process positions.
- Advanced-packaging growth is diluted by lower-growth or lower-margin Group businesses.
- Management's TCB TAM/share targets are company estimates and may prove optimistic.
- Semiconductor-equipment orders remain cyclical despite structural AI growth.
- Multi-architecture capability does not guarantee process-of-record wins in the final HBM architecture.

## Catalysts

- additional HBM4E/16H production orders from multiple memory customers;
- AOR fluxless TCB reaching high-volume production;
- explicit HBM hybrid-bond customer qualification;
- sustained advanced-packaging share above 30% of Group revenue;
- SEMI mix driving durable Group margin expansion.

## Thesis breakers

1. ASMPT loses HBM4/HBM4E TCB share despite overall market growth.
2. Hybrid bonding reaches HBM mass production without meaningful ASMPT qualification.
3. Advanced-packaging revenue growth slows materially below the company's TAM assumptions.
4. Group margins fail to improve as AP mix grows.
5. HBM contribution remains too small to materially change Group earnings.

## Open questions

1. What absolute share of AP revenue comes from HBM versus advanced logic?
2. What is ASMPT's actual HBM TCB share by customer?
3. How much recurring/service revenue follows the installed base?
4. Does AOR produce measurable ASP/margin uplift relative to standard TCB?
5. Which HBM customers are evaluating the hybrid-bond platform?
