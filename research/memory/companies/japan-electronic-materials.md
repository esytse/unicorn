# Japan Electronic Materials (JEM)

**Research stream:** AI memory — HBM / memory probe cards  
**Ticker:** TSE 6855  
**Status:** Investigating  
**Confidence:** Medium-Low  
**Last substantive update:** 2026-09-07

## Snapshot

**FACT:** Japan Electronic Materials develops and manufactures semiconductor probe cards, including advanced probe-card products used in wafer test.

**FACT:** Management says FY2027 Q1 sales and profit rose sharply because memory-probe-card sales expanded and domestic factories maintained high utilisation. On 7 August 2026 the company raised its outlook; full-year guidance is **¥36.4bn revenue, ¥9.45bn operating profit and ¥6.7bn net income**.

**CONCLUSION:** JEM is the highest-priority new valuation investigation in the HBM-test peer set. It is **not yet a high-conviction candidate** because HBM-specific customer/share evidence is less transparent than FormFactor's.

## Why it may matter

**HYPOTHESIS:** HBM increases the economic value of wafer-level known-good-die testing and raises electrical/probe-density requirements. A smaller specialist such as JEM can therefore experience much greater earnings sensitivity than the memory market itself if it wins HBM sockets while fixed-cost factories run at high utilisation.

**INTERPRETATION:** JEM's combination of rapid earnings growth and a much smaller equity value than MJC, FORM or Technoprobe creates the most interesting apparent asymmetry in the peer group.

## Commercial / financial evidence

**FACT:** FY2026 revenue was about **¥29.37bn** and net income about **¥5.45bn**, up roughly 23% and 58% respectively according to S&P Global-derived market data.

**FACT:** At the 7 September 2026 close of roughly **¥6,650**, third-party market data put JEM's equity value at roughly **¥97bn** (using ~14.67m issued shares). The same market-data source showed a low-teens trailing/forward earnings multiple depending on the earnings snapshot.

**INTERPRETATION:** The absolute market value is only a fraction of MJC's and dramatically below FORM/Technoprobe. If JEM's FY2027 earnings are structurally HBM-driven rather than simply peak DRAM-cycle utilisation, the valuation gap is potentially meaningful.

## Competitive position

Known listed peers include Micronics Japan, FormFactor and Technoprobe.

**Positive evidence:** Management directly attributes current growth to memory probe cards and is benefiting from capacity investments already placed into service.

**Negative evidence / uncertainty:** Public materials reviewed so far do not establish the same level of HBM-customer specificity as FormFactor's disclosed volume shipments to all three major HBM manufacturers. This is the single biggest evidence gap.

## Valuation / investability

**Point-in-time reference, 7 September 2026:** price ~¥6,650; market cap ~¥97bn. Treat third-party valuation ratios as monitoring data only and refresh before any capital decision.

**HYPOTHESIS:** JEM can become the preferred probe-card capital-allocation candidate if (1) HBM exposure is verified, (2) current operating margin proves sustainable enough through a normalized cycle, and (3) the equity continues to trade at a material discount to MJC/FORM on normalized earnings.

## Catalysts

- further FY2027 guidance upgrades;
- explicit HBM4/HBM4E customer or qualification disclosure;
- sustained high factory utilisation without disproportionate capex;
- evidence that advanced probe-card mix is raising structural margins;
- improved investor disclosure around HBM revenue/share.

## Risks / thesis breakers

1. Current growth is mainly conventional DRAM-cycle leverage rather than HBM content/share gains.
2. JEM lacks competitive HBM4/high-speed insertions versus MJC/FORM.
3. Customer concentration produces abrupt share loss or order volatility.
4. Margin falls sharply as utilisation normalises.
5. Capacity expansion requires recurring equity issuance or weak returns on capital.
6. The apparent valuation discount disappears on cycle-normalized EPS.

## Open questions

1. What percentage of revenue and gross profit is HBM-related?
2. Which of Samsung, SK hynix and Micron are production customers, and at which insertion points?
3. What is JEM's share in HBM probe cards versus conventional DRAM?
4. What are probe-card ASPs, replacement cycles and repair/service economics?
5. What normalized operating margin should be used after the current capacity ramp?
6. What is the fully diluted share count after recent financing/capital actions?
