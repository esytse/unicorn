# Japan Electronic Materials (JEM)

**Research stream:** AI memory — HBM / memory probe cards  
**Ticker:** TSE 6855  
**Status:** Investigating  
**Confidence:** Medium-Low on investability; Medium on participation in HBM-related DRAM growth  
**Last substantive update:** 2026-09-07

## Snapshot

**FACT:** Japan Electronic Materials develops and manufactures semiconductor probe cards, including MEMS-based advanced probe cards used in wafer test.

**FACT:** Management says FY2027 Q1 sales and profit rose sharply because memory-probe-card sales expanded and domestic factories maintained high utilisation. On 7 August 2026 the company raised its outlook; full-year guidance is **¥36.4bn revenue, ¥9.45bn operating profit and ¥6.7bn net income**.

**FACT:** A JEM company-controlled recruitment page states that the company has a high share in NAND and is **increasing share in DRAM, centered on HBM used in generative AI**. This is direct company evidence of HBM participation, but it is not an investor filing and does not quantify HBM revenue or customer sockets.

**CONCLUSION:** JEM is the highest-priority new valuation investigation in the HBM-test peer set, but it is **not yet a Gate-C or high-conviction candidate**. The low earnings multiple is attractive; cash conversion, dilution and customer/share specificity are the binding evidence gaps.

## Why it may matter

**HYPOTHESIS:** HBM increases the economic value of wafer-level known-good-die testing and raises electrical/probe-density requirements. A smaller specialist such as JEM can therefore experience much greater earnings sensitivity than the memory market itself if it wins HBM sockets while fixed-cost factories run at high utilisation.

**INTERPRETATION:** JEM's combination of rapid earnings growth and a much smaller equity value than MJC, FORM or Technoprobe creates the most interesting apparent asymmetry in the peer group.

## Commercial / customer evidence

**FACT:** FY2026 revenue was **¥29.37bn**, operating profit **¥7.25bn** and net income **¥5.45bn**. Company materials say memory-probe-card expansion and high factory utilisation drove the step-up.

**FACT:** The FY2026 securities-report customer disclosures show:

- **Micron Memory Japan:** ¥4.567bn, **15.6%** of sales;
- **Micron Memory Taiwan:** ¥4.495bn, **15.3%**;
- **FlashForward:** ¥4.054bn, **13.8%**.

The top three therefore accounted for roughly **44.7% of total sales**. This verifies meaningful exposure to major memory customers, especially Micron entities, but does **not** identify which revenue is HBM rather than conventional DRAM/NAND.

**FACT:** FY2026 semiconductor-inspection order intake was about **¥32.53bn**, +24.3%, and backlog about **¥11.72bn**, +40.7%.

**INTERPRETATION:** The combination of Micron customer concentration, rising memory-probe-card sales, backlog and the company's statement that DRAM share is rising around HBM makes the HBM link substantially more credible than a generic AI-market narrative. It still falls short of FormFactor's explicit all-three-HBM-manufacturer production evidence.

## Competitive evidence

Known listed peers include Micronics Japan, FormFactor and Technoprobe.

**Positive evidence:** JEM says HBM-centered DRAM share is rising, and the latest customer disclosures verify large relationships with Micron Memory Japan and Micron Memory Taiwan.

**Contradictory evidence:** A 2026 Korean probe-card competitor regulatory filing reviewed during falsification listed FormFactor and Micronics as overseas DRAM probe-card competitors, while listing JEM in NAND and non-memory categories rather than DRAM. The competitor taxonomy may not be exhaustive, but it is evidence against assuming JEM already has broad HBM/DRAM leadership.

**INTERPRETATION:** JEM appears to participate in HBM-linked DRAM growth, but current public evidence is consistent with a **growing challenger** rather than a proven market leader.

## Cash conversion / capital intensity

**FACT:** FY2026 operating cash flow was **¥5.76bn** and tangible fixed-asset purchases were about **¥3.52bn**. TTM third-party data through June 2026 showed free cash flow around **¥2.24bn**, only ~2–3% of the September equity value.

**FACT:** FY2026 financing cash inflow was **¥12.71bn**, including **¥12.15bn from share issuance** and ~¥1.98bn from bond issuance.

**FACT:** Third-party share data at end-August 2026 showed **14.65m shares outstanding**, up about **5.3% y/y** and **10.4% q/q** after the capital actions.

**INTERPRETATION:** This is the most important correction to the headline low-P/E thesis. JEM is cheap on accounting earnings, but current free-cash-flow yield is much less compelling because the company is funding a major capacity build. The equity raise strengthens the balance sheet and growth capacity, but it also proves that dilution is a real part of the investment case.

## Valuation / capital allocation

**Common reference, 4 September 2026:** price **¥6,230**; ~14.65m shares imply equity value about **¥91.3bn**. FY Mar-2027 management-guided net income of ¥6.7bn implies EPS roughly **¥457** and a price / guided-EPS multiple of about **13.6x**.

The first normalized scenario screen in `research/memory/probe-card-capital-allocation.md` gives an approximate **9.4% base CAGR** to end-2030 from the 4 September reference and a ~12% base-return monitoring zone near **¥5,640**. JEM closed **¥6,650 on 7 September**, widening that gap.

**CONCLUSION:** JEM is the best **valuation lead** in the new probe-card peer set, but should remain below the fully underwritten Onto / SUSS capital frontier until HBM share and normalized cash conversion are verified. A low P/E alone is not enough.

## Catalysts

- investor-grade disclosure of HBM4/HBM4E customer or qualification progress;
- explicit HBM / DRAM mix or share disclosure;
- sustained order/backlog growth while free-cash-flow conversion improves;
- new capacity reaching productive utilisation without further material dilution;
- further FY2027 guidance upgrades supported by cash rather than working-capital build.

## Risks / thesis breakers

1. Current growth is mainly conventional DRAM/NAND-cycle leverage rather than HBM content/share gains.
2. JEM lacks competitive HBM4/high-speed insertions versus MJC/FORM.
3. Top-customer concentration produces abrupt share loss or order volatility.
4. Margin falls sharply as utilisation normalises.
5. Capacity expansion continues to consume most cash flow or requires recurring equity issuance.
6. The apparent valuation discount disappears on cycle-normalized EPS / FCF.
7. The 2026 recruitment-site HBM share claim is not corroborated by customer or investor-grade disclosure.

## Open questions

1. What percentage of revenue and gross profit is specifically HBM-related?
2. Which Micron HBM generations / wafer-test insertions use JEM probe cards?
3. Does JEM have production HBM sockets at Samsung or SK hynix?
4. What is JEM's share in HBM probe cards versus conventional DRAM and NAND?
5. What normalized operating margin and FCF margin should be used after the current capacity ramp?
6. How much further capex and dilution is required for the 2028 Amagasaki factory and other capacity additions?
7. What are probe-card ASPs, replacement cycles and repair/service economics?
