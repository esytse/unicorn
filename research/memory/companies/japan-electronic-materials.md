# Japan Electronic Materials (JEM)

**Research stream:** AI memory — HBM / memory probe cards  
**Ticker:** TSE 6855  
**Status:** Investigating  
**Gate-E portfolio role:** Watch / pre-entry catalyst-asymmetric candidate  
**Confidence:** Medium on current operating capture; Medium-Low on deployability / HBM-generation proof  
**Last substantive update:** 2026-09-09

## Executive conclusion

**INTERPRETATION:** JEM remains the most interesting **valuation/asymmetry lead** in the Japanese memory-probe-card peer set, but the 18-month Gate-E underwrite does **not** support a current research-level `ACTION` at the 9 September 2026 close of **¥6,570**.

The operating evidence is strong:

- FY2027 Q1 revenue and profit accelerated sharply as memory probe-card sales expanded and domestic factories ran at high utilisation;
- Q1 operating margin was roughly **32%**;
- full-year guidance is **¥36.4bn revenue / ¥9.45bn operating profit / ¥6.7bn net income / ¥457.42 EPS**;
- JEM itself says DRAM share is increasing **centered on HBM used in generative AI**;
- large Micron Memory Japan and Micron Memory Taiwan relationships make HBM participation more credible than a generic AI narrative;
- the June balance sheet remains strong, with roughly **¥17.9bn net cash** after subtracting bonds and long-term borrowings from cash/securities.

The key Gate-E constraint is timing. The approximately **¥12.5bn Amagasaki factory is scheduled for completion in August 2028**, after the March-2028 portfolio target. Therefore the new plant cannot be credited as a meaningful completed-factory earnings contributor inside the active horizon. The near-window investment case must be won by existing-factory utilisation, mix, HBM/advanced-DRAM share, earnings upgrades and cash conversion.

What still blocks a deployable role at the current price:

- HBM revenue, exact production sockets and HBM4/HBM4E customer share remain undisclosed;
- the top three disclosed FY2026 customers were about **44.7% of sales**;
- receivables and work-in-process increased materially in Q1 while cash declined, so cash conversion still needs monitoring;
- the Amagasaki expansion is essentially funded through roughly **¥12.2bn of public-equity proceeds**, so per-share returns must absorb the dilution already taken;
- a credible 2x stock outcome by March 2028 requires both a large earnings step-up and rerating, not merely delivery of current guidance.

**Current Gate-E decision:** keep JEM as **Watch / pre-entry catalyst asymmetric** within the portfolio framework and `Investigating` on the research watchlist. It remains #1 in the hunting queue because a ~¥96bn company with real earnings can rerate sharply if the HBM evidence gap closes, but capital should not be forced merely because the long-duration Gate-D valuation zone is nearby.

### Why Amagasaki is not the 18-month catalyst

**FACT:** The current medium-term plan says the new Amagasaki factory is planned to complete in **August 2028** and requires approximately **¥12.5bn** investment. Approximately **¥12.2bn** raised in the February/March 2026 public offering is allocated to the factory.

**INTERPRETATION:** The plant may be strategically valuable, but it sits outside the March-2028 target date. Gate E therefore treats Amagasaki primarily as:

1. a use-of-capital / dilution fact already borne by shareholders;
2. evidence that management expects sustained memory-probe demand;
3. an option on post-horizon growth;
4. a potential risk if construction absorbs more capital before existing capacity demonstrates adequate cash returns.

Do not capitalize completed Amagasaki output into the March-2028 base case.

### Transaction rules — research level only

#### Entry

Two independent paths can justify an initial position; neither is automatic brokerage execution.

**Price-led entry condition:**
- share price at or below approximately **¥6,100**;
- FY2027 guidance remains intact or improves;
- no material deterioration in HBM/advanced-DRAM evidence, margins, customer position or balance sheet;
- no new material equity raise that worsens per-share economics.

If all conditions hold, a **6–8% initial portfolio research size** is supportable as a catalyst-asymmetric position, subject to comparison with then-current SUSS/MJC/non-memory alternatives.

**Evidence-led entry condition:**
- credible investor-grade HBM4/HBM4E production/share evidence or an equivalent operating disclosure materially closes the core evidence gap;
- the updated March-2028 underwrite shows at least roughly **60% remaining-window upside** from the then-current price with tolerable downside and no new material dilution.

This path can justify entry above ¥6,100 because the evidence, not just price, has improved.

#### Add

Increase only after at least one of these improves without compensating deterioration:

- named/quantified HBM4/HBM4E production evidence;
- FY2028 earnings visibility;
- operating cash flow / FCF conversion;
- existing-factory utilization and high-value mix;
- valuation / remaining-window expected return.

A reasonable **maximum research size is 12–14%** only after HBM production evidence and cash conversion improve. Before that proof, keep the position below the maximum because evidence confidence is still Medium-Low on deployability.

#### Trim

Trim/reduce the research allocation when:

- price rerates materially faster than evidence or earnings, leaving **<~30% documented remaining-window upside** under the refreshed base/upside cases;
- the stock trades on a materially higher multiple without corresponding HBM share/FCF evidence;
- position concentration becomes disproportionate to evidence confidence;
- SUSS, MJC or a non-memory candidate offers clearly superior remaining-window asymmetry.

#### Sell / thesis break

Exit/reject the Gate-E thesis if any of the following occurs materially:

1. credible evidence shows JEM is failing to participate in HBM4/HBM4E production or loses important advanced-DRAM sockets;
2. memory growth proves mainly conventional cycle/utilization leverage and margins normalize sharply without a compensating share gain;
3. another material equity raise is required before Amagasaki contributes, without a clearly higher per-share return path;
4. operating cash flow/FCF persistently fails to follow accounting earnings as capex continues;
5. Micron/customer concentration crystallizes into a material share/order loss;
6. existing capacity cannot support the FY2028 earnings bridge required for meaningful March-2028 upside.

## 1. What the company sells

**FACT:** Japan Electronic Materials develops and manufactures semiconductor probe cards, including MEMS-based advanced probe cards used for wafer-level electrical test.

**FACT:** JEM's MC Series combines MEMS probe technology with a thin-film multilayer wiring substrate and supports full-wafer probing on 300 mm wafers. JEM positions the platform for DRAM and NAND testing.

**INTERPRETATION:** This is an enabling layer inside the validated **test / known-good-die bottleneck (4.6/5)**. As HBM stack and package value rises, early wafer-level screening becomes more economically important because an escaped defect can destroy much more downstream package value.

**HYPOTHESIS:** If JEM wins HBM production sockets, its smaller revenue base and fixed-cost factory structure can create materially greater earnings sensitivity than the memory market itself.

## 2. Latest operating evidence

### FY2027 Q1 — April to June 2026

**FACT:** Q1 revenue was approximately **¥9.641bn**.

**FACT:** Q1 operating profit was approximately **¥3.094bn**, implying an operating margin of about **32.1%**.

**FACT:** Q1 ordinary profit was approximately **¥3.192bn** and net income approximately **¥2.169bn**; EPS was about **¥148.1**.

**FACT:** Management attributes the strong quarter to expansion of memory probe-card sales and high utilisation at domestic factories, with foreign-exchange movements also contributing.

**FACT:** On 7 August 2026 management raised its forecast. Full-year guidance is now:

- revenue: **¥36.4bn**;
- operating profit: **¥9.45bn**;
- ordinary profit: **¥9.3bn**;
- net income: **¥6.7bn**.

**FACT:** Management continues to expect solid memory-probe-card demand in the second half but did not extrapolate the entire Q1 profit run-rate into full-year guidance.

**INTERPRETATION:** The current earnings acceleration is real. The conservative full-year framing is useful because it limits the temptation to capitalize a 32% Q1 margin as permanent.

## 3. HBM / customer evidence

### Direct HBM evidence

**FACT:** A JEM company-controlled recruitment / identity page says the company has a high share in NAND and is **increasing DRAM share, centered on HBM used in generative AI**.

**INTERPRETATION:** This is more direct than an inferred AI connection, but it is still weaker evidence than an investor filing or customer qualification announcement. It does not quantify HBM revenue, generation, customer or share.

### Major customers

**FACT:** FY2026 securities-report customer disclosures show:

- **Micron Memory Japan:** ¥4.567bn, **15.6%** of group sales;
- **Micron Memory Taiwan:** ¥4.495bn, **15.3%**;
- **FlashForward:** ¥4.054bn, **13.8%**.

The top three therefore represented roughly **44.7%** of FY2026 sales.

**INTERPRETATION:** Micron Memory Japan + Taiwan at roughly **31% of sales** materially strengthens the probability that JEM participates in advanced DRAM/HBM production. It still does not establish which Micron HBM generations or test insertions use JEM cards.

### Orders / backlog

**FACT:** FY2026 semiconductor-inspection order intake was approximately **¥32.53bn, +24.3%**, and backlog approximately **¥11.72bn, +40.7%**.

**INTERPRETATION:** The operating evidence is consistent with a genuine memory-probe upcycle. The unresolved question is how much is structural HBM content/share gain versus conventional DRAM/NAND cycle leverage.

## 4. Competitive evidence

The principal listed comparators are **Micronics Japan, FormFactor and Technoprobe**.

**Positive evidence:**

- JEM says HBM-centered DRAM share is rising;
- the latest customer disclosures verify large Micron relationships;
- its MEMS + thin-film technology is technically relevant to high-pin-count, full-wafer memory test;
- rapid orders/backlog and high utilization show meaningful current demand.

**Contradictory evidence:** a 2026 Korean probe-card competitor regulatory filing reviewed during the earlier falsification pass listed FormFactor and Micronics as overseas DRAM probe-card competitors, while listing JEM in NAND and other probe-card categories rather than DRAM.

**INTERPRETATION:** The evidence is most consistent with JEM being a **growing HBM/DRAM challenger rather than a proven global HBM leader**. This distinction matters because the valuation thesis assumes that current DRAM growth is not simply a temporary utilization cycle.

## 5. Capacity build and dilution — now a central part of the thesis

### Current expansion

**FACT:** JEM's revised medium-term investment plan increased planned cumulative capital expenditure for 2024–2026 from roughly **¥8.5bn to ¥14.882bn**.

**FACT:** The 2026 single-year investment plan is approximately **¥7.0bn**.

### New Amagasaki factory

**FACT:** JEM plans a new Amagasaki factory focused on expanding M-type probe-card production and development.

**FACT:** Completion is planned for **August 2028**.

**FACT:** The planned investment is approximately **¥12.5bn**.

**FACT:** JEM's financing presentation shows that approximately **¥12.2bn raised through a public equity offering** is intended to fund essentially the entire new Amagasaki factory investment, while existing-factory investments are funded primarily with cash/debt.

**INTERPRETATION:** This is the clearest falsification of a simplistic '13–14x earnings = cheap' thesis. Growth requires substantial capital, and shareholders have already funded the next capacity leg directly through dilution.

**INTERPRETATION:** The equity financing also lowers balance-sheet risk. The correct conclusion is therefore not that the raise is automatically bad, but that **per-share returns must be measured after the capacity and dilution needed to create them**.

**OPEN QUESTION:** Does the 2028 factory create HBM-specific high-return capacity, or simply expand conventional probe-card volume into a more competitive market?

## 6. Cash conversion

**FACT:** FY2026 operating cash flow was approximately **¥5.76bn**.

**FACT:** tangible fixed-asset purchases were approximately **¥3.52bn**.

**FACT:** third-party TTM data through June 2026 showed free cash flow around **¥2.24bn**.

**FACT:** FY2026 financing cash inflow included approximately **¥12.15bn from share issuance** plus bond financing.

**FACT:** In FY2027 Q1, cash fell by approximately **¥2.07bn** while electronic receivables rose by ~¥0.45bn, trade receivables by ~¥2.17bn and work-in-process by ~¥0.39bn.

**INTERPRETATION:** JEM's earnings multiple is substantially more attractive than its free-cash-flow yield. The capacity build may prove economically rational, but until factories mature the accounting P/E overstates current distributable economics.

## 10. Thesis breakers

1. Current growth proves mainly conventional DRAM/NAND cycle leverage rather than HBM content/share gains.
2. JEM fails to win HBM4/HBM4E/high-speed insertions against MJC/FormFactor.
3. Micron concentration translates into abrupt order/share loss.
4. Q1 margins fall sharply as utilization normalizes.
5. Amagasaki and other capacity absorb cash without attractive incremental returns.
6. Further material dilution is required before the 2028 factory contributes.
7. The HBM-centered share claim remains uncorroborated by investor-grade/customer evidence.
8. Cycle-normalized EPS/FCF eliminates the apparent valuation discount.
