# Micronics Japan (MJC)

**Research stream:** AI memory — HBM / memory probe cards  
**Ticker:** TSE 6871  
**Status:** Investigating  
**Confidence:** Medium overall; Medium-High on operating / memory-probe capture  
**Last substantive update:** 2026-09-08

## Executive conclusion

**INTERPRETATION:** Micronics Japan is the **stronger operating and technical franchise** than Japan Electronic Materials in the current Japanese probe-card comparison. It has more direct evidence that HBM is driving DRAM probe-card demand, a much purer probe-card revenue base, very high current margins, positive free cash flow despite heavy capacity investment, and explicit development for next-generation HBM.

The latest results materially strengthen the operating thesis:

- H1 2026 sales **¥49.206bn, +48.6% y/y**;
- H1 operating profit **¥15.557bn, +105.5%**, implying a **31.6% operating margin**;
- full-year guidance raised to **¥103.8bn revenue / ¥31.4bn operating profit / ¥23.0bn net income**;
- guided full-year operating margin is about **30.3%**, well above the prior FV26 target of 25%;
- HBM demand is explicitly cited as a driver of DRAM probe-card sales;
- MJC continues developing technologies for **next-generation HBM**.

But the deeper pass also surfaces a major risk that was underweighted previously:

> **customer concentration is extreme.**

In FY2025, Samsung Electronics represented about **¥35.0bn / 49.9%** of group sales and Micron Memory Taiwan about **¥15.0bn / 21.4%**. Together they represented roughly **71% of group revenue**.

That concentration is partly evidence of deep qualification with major memory manufacturers, but it also means a share loss, qualification change, memory-capex pause or customer-specific HBM architecture change can move total-company earnings abruptly.

At the **8 September 2026 ¥12,310 reference price**, MJC has an equity value around **¥501bn** and trades at approximately **20.8x company-guided FY2026 EPS of ¥593.31**. Re-anchoring the existing normalized scenario to company guidance rather than the earlier ~¥625 consensus anchor lowers the approximate base annualized return to **~4.8%** and the ~12% base-return zone to roughly **¥9,270**.

**Current decision:** remain `Investigating`. MJC is the preferred **quality benchmark** in Japanese memory probe cards, but not the preferred current capital setup. A substantial valuation reset, or evidence that current ~30% margins and HBM share can persist well above the normalized assumptions, is required before Gate C.

## 1. What the company sells

**FACT:** Micronics Japan develops probe cards and semiconductor test equipment.

**FACT:** In FY2025, the probe-card business generated approximately **¥68.525bn**, around **97.7% of group sales**.

**FACT:** MJC describes itself as holding the **world's leading / top share in memory probe cards**.

**INTERPRETATION:** Unlike diversified semiconductor-equipment companies, MJC is very close to a pure listed exposure to wafer-level probe-card demand. If HBM test content rises, the impact can move total-company economics directly.

---

## 2. Technology and HBM relevance

### U-Probe platform

**FACT:** MJC's proprietary U-Probe combines MEMS micro-cantilever probe technology with thin-film multilayer wiring.

**FACT:** MJC positions the platform for DRAM and Flash memory and supports large-area / one-touchdown testing on 12-inch wafers.

**FACT:** published product information describes configurations around **2,500 DUT and roughly 200,000 pins on one card**, illustrating the extreme parallelism required in advanced memory wafer testing.

**INTERPRETATION:** Increasing HBM I/O complexity, die value and known-good-die requirements should raise the value of high-parallelism, stable-contact probe technology. The relevant moat is not simply the number of needles: it is MEMS process control, wiring density, planarity/contact repeatability, thermal behavior, customer qualification and repair/replacement know-how.

### HBM evidence

**FACT:** MJC management says strong HBM demand drove DRAM probe-card sales and that expanded Aomori production capacity contributed to record probe-card sales.

**FACT:** MJC sustainability / technology materials say the company is developing **new technologies for next-generation HBM** and expanding production capability in Aomori and South Korea.

**INTERPRETATION:** This is stronger direct HBM evidence than JEM currently provides publicly. The remaining weakness is lack of exact HBM-generation/customer socket disclosure comparable with FormFactor's explicit all-three-HBM-manufacturer evidence.

## 4. Customer concentration — the largest newly surfaced risk

**FACT:** FY2025 major-customer disclosures show:

- **Samsung Electronics:** approximately **¥35.007bn**, about **49.9%** of group sales;
- **Micron Memory Taiwan:** approximately **¥14.990bn**, about **21.4%**.

Together these two customers represented roughly **71% of FY2025 group revenue**.

**INTERPRETATION:** This cuts both ways.

Positive read:

- MJC is deeply embedded with two of the three major global memory manufacturers;
- large revenue concentration suggests real production qualification rather than peripheral experimentation;
- HBM-driven DRAM demand can therefore transmit rapidly into group earnings.

Negative read:

- a single Samsung qualification/share shift can materially change total-company revenue;
- the current margin level may partly reflect unusually favorable customer/product mix;
- HBM4/HBM4E architecture or sourcing decisions at one customer can create abrupt earnings volatility;
- diversification is materially weaker than headline 'world-leading share' language suggests.

**OPEN QUESTION:** How much of Samsung and Micron revenue is specifically HBM versus conventional DRAM / NAND probe cards?

## 5. Capacity, capex and cash conversion

### Investment programme

**FACT:** MJC's FV26 plan contemplated approximately **¥48bn of capital expenditure** and **¥22bn of R&D** over the 2023–2026 period as it expanded probe-card capacity and technology.

**FACT:** additional Aomori capacity is already contributing to current sales, rather than being entirely future optionality.

### H1 2026 cash flow

**FACT:** H1 operating cash flow was approximately **¥12.17bn**.

**FACT:** capital expenditure was approximately **¥7.65bn**.

**FACT:** simple H1 free-cash-flow proxy was therefore around **¥4.66bn**.

**FACT:** the company remained in a net-cash position in the reviewed period.

**FACT:** working capital also expanded: receivables rose by roughly **¥4.4bn** and inventory by roughly **¥2.5bn**; inventory days remained elevated, with work-in-process a large portion of inventory.

**INTERPRETATION:** MJC's capital intensity is high, but unlike JEM the current expansion is already generating positive FCF without a similarly prominent equity-funded factory requirement. This is a material quality advantage.

**COUNTERPOINT:** high WIP and receivables mean cash conversion should be watched closely if memory demand slows. A high-margin, high-utilization equipment cycle can reverse sharply.

## 6. Competitive position

### Strengths

- claimed world-leading memory probe-card share;
- direct management evidence that HBM is driving DRAM probe-card demand;
- nearly pure probe-card revenue exposure;
- proprietary U-Probe MEMS + thin-film platform;
- expanded Aomori capacity already productive;
- explicit next-generation HBM technology development;
- strong current margins / ROE / ROIC;
- positive FCF through a heavy investment cycle.

### Remaining evidence gap

**OPEN QUESTION:** MJC does not publicly quantify:

- HBM probe-card market share;
- share at Samsung, SK hynix and Micron by HBM generation;
- exact HBM4/HBM4E production insertions;
- probe-card ASP/content uplift as HBM complexity increases.

**INTERPRETATION:** FormFactor still has the cleaner public evidence on all-three-HBM-manufacturer production shipments. MJC appears likely to have stronger memory purity and potentially larger memory share, but the public disclosure is less granular.

## 7. Valuation and normalized return

### 8 September 2026 reference

- share price: **¥12,310**;
- market capitalization: approximately **¥500.9bn**;
- FY2026 guided EPS: **¥593.31**;
- price / guided EPS: approximately **20.8x**.

### Refreshed normalized scenario

The prior common-basis probe-card model used an approximate **¥625 EPS consensus anchor**, 12% subsequent EPS CAGR and an 18x end-2030 terminal multiple.

The deeper pass now anchors the model to **company guidance of ¥593.31** rather than the higher consensus estimate, while keeping the same normalized 12% subsequent EPS growth and 18x terminal multiple.

This produces an approximate end-2030 value around **¥15.0k**.

At **¥12,310**, approximate price-only annualized return to end-2030 is only **~4.8%**.

The approximate base-return zones become:

- ~10%: **~¥10.0k**;
- **~12%: ~¥9.27k**;
- ~15%: **~¥8.3k**.

These are research scenarios, not price targets.

**INTERPRETATION:** MJC's valuation is not absurd for its current quality, but the market already capitalizes a substantial portion of the HBM operating inflection. The company needs either sustained earnings above guidance / normalized assumptions or a meaningful price reset to provide attractive expected return.

## 9. Catalysts

- explicit HBM4/HBM4E qualification / production-share disclosure;
- evidence of material business with the third HBM manufacturer without worsening concentration;
- FY2026 guidance exceeded on both earnings and cash conversion;
- margins remaining near the high-20s/30% range as new capacity matures;
- capex intensity falling while revenue from existing expansion continues to scale;
- valuation moving toward the **~¥9.3k 12% base-return zone** without operating deterioration.

---

## 10. Thesis breakers

1. HBM4/HBM4E share is materially weaker than MJC's conventional memory share.
2. Samsung qualification/share loss materially reduces revenue or margins.
3. Micron order timing adds further customer concentration volatility.
4. new capacity arrives into a memory-capex digestion cycle.
5. current ~30% operating margins prove peak-cycle and normalize materially below the mid-20s.
6. inventory/WIP and receivables absorb cash as demand slows.
7. current valuation continues to require high growth even after the operating cycle normalizes.
8. alternative probe technologies or competitors gain share as pin density / HBM architecture changes.

---

## 11. Open questions

1. What is MJC's HBM share at Samsung, SK hynix and Micron individually?
2. Which HBM test insertions / generations use U-Probe and how does card content scale with HBM4/HBM5?
3. What percentage of Samsung and Micron revenue is HBM-related?
4. Can MJC diversify customer exposure while maintaining margins?
5. What normalized FCF margin is sustainable after the current capex program?
6. How much of the ~30% operating margin is structural versus utilization/mix?
7. What terminal multiple is justified once the current HBM investment cycle matures?
