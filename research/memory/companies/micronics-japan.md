# Micronics Japan (MJC)

**Research stream:** AI memory — HBM / memory probe cards  
**Ticker:** TSE 6871  
**Status:** Investigating  
**Confidence:** Medium  
**Last substantive update:** 2026-09-07

## Snapshot

**FACT:** Micronics Japan develops probe cards and semiconductor test equipment. The company describes itself as having the world's leading share in memory probe cards.

**FACT:** Management says strong HBM demand drove DRAM probe-card sales and that additional Aomori production capacity contributed to record probe-card sales.

**CONCLUSION:** MJC is the most important **quality/value comparator** for JEM and FormFactor. Its incumbent memory position appears stronger than JEM's, while its valuation is materially below the premium attached to some US/European peers.

## Why it may matter

**HYPOTHESIS:** If HBM test content rises faster than DRAM bit growth, the incumbent supplier with leading memory share can compound revenue through both unit growth and increasing probe-card complexity/content.

**INTERPRETATION:** MJC is less likely than JEM to be a neglected micro/small-cap rerating story, but it may offer a better balance of competitive evidence and valuation than FormFactor.

## Strategy / financial evidence

**FACT:** MJC's FV26 plan targets **¥80bn revenue, ¥20bn operating profit, 25% operating margin and 23% ROE** in FY2026. The plan assumes roughly 20% probe-card market CAGR from 2024 to 2026 and includes substantial capex/R&D to expand capacity and technology.

**FACT:** Third-party market data on 4 September 2026 showed roughly **¥463bn market cap**, **¥449bn enterprise value**, a trailing P/E around **25x** and forward P/E around **19x**. At the 7 September close near ¥12.9k, the equity value is roughly around the **¥0.5tn** level using the reported share count.

**FACT:** Third-party data also showed trailing operating margin around **28%**, ROE around **26%** and ROIC around **24%** in the late-August/early-September snapshot.

**INTERPRETATION:** MJC already commands a much richer valuation than JEM, but its profitability and incumbent position justify part of that premium. The key question is whether the premium is too large relative to JEM or still attractive relative to FORM.

## Competitive position

**Positive evidence:** Leading claimed memory probe-card share, direct HBM-driven DRAM demand, capacity expansion and strong margins.

**Negative evidence / uncertainty:** Public high-level materials do not yet establish exact share by HBM manufacturer, HBM generation or test insertion. FORM's disclosure around all-three-customer HBM shipments remains more specific.

## Valuation / investability

**Point-in-time reference:** ~¥463bn market cap on 4 September 2026; price rose further on 7 September. Published forward P/E was around the high teens before the 7 September move.

**INTERPRETATION:** MJC is no longer obviously cheap after its large rerating, but it remains essential to the capital-allocation frontier because it combines much more direct memory sensitivity than FORM with lower valuation than Technoprobe on the reviewed market-data snapshots.

## Catalysts

- HBM4/HBM4E qualification and share evidence;
- additional capacity ramp without margin dilution;
- FY26 targets exceeded or extended into a higher medium-term plan;
- disclosure showing memory leadership translates specifically into HBM leadership.

## Risks / thesis breakers

1. HBM4/high-speed share is materially lower than conventional memory share.
2. Capacity additions arrive into a memory-capex digestion cycle.
3. Probe-card market growth falls materially below the FV26 planning assumption.
4. Margins normalize well below the mid-20s after utilization peaks.
5. The current rerating leaves insufficient normalized return despite strong operations.

## Open questions

1. What is MJC's share at each of the three HBM manufacturers?
2. Which HBM test insertions use MJC products and how does content scale with HBM4/5?
3. What is the normalized free-cash-flow conversion after the current capex program?
4. How much of current revenue is HBM versus conventional DRAM?
5. What normalized multiple is justified by ROIC, cyclicality and market leadership?
