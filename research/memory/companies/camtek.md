# Camtek

**Research stream:** AI memory — advanced-packaging inspection / metrology  
**Status:** Watch  
**Confidence:** Medium-High  
**Last substantive update:** 2026-09-07  
**Backlog:** closes issue #22 when merged

## Snapshot

**FACT:** Camtek (NASDAQ/TASE: CAMT) develops optical inspection and metrology systems used by IDMs, foundries, OSATs and wafer-level packaging customers. Its strongest AI-memory exposure is advanced packaging: HBM, chiplets, hybrid bonding and CoWoS-like 2.5D/3D assembly.

**FACT:** Camtek entered 2026 with an installed base above 3,000 systems. During 2026 it reported a sharp acceleration in AI-related orders, including more than $90m of Q1 orders from leading OSATs, $45m of Hawk orders from a tier-1 IDM, and over $105m of additional June orders split between a tier-1 OSAT and a leading HBM manufacturer.

## Why it may matter

**HYPOTHESIS:** As package area, bump count, layer count and hybrid-bond precision increase, inspection and metrology content can rise faster than package volume. Unlike a supplier tied to one bonding architecture, Camtek's process-control role may persist across CoWoS-like, HBM, chiplet and hybrid-bond transitions.

**CONCLUSION:** Camtek converts the validated advanced-packaging bottleneck into strong financial exposure, but the market has already rerated the shares aggressively. **Investment Capture Score: 3.9/5.** Status moves from `Investigating` to `Watch`, with valuation and competitive intensity the primary reasons not to promote further.

## Evidence

### Product / technology

**FACT:** Camtek's Hawk is designed for high-end advanced packaging applications including HBM, chiplets and hybrid bonding, with inspection/metrology capability for wafers containing hundreds of millions of micro-bumps.

**FACT:** Camtek's SEC-filed investor materials describe inspection/metrology across 100% of dice on the wafer to support known-good-die decisions. The same materials describe the company as a market leader in advanced-packaging inspection/metrology and show an installed base exceeding 3,000 systems.

**INTERPRETATION:** Process control is attractive because architecture transitions can create *more* inspection steps rather than eliminate the need for inspection. Hybrid bonding, for example, reduces bump height but raises the sensitivity to particles, voids and overlay errors.

### Customers / adoption

**FACT:** February 2026: Camtek disclosed $45m aggregate Hawk orders from a tier-1 IDM for AI applications, including repeated orders.

**FACT:** March 2026: a leading OSAT placed a $31m multi-system order mainly for CoWoS-like AI packaging; Q1 orders from leading OSATs exceeded $90m, mostly for similar applications.

**FACT:** June 2026: Camtek disclosed more than $105m of orders — $55m from a tier-1 OSAT and over $50m of Hawk systems from a leading HBM manufacturer, with delivery scheduled for 2027.

**FACT:** By the Q2 results announcement, year-to-date orders exceeded **$600m**, with deliveries extending through 2027.

**INTERPRETATION:** Repeat orders, multi-customer exposure and 2027 delivery schedules strengthen the case that this is more than a single-quarter AI capex spike.

### Commercial / financial

**FACT:** Q2-26 revenue reached a record **$133.2m**, +10% q/q and +8% y/y. GAAP gross margin was **50.1%**, non-GAAP gross margin **51.4%**, and non-GAAP operating margin **27.0%**.

**FACT:** Q3-26 revenue guidance was **$158m–$160m**, roughly +20% sequentially. Management expected more than 30% H2-26 growth versus H1-26 and further growth into 2027.

**FACT:** Management said advanced-packaging revenue was expected to grow approximately **70% from Q1 to Q4 2026**.

**Negative evidence:** Q2-26 GAAP and non-GAAP operating margins were lower year over year despite revenue growth, partly reflecting investment/acquisition effects and mix. Strong orders do not automatically guarantee margin expansion.

### Competitive position

Camtek competes directly or indirectly with Onto Innovation, KLA, Applied Materials and other specialist inspection/metrology suppliers.

**Positive evidence:** Hawk has direct production orders from a leading HBM manufacturer, a tier-1 IDM and leading OSATs, which is stronger evidence than generic "AI exposure." Camtek also has a substantial installed base and service infrastructure.

**Negative evidence:** Large competitors can bundle broader process-control portfolios, invest heavily in R&D and use established foundry/IDM relationships. Advanced packaging inspection is structurally attractive but not a single-supplier market.

### Customer concentration / installed base

**FACT:** Camtek's 2025 annual report said one customer represented 11% of revenue; in 2024, three customers represented 15%, 10% and 10%. Concentration is material but not extreme for semiconductor capital equipment.

**FACT:** The installed base exceeded 3,000 systems at year-end 2025, providing a service and upgrade footprint.

## Evidence against the thesis

- 2026 order intake is unusually strong and may pull forward demand from 2027/2028.
- Customers may dual-source inspection systems to reduce dependency.
- KLA/Onto/Applied can compete aggressively at the highest-value process steps.
- Visual inspection/metrology requirements can be redesigned or consolidated as yields mature.
- Advanced-packaging revenue may be cyclical even if the structural trend remains positive.
- Current valuation leaves limited room for execution misses.

---

## Risks and substitutes

- semiconductor capex cycle / order digestion;
- customer concentration;
- competitor technology catch-up;
- OSAT/foundry internal process-control changes;
- acquisition/integration risk from Visual Layer;
- high valuation multiple and volatility.

## Catalysts

- conversion of the >$600m 2026 order intake into revenue through 2027;
- additional HBM4/HBM4E production wins;
- Hawk penetration into more process steps and customers;
- sustained 50%+ gross margin as AP mix rises;
- evidence that panel-level / hybrid-bond transitions increase Camtek tool content.

## Thesis breakers

1. Advanced-packaging orders normalize sharply without a corresponding 2027 revenue ramp.
2. Gross margin falls materially below ~50% despite higher AP mix.
3. Major HBM/OSAT customers shift high-end inspection share to Onto/KLA/Applied.
4. AP tool content per production line fails to rise as package complexity increases.
5. Valuation remains >30x forward earnings while order growth slows materially.

## Open questions

1. What exact percentage of 2026 revenue and backlog is HBM versus CoWoS-like versus other advanced packaging?
2. How much recurring service revenue is attached to the >3,000-tool installed base?
3. What is Hawk's share at the leading HBM customer and leading OSATs?
4. Does Visual Layer materially improve defect-classification economics or mainly add operating cost?
5. How much incremental tool content is required as customers move from micro-bump to hybrid-bond processes?
