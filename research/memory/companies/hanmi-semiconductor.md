## Snapshot

**FACT:** Hanmi Semiconductor (KRX: 042700) is a semiconductor-packaging-equipment supplier with unusually direct exposure to HBM through thermo-compression (TC) bonders and related vision/singulation equipment. Its current earnings sensitivity to HBM is among the highest in this research set.

**FACT:** On 8 June 2026 Hanmi disclosed a KRW44.2bn HBM4 TC Bonder 4.5 Griffin order from SK hynix, equivalent to 7.66% of 2025 revenue. In Q2 2026, revenue reached KRW251.1bn and operating profit KRW130.3bn, an operating margin of 51.9%.

**FACT:** Hanmi is investing KRW130bn in an eighth plant that will make TC and hybrid bonders, while its seventh plant is dedicated to hybrid bonders. This shows management is actively preparing for the technology transition rather than assuming current TCB architecture persists indefinitely.

## Why it may matter

**HYPOTHESIS:** If HBM stack growth keeps TC-bonder demand high through HBM4/HBM4E, Hanmi's concentrated product mix can translate memory-industry capex into exceptional earnings sensitivity. But the same concentration creates a large downside if hybrid bonding shifts process-of-record share away from Hanmi or a major customer dual-sources aggressively.

**CONCLUSION:** Hanmi captures today's validated 4.6/5 HBM stacking/bonding bottleneck very strongly, but its investment case is unusually exposed to customer concentration, technology transition and a premium valuation. **Investment Capture Score: 3.6/5.** Status moves from `Investigating` to `Watch`, but with materially higher downside risk than FormFactor, Camtek or Onto.

## Evidence

### Current HBM4 economics

**FACT:** Hanmi's June 2026 SK hynix HBM4 TC-bonder order was KRW44.2bn and represented 7.66% of 2025 revenue according to the KRX filing.

**FACT:** Q2-26 revenue rose 39.5% year over year to KRW251.1bn and operating profit rose 51.0% to KRW130.3bn. The 51.9% operating margin was a record.

**INTERPRETATION:** Unlike suppliers where AI exposure is a small line item, HBM equipment demand can move Hanmi's total-company earnings materially.

### Customer and product concentration

**FACT:** Public reporting describes TC bonders as roughly 60–70% of Hanmi revenue in recent periods and estimates a leading current market position. SK hynix is a major customer, while Micron-related business provides some diversification.

**Negative evidence:** Concentration creates qualification lock-in when Hanmi wins, but it also creates cliff risk if one customer changes process-of-record, dual-sources or changes bonding architecture.

### Hybrid-bond transition

**FACT:** Hanmi is building a hybrid-bonder-only seventh plant and an eighth plant capable of producing both TC and hybrid bonders, with production capacity scheduled to expand in 2027.

**FACT:** The company is developing next-generation hybrid-bonding equipment, but public evidence of high-volume HBM production qualification remains weaker than its TCB evidence.

**INTERPRETATION:** Hanmi is investing to defend the transition, but investors should not treat planned hybrid capability as equivalent to a production moat.

## Evidence against the thesis

- customer concentration can turn qualification strength into dependency;
- competing TC-bonder suppliers can gain share as HBM4/HBM4E capacity expands;
- hybrid bonding may shift equipment value away from today's TCB leaders;
- HBM capex is cyclical and equipment orders can be lumpy between generations;
- extremely high current operating margins may attract competition and normalise;
- current valuation leaves little tolerance for an order gap or technology miss.

## Catalysts

- repeat HBM4E/HBM5 orders from SK hynix and additional memory customers;
- explicit high-volume HBM qualification for Hanmi hybrid bonders;
- customer diversification beyond the current major accounts;
- 2027 plant expansion translating into sustained revenue without margin dilution;
- valuation compression while order momentum remains intact.

## Thesis breakers

1. Hanmi loses material SK hynix bonding share to Hanwha/ASMPT or another supplier.
2. Hybrid bonding reaches HBM high-volume production without Hanmi as a meaningful qualified supplier.
3. Operating margin normalises sharply while capex remains elevated.
4. HBM-equipment revenue falls materially between generations without diversification.
5. Valuation remains near extreme levels while growth decelerates.

## Open questions

1. What percentage of 2026 revenue and EBIT is directly attributable to HBM TC bonders?
2. What is Hanmi's actual share by HBM customer rather than industry-wide estimates?
3. What specific customer qualification has the hybrid bonder achieved?
4. How much revenue diversification comes from Micron or other memory customers?
5. What sustainable operating margin should be used through a normal memory-capex cycle?
