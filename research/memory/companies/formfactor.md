# FormFactor

**Research stream:** AI memory — HBM test / probe cards  
**Status:** Watch  
**Confidence:** Medium-High  
**Last substantive update:** 2026-09-07  
**Backlog:** closes issue #21 when merged

## Snapshot

**FACT:** FormFactor (NASDAQ: FORM) supplies semiconductor probe cards and test/measurement systems. Its HBM exposure sits primarily in advanced DRAM probe cards used to test known-good die and stacked HBM at multiple insertion points before expensive heterogeneous packaging.

**FACT:** FormFactor says it ships HBM probe cards in volume to all three major HBM manufacturers. Management has also said its differentiated high-parallelism / high-speed SmartMatrix architecture is gaining share at all three customers.

## Why it may matter

**HYPOTHESIS:** HBM creates more probe-card content per shipped memory stack because higher layer counts, higher I/O speeds and the rising value of the completed package make early defect detection increasingly valuable. If FormFactor can retain technical leadership across several HBM test insertions, HBM growth can drive revenue faster than wafer volume alone and support higher margins.

**CONCLUSION:** The thesis is credible and financially visible, but the stock already discounts a large portion of the improvement. **Investment Capture Score: 4.0/5.** FormFactor moves from `Investigating` to `Watch`: strong business evidence, but valuation is now the main limiting factor.

## Evidence

### Product / technology

**FACT:** FormFactor's HBM memory-test products combine high probe count, high-speed electrical performance and high-temperature operation. Its HFTAP K32 platform supports wafer-level DRAM testing up to 3.2 GHz / 6.4 Gbps, and the company describes advanced HBM requirements exceeding 80,000 probes per device under test as architectures scale.

**FACT:** Management's Q4-25 discussion said HBM4 more than doubles stack bandwidth versus HBM3 and raises the required I/O test rate to 10 Gbps+ while customers test hundreds of die in parallel. The company positions SmartMatrix as uniquely combining high parallelism and high-speed production test.

**INTERPRETATION:** Higher HBM speed does not merely increase unit volume; it raises the technical bar for the probe-card subsystem. That is exactly the type of content-intensity growth this research is looking for.

### Customers / adoption

**FACT:** In 2025 FormFactor said it was shipping HBM probe cards in volume to all three major HBM manufacturers.

**FACT:** Q4-25 management commentary said its high-speed / high-parallelism insertion was producing share gains at all three HBM manufacturers. Management also acknowledged that overall DRAM share at two customers remained below the incumbent leader, creating runway rather than an already-saturated position.

**FACT:** Q1-26 commentary said first-half 2026 HBM probe-card revenue was expected to be more than 50% above first-half 2025 and that a second HBM customer was increasing adoption of SmartMatrix for at-speed stacked-die test.

**Risk:** Revenue remains skewed toward the HBM market-share leader, so customer production schedules can create significant quarterly volatility.

### Commercial / financial

**FACT:** Q2-26 revenue was **$258.2m**, +31.9% y/y and +14.2% q/q. GAAP gross margin reached **50.7%** and non-GAAP gross margin **53.3%**. Non-GAAP diluted EPS was $0.82. Free cash flow was $52.6m.

**FACT:** Q3-26 guidance was approximately **$270m revenue**, **54% non-GAAP gross margin**, and **$0.86 non-GAAP EPS** at the midpoint.

**INTERPRETATION:** The operating leverage is real. The evidence does not prove HBM alone caused the margin step-up — foundry/logic, systems mix and execution also contributed — but the company is converting the current demand environment into materially better economics.

### Competitive position

Main competitors include Technoprobe, Micronics Japan (MJC), Japan Electronic Materials and other specialist probe-card vendors. The key differentiation question is not broad probe-card market share but **high-speed, high-parallelism HBM insertions**.

**Positive evidence:** FormFactor has multi-customer HBM production exposure and customer-specific qualification, reducing single-vendor technology risk.

**Negative evidence:** Probe cards are replaced and requalified regularly, and customers deliberately maintain second sources. FormFactor itself says it is number two overall at two major DRAM customers, proving competitors remain viable.

### Manufacturing capacity / Keystone

**FACT:** In July 2026 FormFactor expanded its partnership with Keystone Microtech in Taiwan. Keystone will add assembly, test, repair and distribution support for designated products while FormFactor retains proprietary probe-card technology and IP.

**INTERPRETATION:** This is an intelligent way to increase regional scale and support without giving away core technology. It reduces one execution risk in the HBM ramp.

## Evidence against the thesis

- HBM probe-card demand is design-driven and can be volatile quarter to quarter.
- Customers use multiple suppliers and can shift share after qualification.
- Increasing test parallelism may partly offset rising test content per bit.
- HBM packaging/test architectures may evolve in ways that change insertion points.
- A large part of the 2026 margin expansion comes from broad company execution and mix, not necessarily a permanent HBM-specific moat.
- The stock has already rerated dramatically, increasing downside if growth normalises.

---

## Risks and substitutes

- customer concentration / HBM market-share concentration;
- Technoprobe/MJC/JEM technical catch-up;
- memory capex cyclicality;
- faster-than-expected yield learning reducing probe-card spending intensity;
- advanced packaging shifts that alter wafer/stack test methodology;
- valuation multiple compression.

## Catalysts

- continued HBM4 volume ramp and HBM4E/HBM5 qualification;
- rising contribution from the second and third HBM manufacturers;
- proof that high-speed stacked-die test becomes a standard insertion at all three customers;
- sustained >50% gross margin with rising HBM mix;
- additional advanced-logic / co-packaged-optics test wins that diversify HBM exposure.

## Thesis breakers

1. HBM probe-card revenue stops outgrowing HBM bit growth / test intensity.
2. Material share losses at the HBM market leader or failure to gain share at customers two and three.
3. Gross margin falls back toward historical high-30s/low-40s despite high HBM demand.
4. Competitors demonstrate equivalent high-parallelism, high-speed production performance and win major sockets.
5. Valuation remains >30x forward earnings while forward growth expectations fall sharply.

## Open questions

1. What percentage of total Probe Cards revenue and gross profit is HBM today?
2. What is FormFactor's share by HBM test insertion at each memory manufacturer?
3. How many probe-card fleets are required per HBM4/HBM5 design and how does spending scale with layer count?
4. What share of Q2-26 margin expansion is structural versus mix/restructuring/execution recovery?
5. What is the replacement/repair cycle and service gross margin for HBM probe cards?
