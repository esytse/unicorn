# Probe-card Capital Allocation — JEM, Micronics, FormFactor and Technoprobe

**Research stream:** AI memory — HBM test / probe cards  
**Status:** Capital-allocation comparison  
**Confidence:** Medium-Low on JEM competitive evidence; Medium on relative valuation  
**Last substantive update:** 2026-09-07  
**Backlog:** issue #31

## Purpose

Test whether the probe-card peer screen from PR #30 changes the AI-memory capital-allocation frontier.

The question is deliberately narrower than the technical HBM-test thesis, which is already validated:

> **Does a listed probe-card supplier offer enough normalized earnings and valuation asymmetry to improve on the existing Onto / SUSS capital watch?**

This is a research framework, not an investment recommendation. Scenario outputs are deliberately approximate and are intended to expose what must go right.

---

## 1. Current conclusion

**CONCLUSION:** **JEM is the strongest new probe-card valuation lead, but it does not yet pass Gate C.** It has a much lower earnings multiple and smaller equity value than Micronics, FormFactor or Technoprobe, but the evidence that its current earnings acceleration is specifically HBM-driven and durable is materially weaker than FormFactor's all-three-HBM-customer evidence or Micronics' claimed memory leadership.

**CONCLUSION:** The new peer work **does change the research frontier**. The provisional capital-watch order becomes:

1. **Onto Innovation — still the best validated quality / valuation balance.**
2. **JEM — provisional challenger, pending HBM-specific share/customer verification.**
3. **SUSS — stronger direct HBM qualification than JEM, but materially wider execution downside.**
4. **Micronics Japan — strong memory quality, but current valuation discounts much more success than JEM.**
5. **Technoprobe — extraordinary operating benchmark, but current valuation is already premium.**
6. **Camtek / FormFactor — excellent businesses with demanding normalized return hurdles.**

JEM therefore moves **ahead of Weebit as the immediate AI-memory research priority**, but remains `Investigating` rather than `Watch` until the HBM evidence gap is closed.

## 4. Normalization discipline

The current probe-card results are occurring during an unusually strong HBM / AI memory cycle. The model therefore does not capitalize current utilization or margins indefinitely.

Common principles:

- reference prices are dated and must be refreshed before a capital decision;
- bear cases assume memory demand / utilization normalizes and terminal multiples compress;
- base cases assume HBM test content remains structurally important but growth slows materially after the current step-up;
- bull cases require durable HBM share/content gains, not simply a stronger DRAM cycle;
- current exceptional margins at Technoprobe and MJC are not assumed permanent;
- JEM receives a lower terminal multiple than higher-evidence peers because customer/share visibility is weaker;
- returns are measured approximately from September 2026 to end-2030 (~4.25 years), ignoring dividends.

These are **HYPOTHESES**, not forecasts.

## 8. Falsification of the JEM undervaluation thesis

### Evidence supporting mispricing

- management explicitly attributes the earnings acceleration to rapid memory-probe-card demand;
- prior capacity investments are now producing high utilization and operating leverage;
- the reference valuation is only ~13–14x guided earnings, far below the premium multiples of FORM / TPRO and below MJC;
- equity value is only ~¥91bn on the 4 September common reference, preserving small-cap asymmetry.

### Evidence against / unresolved

1. **HBM specificity is not proven.** Public evidence reviewed does not identify which of SK hynix, Samsung or Micron use JEM at HBM production insertions.
2. **Share durability is unknown.** Memory-probe growth could be conventional DRAM or cyclical utilization rather than HBM content/share gain.
3. **Margin normalization is under-tested.** Current high utilization can exaggerate incremental profitability.
4. **Capital intensity matters.** The thesis weakens if continued capacity additions absorb most incremental cash flow.
5. **Dilution needs reconciliation.** Share-count / capital actions must be checked before treating per-share upside as clean.
6. **The stock has already rerated sharply.** The 7 September price is ~7% above the 4 September reference and the 52-week range shows extreme volatility.

**CONCLUSION:** The apparent cheapness survives a first normalization pass, but **not yet the evidence-quality test required for Gate C**.

## 9. Decision and backlog implication

### JEM

**Status remains `Investigating`.** It becomes the **highest-priority AI-memory company investigation** because closing the HBM-customer/share and normalized-margin gaps could materially change the capital-allocation frontier.

Promote toward `Watch` / Gate C only if:

- HBM production customers / sockets are verified from primary or strong customer evidence;
- normalized margin and FCF conversion remain attractive after capex;
- fully diluted share count does not erase the valuation advantage;
- the valuation still offers roughly 12%+ base-case return after refreshing the price.

### Micronics

**Remain `Investigating`.** It is the best probe-card quality comparator and may deserve a premium to JEM, but the current multiple and heavy growth capex reduce immediate asymmetry.

### Technoprobe

**Remain `Watch` as the operating benchmark.** The current share price / forward valuation does not improve the capital frontier despite exceptional operating performance.

### FormFactor

**Remain `Watch`.** PR #30 strengthens the relative-valuation objection; its technical thesis remains intact.

### Research sequence

1. **Close #31 only after the JEM evidence-gap work is captured in the canonical company file.**
2. Then **#5 Weebit Nano** — separate architectural-discontinuity optionality.
3. Then **#3 robotics actuators**.

Do not reopen broad AI-memory supplier discovery unless another peer shows a materially better combination of evidence quality and normalized return than JEM / Onto / SUSS.
