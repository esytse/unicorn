# D02 — Smartphone single- to multi-camera transition

**T0:** 1 September 2016
**Prediction phase:** FROZEN before outcome reveal
**Architecture:** smartphones moving from one rear camera toward coordinated multi-camera systems.

## T0 architecture evidence

**FACT:** By T0, multi-camera handsets were shipping rather than merely being discussed. The LG G5 and Huawei P9 had two rear cameras, and LG had carried the design into the mid-range X cam. Contemporaneous reporting described several functions enabled by paired sensors and lenses: wider effective light collection, wide/telephoto fields of view, faster autofocus, depth estimation, refocus and 3-D/augmented-reality features.

**INTERPRETATION:** The transition multiplies some physical content, but the scarce complement is not simply “two of every camera part.” Coordinated cameras require matched lenses/sensors, compact actuation, active alignment, calibration, acceptable yield and an image-processing/software pipeline that can fuse unlike images. The most promising listed suppliers should therefore show architecture-specific qualification or production capability, not only generic smartphone-camera exposure.

**HYPOTHESIS:** Module makers that can execute active alignment and calibration at scale may initially capture more incremental content than generic component suppliers. That capture remains vulnerable to severe ASP erosion, low assembly margins and value migration to upstream lenses/sensors or downstream OEM/ISP software.

## Listed candidate search using only <=T0 evidence

### Q Technology (HKEX: 1478) — PROMOTE / Medium

**FACT:** Q Technology's 19 August 2016 interim results said it had relationships with vivo, OPPO, Huawei and LeTV for dual-camera modules and/or fingerprint modules. It described itself as one of the few Chinese module makers with large dual-camera sales volume and said it was expanding back-end capacity and adding active-alignment equipment and technology. In H1 2016, total camera-module sales volume rose 100.2% y/y to 74.08m units and group revenue rose 98.8% to RMB1.73bn. Modules of 8MP and above rose 309.6% to 46.43m units and represented 75.5% of group revenue.

**FACT:** A December 2015 plant-visit report had already observed a fleet of ASM Pacific active-alignment machines dedicated to dual-camera modules. At that date the shares traded at 7.1x the report's 2016 earnings estimate and more than a 50% discount to Sunny Optical. This is stale rather than exact T0 valuation evidence, but it establishes that the capability was not universally priced as a premium franchise before the operating inflection.

**INTERPRETATION:** Q Technology best fits the desired early conversion shape: architecture-specific process equipment and customer access, a small listed company base, and a sharp group-volume/revenue state change. Dual-camera revenue was not isolated, so the prediction rests on a credible capture mechanism plus operating acceleration—not on a proven 5–15% revenue disclosure.

**Strongest disconfirming evidence:** H1 gross margin fell to 9.3% from 12.8% because ASP decline exceeded component-cost decline, currency moved adversely and the group invested ahead of demand. Dual-camera, high-pixel modules and fingerprint modules were bundled in the growth narrative, so dual-camera causality and economics remain unproven. Customer concentration and Chinese smartphone saturation add risk.

**Missing bridge:** dual-camera qualification and active-alignment capacity -> disclosed dual-camera volume/mix -> sustainable ASP/yield -> material group profit and diversified customer capture.

**Expected next observable state change:** dual-camera modules become a separately important shipment or revenue driver, while module ASP/gross margin stabilize or improve despite industry price pressure.

**Frozen state:** **PROMOTE / Medium**.

### Sunny Optical (HKEX: 2382) — EVIDENCE-BUILD / Medium

**FACT:** Sunny's 15 August 2016 interim results reported that it had begun mass production of 12MP dual-camera modules and had become a major supplier. H1 group revenue rose 27.1% to RMB5.91bn, profit rose 50.4% to RMB465m and camera-module ASP increased. Yet optoelectronic-product gross margin was only 8.7%, down from 9.1%, and the company did not disclose dual-camera revenue or profit.

**FACT:** A 14 June 2016 BofA report estimated only 3%/8% earnings upside from dual cameras in 2016/17, citing 8–10% module gross margins, low initial yields and likely ASP erosion as yields improved. It also argued that optimism was already reflected after a 56% year-to-date share-price rise, at 22x/18x estimated 2016/17 earnings.

**INTERPRETATION:** Qualification and production capture are stronger than for most peers, but the new engine is not shown to be company-transforming and contemporaneous valuation evidence indicates substantial recognition. Sunny is an architecture validator, not yet a clean asymmetry.

**Strongest disconfirming evidence:** dual-camera assembly appears revenue-accretive but low-margin; upstream sensor/lens suppliers have greater bargaining power, module ASP erosion is structural and the stock had already rerated.

**Missing bridge:** dual-camera shipments -> incremental module/lens gross profit -> meaningful group earnings contribution at an unrecognised valuation.

**Expected next observable state change:** dual-camera mix or revenue becomes quantifiable and raises consolidated optical/module ASP or margin rather than only units.

**Frozen state:** **EVIDENCE-BUILD / Medium**.

### InvenSense (NYSE: INVN) — EVIDENCE-BUILD / Medium

**FACT:** InvenSense's FY2016 filing showed optical-image-stabilisation revenue growing from $16.9m / 7% of group revenue in FY2014 to $44.4m / 12% in FY2015 and $66.3m / 16% in FY2016. It attributed the latest increase to adoption of its technology in smartphone camera modules. Total FY2016 revenue was $418.4m.

**INTERPRETATION:** This is the clearest early-materiality curve among adjacent component suppliers. However, OIS motion sensing is not uniquely caused by multi-camera architecture: a high-end single camera can use it, and a multi-camera system need not multiply OIS content proportionally.

**Strongest disconfirming evidence:** Apple and Samsung were 40% and 16% of FY2016 revenue; smartphone/tablet revenue declined, Korea revenue fell after lost share at a major customer, and overall ASP declined. No T0 filing tied InvenSense to a named dual-camera design.

**Missing bridge:** OIS growth -> multi-camera-specific sockets/content multiplication -> customer diversification and durable group-margin expansion.

**Expected next observable state change:** named multi-camera/OIS qualifications and OIS revenue moving clearly beyond the mid-teens without worsening concentration or ASP.

**Frozen state:** **EVIDENCE-BUILD / Medium**.

### LG Innotek (KRX: 011070) — EVIDENCE-BUILD / Low

**FACT:** In November 2015 LG Innotek said its camera-module business had held the largest global share for five years and was leading with OIS and dual-camera modules. Its Q3 2015 optics-solutions sales were KRW774.7bn, up 22% y/y, driven by high-performance 12MP/OIS modules.

**INTERPRETATION:** Manufacturing position and architecture relevance are credible, but the recovered T0 evidence does not separate dual-camera production, customers, revenue, margins or valuation. The state change is too weak for promotion.

**Strongest disconfirming evidence:** segment growth can be explained by high-resolution/OIS modules without a multi-camera inflection; group diversification reduces transformation purity.

**Missing bridge:** named dual-camera qualification -> production volume -> optics-segment margin/profit contribution -> group materiality.

**Expected next observable state change:** disclosed dual-camera design wins or shipments become a material driver of optics-solutions growth and profitability.

**Frozen state:** **EVIDENCE-BUILD / Low**.

### Sony (TSE: 6758) — REJECT for D02 pre-inflection promotion / Medium

**FACT:** Sony's June 2016 strategy explicitly expected dual-lens cameras and higher pixel density to increase mobile image-sensor demand. It had also planned to lift image-sensor capacity from about 60,000 to 80,000 300mm-equivalent wafers per month by June 2016.

**INTERPRETATION:** Sony is an upstream architecture beneficiary with process/capacity scarcity, but the available evidence is a broad demand expectation rather than a dual-camera qualification or emerging engine capable of transforming the diversified group.

**Strongest disconfirming evidence:** the same strategy update said slower smartphone growth had caused lower-than-expected image-sensor sales, a significant FY2015 forecast reduction and slower profit growth expected through FY2017. Large prior capacity investment raises utilisation risk.

**Missing bridge:** dual-camera sensor content -> attributable sensor volume/capacity absorption -> devices-segment profit inflection -> group materiality.

**Expected next observable state change:** dual-camera-linked sensor volume absorbs capacity and visibly revives devices-segment profitability.

**Frozen state:** **REJECT / Medium**.

### Alps Electric (TSE: 6770) — REJECT for D02 pre-inflection promotion / Medium

**FACT:** Alps began mass production in February 2016 of a low-profile bi-directional autofocus actuator for 16MP smartphone modules, targeting one million units per month from May. It highlighted large-lens compatibility, low power and tilt control below 0.1 degrees.

**INTERPRETATION:** Precision actuation is relevant to increasingly complex camera stacks, but the evidence establishes a high-resolution autofocus product—not a dual-camera qualification, content multiplication or company-material new engine.

**Strongest disconfirming evidence:** no named dual-camera customer, design win, revenue mix or margin evidence was recovered; Alps was a broad components group.

**Missing bridge:** dual-camera qualification -> more actuator content per handset -> revenue/profit materiality.

**Expected next observable state change:** a named dual-camera socket or capacity ramp attributable to multi-camera adoption.

**Frozen state:** **REJECT / Medium**.

### Hutchinson Technology (NASDAQ: HTCH) — REJECT / High

**FACT:** Hutchinson's May 2016 filing said its shape-memory-alloy OIS actuator was still undergoing testing in prototype smartphone camera modules and that all related costs were R&D. For the 26 weeks ended March 2016, sales fell to $118.1m and the company posted a $10.0m operating loss. About 95% of revenue still came from three hard-disk-drive customers.

**FACT:** The company was already subject to a shareholder-approved cash merger agreement at T0, with $3.62 plus up to $0.38 per share of contingent consideration. That capped independent public-equity asymmetry even if the prototype succeeded.

**INTERPRETATION:** This is a useful failed/obscure-name control: potentially relevant actuation technology, but no production economics and no clean investable route at T0.

**Strongest disconfirming evidence:** prototype-only status, losses, legacy-customer concentration, manufacturing/capital risk and the pending cash acquisition.

**Missing bridge:** production qualification and smartphone revenue existed only as an unproven possibility; investable upside was contractually bounded.

**Expected next observable state change:** production qualification and attributable OIS revenue would be required, but the pending acquisition prevents D02 promotion.

**Frozen state:** **REJECT / High**.

## Negative / boundary search

- **OmniVision:** excluded from the investable T0 set. Its cash acquisition completed on 28 January 2016 and Nasdaq trading was halted; this is a non-investable boundary result rather than a scored operating candidate.
- **Largan Precision:** a broad discovery query exposed post-T0 result snippets before a clean Largan prediction could be frozen. Largan is therefore excluded from scored D02 candidates rather than risk hindsight contamination.
- Other module suppliers surfaced, but without recoverable <=T0 evidence that linked dual-camera qualification to group materiality and valuation. Generic camera-module exposure is insufficient.
- The scan does not assume that doubling cameras doubles supplier economics. Yield loss, active-alignment cost, module commoditisation, upstream bargaining power and OEM/ISP software integration are explicit falsifiers.

## Research-boundary incident

During candidate discovery, general web-search result pages emitted post-T0 snippets concerning Largan, InvenSense and Hutchinson. No post-T0 fact is cited or used in the states above; the InvenSense and Hutchinson decisions rest on independently recovered <=T0 filings, while Largan was removed from the scored set. This leakage caveat must remain attached to D02 when its outcome is later scored.

## D02 frozen prediction

The strongest v1 prediction is **Q Technology PROMOTE**. Before 1 September 2016, it combined dual-camera customer access, architecture-specific active-alignment investment, claimed scaled dual-camera production and a near-doubling of group volume/revenue from a small listed-company base. The weak link is economic capture: total gross margin deteriorated, and dual-camera contribution was not isolated.

Sunny Optical and InvenSense provide stronger evidence that the broader imaging upgrade was real, but both fail the clean promotion test for different reasons: Sunny's dual-camera economics appeared low-margin and already recognised, while InvenSense's OIS growth was not demonstrably multi-camera-specific. LG Innotek lacks recovered materiality evidence; Sony and Alps lack a company-transforming state change; Hutchinson lacks both production economics and independent equity asymmetry.
